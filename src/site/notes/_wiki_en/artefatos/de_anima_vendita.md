# De Anima Vendita

De Anima Vendita, also known as On the Sold Soul or Ambrosin’s Treatise, is a theological treatise written in church Latin by the [[_wiki_en/racas/alvarar\|Álvarar]] cleric Ambrosin de [[_wiki_en/locais/myrgarde\|Myrgarde]], of the Scholarium attached to the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/igreja_de_sao_lucian\|Church of Saint Lucian]]. Composed in the decades before the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/guerras_reinkardicas\|Reinkardic Wars]], it argues that the [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]] sold their souls and have nothing left to lose. Its conclusion feeds the doctrine preached among the free peoples.

### The Thesis

Ambrosin interprets the [[_wiki_en/magia/pactum_diaboli\|Pactum Diaboli]] as the completed surrender of the soul, with the body held in usufruct until collection. The [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/culto_sombrio\|Shadow Cult]] understands the pact as a clause negotiated between parties. For the treatise, all that remains of the people who pledged their souls is the body, discipline, and appetite; threats lose their effectiveness because they presuppose something still left to lose. Ambrosin recommends treating each Shadar as already dead and judging him by the soul he considers lost.

### The Generalization

The treatise extends the pledge of those bound by pacts to the entire race, although it arises from the individual contract. It also predates the spread of [[_wiki_en/magia/teru\|Teru]], which allows arcane access through study without pledging the soul. A [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/justiciares\|Justiciar]] trained through this path contradicts the work’s central thesis.

The text continues to be copied without revision. In popular preaching, the conclusion that the Shadars have nothing left to lose circulates among people who have never read Ambrosin, separated from the demonstration and the qualification that the pledge depends on a contract.

### Dissemination and Survival

During the conquest, [[_wiki_en/personagens/arothistar\|Arothistar]] extinguished the sacred names of [[_wiki_en/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]], and most copies burned with the libraries of Myrgarde. The surviving volumes are in [[_wiki_en/locais/khar_tabreth\|Khar’Tabreth]], preserved by administrative decision: the Cult considers it useful to know the written thought of its enemies. Copies are rare outside the imperial archives. Partial copies circulate among secluded Álvarar communities, and passages survive in sermons memorized by clerics who have never seen the volume.

In the archives, the treatise is cataloged alongside the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/maleficarios\|Maleficaries]], with which it shares an institutional origin and method. Ambrosin writes about a people; the Maleficaries, about entities. The association between the works stems from their cataloging.
