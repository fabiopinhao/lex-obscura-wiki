# Sylvaen

Sylvaen is the everyday language of the [[_wiki_en/racas/sylvano\|Sylvans]] and also the language of druidic invocations. Softer than [[_wiki_en/linguas/aelarian\|Aelarian]], it is distinguished by open syllables and a descending cadence, compared to falling leaves. It descends from [[_wiki_en/linguas/luminar\|Luminar]] fused with [[_wiki_en/linguas/titanico\|Titan-tongue]], a heritage that defines both its sound and its grammar.

### Sound and Registers

Its consonants are soft fricatives, with no stops, and it incorporates natural onomatopoeias such as *thris*, the wind among branches, or *murael*, the rapids. The language has two markedly distinct registers. The everyday register is fluid and musical, evoking a lullaby whispered in the forest. The magical register, known academically as Titan-tongue, slows the cadence, lengthens the pauses between roots, and reintroduces the final-syllable stress characteristic of primordial Titan-tongue; it sounds deeper and slower, as though the earth were breathing between words. Druids alternate between the two registers, while ordinary Sylvans use only the everyday register.

### Origin and Grammar

In deriving from Luminar, Sylvaen lost that language’s inherent magical capacity: speaking it produces no residual effects. In exchange, it gained the organic quality of Titan-tongue. Its grammar is based on states, a legacy of Titan-tongue, with chained subordination inherited from Luminar. Sentences tend to branch, one clause generating two subordinate clauses that generate another four, in a structure that imitates the growth of trees. This makes the language elegant for description and inefficient for command, which is why military orders in [[_wiki_en/locais/thal_serin\|Thal’Serin]] are given in [[_wiki_en/linguas/reinkardico\|Reinkardic]] or in short sentences in the everyday register, never in the magical register.

### Writing and Use

The Sylvan written tradition is organic and perishable as a matter of conviction: marks on tree bark, natural pigments on treated leaves, carvings in exposed roots. Permanent texts are seen as presumption against the natural cycle, with exceptions reserved for ritual maps and records of sacred boundaries. Among the Sylvans of Thal’Serin, it is spoken in everyday life and in the druidic invocations of [[_wiki_en/personagens/gaelwyn\|Gaelwyn]] and [[_wiki_en/personagens/peredur\|Peredur]], and serves as a marker of cultural resistance in the face of Reinkardic.

### Druidic Liturgy

The magical register preserves liturgical pieces older than the current spoken language itself. A druidic prayer predating the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/primeira_era\|First Era]], from a time when elves and trees still shared the same language, remains in ritual use; each syllable resonates with the frequency of plant growth and speaks to the soul of the forest. The Chant of the Deep Roots belongs to the same register, sung by druids in an appeal to the life beneath the earth, so that it may support their feet and recognize its children as part of it, not as a weight upon it. Among the druidic invocations recorded in Thal’Serin is Gaelwyn’s, which summons defensive roots.

### Epithets and Vocabulary

From Sylvaen come the epithets of [[_wiki_en/panteao/titania\|Titânia]]: *Enna-sylwen*, “Green Mother”; *Anwen-e-braeli*, “First of the Trees”; and *Anwen-e-laen*, “First of the Hunt”, the hunting epithet of the shooting blessing. The prefix *Anwen-e-* means “First of”. The word *laen* is a homonym: it means “hunt” in this epithet and “light” in Peredur’s healing chant, in *laen-e-elaeni*, “starlight”, and in *Gwil-laen*, “Lunar glow”; it is also found within *elaeni*, the stars.

At the council beside the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/carvalho_mae\|Mother Oak]], Peredur gave the warning in Sylvaen *Dhar-Morveth davra. Braeloth vurtha na davren.*, which means “Dhar-Morveth in motion. The grove darkens with its march.” In it, *davra* means “advances”; *vurtha*, “darkens”, from the root *vurth*, shadow; and *davren*, “the march”.
