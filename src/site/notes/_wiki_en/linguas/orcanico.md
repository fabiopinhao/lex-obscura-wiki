# Orcanic

Orcanic is the language of the [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orcs]], used both in everyday speech and in berserker and shamanic rituals. Monosyllabic by preference and polysyllabic only by necessity, it is made up of hard, aspirated consonants (*kh*, *gh*, *hrr*) and open, shouted vowels. It has no quiet tone: it was shaped for battlefields, not for intimate conversations.

### Register and Ritual

There is no real separation between the everyday and sacred forms, only between speed and intent: the same language serves to bark orders and to channel [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/demonios\|demons]] under [[_wiki_en/magia/vinculum_daemonis\|Vinculum Daemonis]], and the difference lies in the cadence. In the berserker register, grammar disintegrates as fury escalates, and entire sentences break down into explosive monosyllables, as though the language were accompanying the speaker back to instinct. Shamans warn that prolonged exposure to this register erodes mental stability, a cost that comes not from the language but from the fury it channels. Demons that answer the call can distort the language during channeling, forcing the caster to improvise from one invocation to the next.

### Writing and Grammar

Orc writing is rudimentary: marks carved into wood, bone, or stone for practical records such as counting, territorial marking, and warnings. There is no literary tradition in Orcanic, and epics such as the [[_wiki_en/personagens/grasnak\|Lays of Grasnak]] are transmitted only by voice; to an orc, writing down a song would be as absurd as bottling the wind. Grammar is imperative by default, with the subject frequently omitted, articles and prepositions suppressed for economy, and verb tense reduced to two states, the “now” and the “not-now”.

### Relations

Imperial orcs speak [[_wiki_en/linguas/reinkardico\|Reinkardic]] with an accent and betray Orcanic interference. Some demons even prefer Orcanic to [[_wiki_en/linguas/draconico\|Draconic]] as a channeling language. The invocation of berserker fury attributed to [[_wiki_en/personagens/grothar\|Grothar]], *Vrokh gûm-ûrz! Kazh-rakh drûzkar!* (“Fire in the entrails! The weak will be devoured!”), is an example of its combat register.

### Lexicon

This invocation establishes the core of the Orcanic lexicon attested in use. From it are derived *vrokh*, fire; *gûm*, belly or entrails; the violent intensifying suffix *-ûrz*; *kazh*, the weak one or the weak; the collective suffix *-rakh*, which forms names for hordes and peoples; and *drûz*, devour in the imperative, whose form *drûzkar* means devour everything.

Alongside these, *khar* names the fury that serves as the root cry of the berserker register. Two suffixes complete the productive set: *-makh*, a structural intensifier, and *-hal*, cleft or passage. Two other morphemes belong to the lexicon without any recorded occurrence in the sources: *grommh*, blood; and *kragh*, break or shatter, in the imperative.

The combination of *gûm* with *-rakh* gives rise to **Gûmrakh**, “the belly of the horde”, the Orcanic name for the [[_wiki_en/locais/terras_geladas_do_norte\|Northern Frozen Lands]]. The same formation names the fortresses that guard the outskirts of the plateau: **[[_wiki_en/locais/kharurz\|Kharûrz]]**, from *khar* with *-ûrz*, fury taken to the extreme; **[[_wiki_en/locais/vrokhmakh\|Vrokhmakh]]**, from *vrokh* with *-makh*, the great structure of fire; and **[[_wiki_en/locais/druzhal\|Drûzhal]]**, from *drûz* with *-hal*, the passage that devours.

The distinction between the two intensifiers organizes the entire series: *-ûrz* intensifies the act and its violence, whereas *-makh* intensifies the thing and its structure.
