# Grothar

Grothar is a veteran [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orc]], captain of [[_wiki_en/personagens/milorsevogh_velshard\|Milorsevogh Velshard]]’s guard, and commands [[_wiki_en/personagens/mur\|Mur]] and [[_wiki_en/personagens/grub\|Grub]] with a heavy hand. He is unlike ordinary orcs. He maintains discipline where others give in to impulse, and is the most perceptive and skeptical of the group: he speaks [[_wiki_en/linguas/reinkardico\|Reinkardic]], knows the [[_wiki_en/personagens/grasnak\|Lays of Grasnak]], and reads some ancient runes. A learned orc, something rare enough that few know what to do with him.

His learning is measured by his own race’s standards: preserving the Lays and deciphering a few runes is remarkable among orcs, and some of that accumulated knowledge also comes from his advanced age.

### Appearance

He has olive-green skin weathered by the sun and a prominent right tusk; his tusks are yellowed and worn with age. One eye is blind and milky, lost while he was still young in the carnage of [[_wiki_en/locais/ghargash\|Ghargash]], when he served the old barbarian clan from the frozen lands northwest of the plateau. Years after that slaughter, most of that clan joined the shadows and came to inhabit the outlying fortresses of [[_wiki_en/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]]. He stands about one meter and eighty centimeters tall, below his race’s average and shorter than his own subordinates, who surpass him in height. The imposing presence he projects comes from his density: a stocky, muscular trunk, no belly, with his mass concentrated in the torso. At forty years old he is an elder by orc standards, as their longevity rarely exceeds that age, and the weight of the years shows in wear before stature: worn tusks, faded skin, a still-erect posture carrying accumulated experience. He is strong for his age, with a vigor that orcs so old rarely retain. He wears high-quality plate armor, providing more coverage and more fully enclosed than that of ordinary orcs, uncommon among them and befitting his rank as captain, though still recognizable as orc equipment rather than a human knight’s armor, marked by the battles he has been through. In his hand he carries the “[[_wiki_en/artefatos/machadacha\|machadacha]]”, a double-bladed axe with poorly maintained runic carvings, a nickname he himself gave the weapon. He rides a hardy gray mule, and woe to anyone who jokes about it: the mule is a sensitive subject.

### Method and Voice

He speaks directly and says little. Beneath his brutality runs a vein of irony that surfaces when no one expects it, and a disdain calibrated by someone who has seen real war and measures others against it. He looks at a village’s softened orcs and calls them soft, fat as feast pigs. He voices his puzzlement when two authorities seem to be giving orders at the same time, until someone asks him to be quiet. He accepts a dangerous mission by disguising his acceptance as a threat, warning that if it reeks of a trap, the fellow will find out how many teeth a mule has. And when he saves the life of someone who irritated him, he grumbles that he already regrets doing it. He is more intelligent than he appears and less outgoing than his subordinates, and uses both to his advantage.

He enjoys mead, the drink that, to him, a decent warrior’s work deserves.

### Magic

The bond with [[_wiki_en/panteao/thurgash\|Thûrgash]] is his, and his alone. It is a [[_wiki_en/magia/vinculum_daemonis\|Vinculum Daemonis]]: Thûrgash is a demon, and so the connection is called a bond, not a pact, a word reserved for devils. Mur and Grub revere Thûrgash as a god, but are not among the bonded: the connection that matters runs through Grothar.

### Relationships

Grothar’s loyalty to Milor was earned, not bought, and hardens over the course of the journey until it becomes open endorsement: he knows what he is saying and acts on what he knows, he is not like the others Grothar has served. He commands Mur and Grub with rough authority, knuckle blows to the head, and threats of punches, and feels the reins slipping when the two decide to think for themselves. His brother [[_wiki_en/personagens/ghur\|Ghur]], who recited the Lays of Grasnak around campfires, died in [[_wiki_en/locais/lendalas\|Lendalas]], hanged by elven druidic roots; this is the source of a restrained resentment toward elves, far removed from Mur’s obsessive hatred. He still struggles to say his brother’s name; in [[_wiki_en/locais/thal_serin\|Thal’Serin]], when a root reached his boot, his good eye blinked rapidly and the milky one did not move. He was recruited as an informant by [[_wiki_en/personagens/syndrith_morghast\|Syndrith]], who elegantly achieved what orders could not: she treated him as an equal; she was also the one who attributed the scars of Khaz-Ankor to him, a legendary feat from an uncertain era, flattery that his biography does not support.
