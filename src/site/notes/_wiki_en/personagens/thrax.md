# Thrax

Thrax is a brown [[_wiki_en/racas/draconideo\|Draconid]] [[_wiki_en/racas/sauriano\|Saurian]] of [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/cla_myr_vethan\|Clan Myr’vethan]], called the Warrior of the Deep Flames, and one of the warriors in [[_wiki_en/personagens/yzmareth\|Yzmareth]]’s circle. He wields a spiked mace that [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]] would need both hands to hold and has been bound by a pact to [[_wiki_en/panteao/kaelgrym\|Kaelgrym]] since he was an egg, in accordance with the universal tribal ritual of the Drakhonins. He is currently gravely wounded and chained in [[_wiki_en/locais/mor_severith\|Mor’Severith]].

### Captivity

He is imprisoned in Mor’Severith, held captive by [[_wiki_en/personagens/arothistar\|Arothistar]]. He was burned up to the waist by the monarch’s fire; his lower scales turned black from ankle to knee, crazed like old crockery, with the flesh exposed. He is restrained by [[_wiki_en/artefatos/grilhoes_de_contencao_arcana\|shackles of arcane containment]] and a metal muzzle that prevents him from breathing fire. With the last of his strength, he tore off a piece of [[_wiki_en/personagens/fing\|Fing]]’s tail, looking at him with indifference, and keeps it: the first fragment he took for himself in the Tower.

### Philosophy

Even in chains, Thrax is defiant. He upholds a draconic doctrine of brutal pragmatism, in which no word is worth more than survival: words bend to keep the speaker alive, and anyone who dies for a promise is, in his eyes, a fool. It is this conviction that he sets against [[_wiki_en/personagens/odran\|Odran]] in the debate over the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/verbum_arkhon_codex\|Verbum Arkhon]], a doctrine he considers inflexible to the point of ruin. He harbors a deep hatred for [[_wiki_en/personagens/wijmarv\|Wijmarv]], the [[_wiki_en/racas/varnakiano\|Varnakian]] shaman whom he calls *mraz’kor*, “Heart of Ash”: the shaman fled back to [[_wiki_en/locais/varnak\|Varnak]] when passage through the underground ceased to be safe, and his treatise delegitimizes the draconic myth.
