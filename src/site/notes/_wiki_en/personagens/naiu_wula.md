# Naiu’Wula

Naiu’Wula is a wandering [[_wiki_en/racas/gerut\|Gerut]] master of the late [[_wiki_en/conceitos/segunda_era\|Second Era]], before the Reinkardic Wars, remembered as the founder of the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/doutrina_da_alma\|Doctrine of the Soul]] and the philosophical foundation of the [[_wiki_en/magia/teru\|Teru]] magic system. He preached without a chair or a surname, in open spaces, teaching more through demonstration than through argument.

### Doctrine

He taught that all magic is born of the soul, and that [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/magi\|Magi]] is merely “the soul’s trace in the world, not its flame”. For Naiu’Wula, the gods are ancient and vast souls, and mortals, young souls potentially capable of awakening. This awakening arises in moments of profound emotion: it cannot be taught, predicted, or forced. The practice that inherits this view is the “inner listening of Teru”, still passed down today in secret circles of disciples.

### Reception

During his lifetime, he divided the world that heard him. Religious figures called him a heretic, alchemists a charlatan, and academic mages simply ignored him. [[_wiki_en/racas/sylvano\|Sylvan]] elves, Gerut, woodland rebels, and hermits, however, treated him as a prophet. His disciples were mostly pariahs and rebels, organized in discreet circles.

### Legacy

The system derived from his doctrine allows spellcasting without divine subordination, and it is through this path that the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/justiciares\|Justiciars]] access magic. [[_wiki_en/personagens/milorsevogh_velshard\|Milorsevogh Velshard]] masters Teru as a practical heir to Naiu’Wula’s tradition. At the opposite extreme stands [[_wiki_en/personagens/jospin_valheorn\|Jospin Valheorn]], who lived later: Jospin’s materialism and Naiu’Wula’s spiritualism are poles that never recognized each other, and Jospin does not mention him in his writings.
