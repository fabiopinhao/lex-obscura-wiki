# Lucian

Lucian, venerated as Saint Lucian, was a human warrior from the time of the [[_wiki_en/racas/luminares\|Luminars]], canonized in [[_wiki_en/locais/myrgarde\|Myrgarde]] some time after his death. He gives his name to the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/igreja_de_sao_lucian\|Church of Saint Lucian]], the city’s cathedral, and the House of Saint Lucian, the orphanage adjoining the temple.

### Life

He lived in the era when the [[_wiki_en/racas/alvarar\|Álvarars]] emerged and, after them, the [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]], and remained human: he became neither an Álvarar nor a Shadar. He took up arms in defense of the Luminars against human aggression, fighting against those of his own race at a time when human armies were advancing on elven domains. He prophesied the [[_wiki_en/eventos/queda_dos_luminares\|Fall of the Luminars]] and died in battle. His fame spread across the continent of [[_wiki_en/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]].

### Worship

In Myrgarde, before the conquest, Saint Lucian presided over worship alongside [[_wiki_en/panteao/borjean\|Saint Borjean]]; they were the two warrior saints of the city’s human faith. His symbol is the silver wing-blade, worn around the neck as a pendant by devotees and reproduced on amulets. Images show him as a warrior saint, with painted eyes and a serene expression, and in the stained-glass windows of the High Temple of Myrgarde he subdues demonic legions.

Devotion is also domestic: some of the faithful keep his image at home and pray to him in their daily lives, as [[_wiki_en/personagens/marta\|Marta]] did. In the chapel of [[_wiki_en/locais/niedellin\|Niedellin]], the image of Saint Lucian stands beside the altar, along with that of Saint Merania, and Borjean’s niche stands between the two.

### In Time of War

During the mobilization against the Shadars of the north, the church of Saint Lucian in Myrgarde was sealed, and its activities moved to the High Temple, which also served as a recruitment post. In [[_wiki_en/locais/valdris\|Valdris]], in the years preceding the city’s fall, his name and that of Saint Borjean were invoked in taverns by both factions, and a rumor circulated that the saints now blessed Myrgarde’s advance on the city, or that their images had been melted down to mint coins.
