# Calel

Calel is a young [[_wiki_en/racas/sylvano\|Sylvan]] ranger of fourteen summers, brother of [[_wiki_en/personagens/mirin\|Mirin]] and a member of [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/resistencia_de_thal_serin\|the Resistance of Thal’Serin]]. An orphan and [[_wiki_en/personagens/gaelwyn\|Gaelwyn]]’s apprentice, he is the fastest in the group after the master himself. Where others see brush and shadow, he sees routes, and this is how he survives: reading the world as a system of paths and risks before setting foot in it.

### Appearance

He has the build of someone still growing: two years younger than his sister, with a voice that breaks with youth. His fingers are perpetually stained with cartographer’s charcoal, a mark of the craft he practices at every stop. When tension mounts, his hand seeks the hilt of his curved dagger in a nervous gesture he cannot fully control.

### Origin and Role

He grew up without parents, taken in by the resistance and trained by Gaelwyn in the ranger’s craft. He learns to move through the woods of [[_wiki_en/locais/thal_serin\|Thal’Serin]] and memorizes the secret trails linking the refuge to [[_wiki_en/locais/niedellin\|Niedellin]], routes that few know and that he records on parchment as he travels them. He is chosen for a mission of trust: to carry to [[_wiki_en/personagens/abramis\|Abramis]], in Niedellin, an encrypted warning message, darkness wears scepter and bone. [[_wiki_en/personagens/koliah\|Koliah]] instructs him to run without answering if he crosses paths with the steward, who knows the refuge’s location. Calel is the resistance’s new generation, raised in the shadow of a threat he still cannot fully name.

### Method and Voice

His caution borders on obsession. He trusts neither memory nor chance: he draws what he sees, turns trails into lines and danger into annotations. To him, the map is a language, perhaps the only one in which he feels fluent, and planning is less a habit than a way to stay alive. He thinks before acting, gauges the exit before the entrance, and prefers the longer route to the riskier one. Even so, he has the haste of a young man who wants to prove his worth, held in check with difficulty by the master’s voice that echoes in every decision.

Where Mirin reads the open forest as a gift, Calel reads it as a house without doors, and the traps he scatters along the trails serve him as locks: a covered pit where the boars pass, a sinew snare at the bend in the stream, wooden teeth. Every night, before sleeping, he goes over each one from memory, like someone locking a house door by door. His sister calls it a fixation. The traps also serve as his sentinels: it is through them, rather than tracking, that he notices the movement of the [[_wiki_en/criaturas/troll\|trolls]], when dawn reveals trampled pits and snares snapped without any prey.

He keeps what does not fit on the map. He adds people and questions to a mental tally, and records the rule he receives without explanation alongside the others. When instruments fail, he has only memorization left: he fixes what he saw in the order he saw it, the way he memorizes trails he may need to retrace in the dark.

With his sister, he maintains a code of their own, tactile and gestural, learned so thoroughly that words are unnecessary: a touch on the ear warns that someone is approaching, a clenched fist indicates that a secret has been noticed, two quick touches and one long touch on the elbow ask for silence in the face of danger.

### Relationships

With his sister Mirin, he forms a contrast that defines them both: he plans, she acts. From Gaelwyn he inherits the craft and the place of apprentice, and follows him as one follows north. In the master, however, he records a sign he does not know how to read, an old mark he carries on his body that Calel notes as he would a terrain feature, without having the key to decipher it. It is this mixture of devotion and bewilderment that moves him: he trusts Gaelwyn without understanding him, obeys Koliah without having seen the enemy’s entire face, and carries his message out into the world knowing only that he must arrive.

When it comes to Koliah, brother and sister are divided. Where Mirin resents the newcomer’s arrival, Calel defends her for what she delivers: safe routes and steady supplies, and the fact that even the older druids begin to listen to her.

In dealing with others, he is a peacemaker: he skirts conflict with his sister instead of going through it, and answers authority with a smooth reply that leaves no tracks.
