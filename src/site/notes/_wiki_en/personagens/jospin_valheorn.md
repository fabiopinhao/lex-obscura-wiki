# Jospin Valheorn

Jospin Valheorn is a human alchemist and theorist of magic, born in [[_wiki_en/locais/myrgarde\|Myrgarde]], author of *De Magia Alchemica: Fundamenta Physica* and founder of [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/teoria_materialista\|the Materialist Theory]]. A historical figure, he lives on through his intellectual legacy. He was executed by Myrgarde itself during the Reinkardic Wars.

### The Materialist Theory

He postulated that all magic is manipulation of [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/magi\|Magi]], a real and invisible alchemical force, and defined the gods by their abundance of Magi, not by spiritual status. He considered alchemy the only conscious and autonomous form of magic, treated [[_wiki_en/magia/teru\|Teru]] as a “metabolic anomaly,” and rejected the concept of the soul, consistent with a system that measures the sacred by the quantity of Magi and not by spiritual essence. His voice comes through only in the written word: an academic, precise, radically materialist tone.

### Legacy and Irony

He descended into the underground laboratories of the [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]] and worked there for military and energy purposes, which earned him accusations of treason in the city where he had been born. Alongside the military accusation stood a doctrinal one: denying the soul in a capital that had the human faith of [[_wiki_en/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]] as its civic center made him a heretic before it made him a traitor. The city that executed him would later fall under Shadar rule. Copies of his treatises were destroyed or banned in several kingdoms, but his influence spread among rebellious humans, technomage goblins, engineers of the Cult, and independent scholars. Surface gnomes venerate him as their greatest theorist, and most educated Shadars, including [[_wiki_en/personagens/nidzina_avaroth\|Nidzina]], accept his materialist theories as a valid explanation for magical phenomena. His theory was officially adopted by the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|Shadow Empire]]. [[_wiki_en/personagens/naiu_wula\|Naiu’Wula]], who preceded him, represents the opposite pole: a spiritualism that Jospin’s materialism never recognized.
