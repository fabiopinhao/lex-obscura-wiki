# Karashim

The Karashim are the dwarves of [[_wiki_en/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]], miners, smiths, and caravan traders, possibly the oldest race on the continent after the [[_wiki_en/racas/luminares\|Luminars]]. They have inhabited mountains and underground regions since before any named era, and whenever someone digs deep enough anywhere on the continent, they find Karashim chisel marks on a rock no one should have reached. The race’s origin is as ambiguous as that of humans: no creation myth has survived intact, and those that exist contradict one another across clans. The only constant is that the Karashim were already in the mountains when everything else began.

### Cosmogonic Position

The Karashim have no dominant divine bond, and this absence is no deficiency, but a singular position in the spectrum of races. Conventional magic ignores them and they ignore conventional magic. Some are devoted to angels, others are unaffiliated pragmatists, and most treat the gods with the same respect they give a customer, being polite while he pays and forgetting him when he stops. “Karashim” is the proper name of the dwarven race; the [[_wiki_en/racas/derro\|Derro]] share their underground habitat and the same caravan routes, but are a distinct race, gnomes and not dwarves.

### Physiology

Their bodies are square, broad-shouldered, with short, dense legs, and range in height from one meter twenty to one meter fifty. Their calloused, thick-fingered hands achieve surprising precision in fine work, their brute strength exceeds that of humans of the same weight, and their resistance to impact and heat is notable. Their longevity is exceptional, up to one hundred and fifty years, the greatest among non-elven races: at eighty they still work at the forges, and at one hundred and twenty they slow down without stopping, since retirement is a foreign concept. Those who live underground for generations develop grayish skin, wide pupils, and pale irises, environmental rather than racial variations.

Beards are a mark of identity, braided with strands of copper, iron, silver, or gold according to clan and trade. A Karashim who knows how to read beards identifies clan, guild, accomplishments, and marital status at a glance. Cutting off a Karashim’s beard is a humiliation equivalent to social execution.

### Psychology

If there were one word to sum up the race, it would be resentment. Holding grudges is a cultural trait so deep that the Karashim themselves have ceased to recognize it as a choice: grievances are cataloged, classified by severity, and passed down to children as an inheritance as tangible as the tools of a trade. A clan betrayed six generations ago still demands payment of the debt, and longevity aggravates the problem, since a Karashim lives long enough to nurture the resentment personally for a century.

Added to this trait is a mutilated pride. The Karashim know they are the best smiths, miners, and engineers in Reinkard, and they know that [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|the Empire]] depends on them for every sword, every suit of armor, and every siege mechanism; they know, and it changes nothing, because the competence that should have meant freedom has become a collar. Among themselves, however, they are loud, generous, and outgoing: they feast abundantly, drink enthusiastically, and take food seriously. Outside their circle of trust, the same race that sings arm in arm becomes stony-faced and weighs every word, with a distrust of foreigners rooted in centuries of betrayal.

### Social Structure

Society is organized along two axes. The **clan** is lineage, mountain of origin, and ancestry; the **guild** is trade, competence, and specialization. Every Karashim belongs to a clan by birth and a guild by choice, and these loyalties overlap and frequently conflict. The clans trace their origins to specific mountains, each mountain range producing its own, and clans from different mountains are ancient rivals, with grievances reaching back to the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/primeira_era\|First Era]]. The guilds of smiths, miners, traders, engineers, and stonemasons cut across clans and serve as neutral ground, arbitrating trade disputes and protecting technical secrets. There is no hereditary aristocracy: leadership is collective and pragmatic, and whoever solves problems leads for as long as the problem exists.

The principal trading house is [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/khelg_dur\|Khelg’Dur]], the Black Anvil Convoy, which operates caravans of [[_wiki_en/criaturas/isopode\|isopods]] through the tunnels of [[_wiki_en/locais/vor-ghol\|Vor-Ghol]] along routes mapped over centuries. The house acts as an autonomous entity, negotiating with [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Druls]], [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]], and anyone who pays, transporting cargo without asking about its contents and delivering on time. [[_wiki_en/personagens/borzrun\|Borzrun]] leads the main caravan, his gray beard tied with copper wire and his humor as dry as that of someone who has seen everything in the depths.

### Culture

Guild banquets are the central celebration: heavy food and dark beer served at long tables where guild and clan sit together, and they are the only occasions when rivalries are suspended by convention. Fights during the banquet are taboo; fights immediately afterward are tradition. Professional accomplishments are recorded in beard braids, in a system of metal strands, knots, and beads so complex that it functions as a visual language legible only among Karashim. The rhythm of work at the forge produces songs with a musical cadence, passed down from master to apprentice, whose lyrics mix technical instruction and resentment concentrated in verse.

Serious offenses between clans are engraved on iron plates kept in the halls, and no one destroys them. Reconciliation requires a public ceremony in which the plate is melted down before both clans and the resulting metal is forged into a utilitarian object offered to the offended party. Such meltings are exceedingly rare. Funerals are private: the body is cremated in the clan’s forge, the ashes are mixed into the next batch of molten metal, and the first object forged from that metal is kept by the family, so that the Karashim carry their dead in their tools.

### Life Cycle

Karashim are born one at a time, and twins are exceedingly rare. Childhood is long: they are considered adults at twenty-five. Training in a trade begins at ten and lasts decades, and anyone who changes guilds after forty is viewed with suspicion. Marriages are practical arrangements between clans, frequently used to seal alliances or end disputes, with varying success. Children belong to their mother’s clan and follow their father’s trade. Separations are rare and stigmatized.

### Wars with the Drakhonins

Shadar records of the ancient wars describe the Karashim’s battles against the [[_wiki_en/racas/draconideo\|Drakhonins]] as legendary. The Drakhonins, a people of the western continent, were not known for their skill with metal and, to obtain weapons of dwarven quality, invaded and plundered the fortresses inside the mountains. The practice ceased when [[_wiki_en/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]] took those fortresses.

### Relationship with the Empire

The Karashim citadels resisted the [[_wiki_en/eventos/sopro_do_eclipse\|Breath of the Eclipse]] in the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/segunda_era\|Second Era]] by sealing off the mountains, but did not escape the conquest that followed. During the [[_wiki_en/eventos/guerras_reinkardicas_inicio\|Reinkardic Wars]], the independent mountains were taken in a coordinated campaign between Shadars on the surface and Druls underground, and [[_wiki_en/locais/thandruk_varn\|Thandruk Varn]] fell through conditional surrender, with forges redirected and Luminar artifacts handed over. The terms deteriorated into de facto servitude.

The imperial strategic error was bringing rival clans together. Upon taking the smaller citadels, the Empire transferred the clans to Thandruk Varn, centralizing production and ignoring the fact that these clans had been rivals for eras. Grievances once kept in check by geographic distance were confined within the same mountain, and the result was permanent internal disputes, mutual sabotage, and a simmering hostility that no decree resolves. The grudge plates, which accumulate gradually in other halls, occupy entire halls in Thandruk Varn. The citadel has been at a standstill for two moons, new legions appear with weapons of unknown origin, and the Justiciar assigned to the site has disappeared without a trace. Khelg’Dur operates outside this conflict, along Vor-Ghol’s caravan routes, transporting imperial cargo and Drul cargo with the same professional indifference.

### Magic and Material Mastery

Without a divine bond, the Karashim cannot consistently access any of the six recognized systems. Karashim mages exist as individual exceptions, adopting the system of the region where they live, but the race as a whole has no arcane tradition. What the Karashim possess is material mastery: artifacts forged by their masters exhibit properties that transcend mundane metallurgy, and imperial scholars debate whether they are magical or purely technical. The Karashim do not participate in the debate; they call it a job well done and do not elaborate.

### Internal Conflicts

The race’s central conflict is not Karashim against the Empire, but Karashim against Karashim, within Thandruk Varn, where clans that hated one another from a distance share forges, dormitories, and corridors. The divide between old and young deepens the fracture: those who lived through the surrender remember what it meant to be free and bear resentment against the Empire with the patience of those who plan in decades, while the young, born under occupation, bear resentment against the elders who surrendered the mountains and call it pragmatism. Hanging over everything is the logic of indispensability, which is both protection and prison: as long as the Empire needs weapons, the Karashim live, but the weapons of unknown origin suggest that there may already be an alternative to that need.
