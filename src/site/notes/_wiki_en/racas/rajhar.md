# Rajhar

Rajhars are large [[_wiki_en/racas/lynxiano\|Lynxians]], nomadic predators of the green world. Where other lineages claim a heritage or carry a civilizational project, Rajhars simply exist: they are born, learn to hunt, roam the forest as long as their bodies keep up, and die where they stop. They form no lasting society because they have no need to.

### Nature

Intelligent, observant, and survivalist, Rajhars have a pragmatic understanding of the world that rivals that of any [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadar]] trader. There is no racial mythology, collective project, or species resentment, nor any origin myth: myths require a community to transmit them, and Rajhars do not form lasting communities. What survives is fragments. Old Rajhars, during rare encounters, mention the *Rakh*, a word that means “forest,” “blood,” and “before” at once: the recognition that there was forest before them and there will be after them, and that the blood they carry belongs to the forest as much as to them. The [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/titas\|titans]] who gave rise to the feline lineage during the [[_wiki_en/eventos/guerras_raciais\|Racial Wars]] do not lay claim to their creation, and Rajhars do not ask.

### Physiology

Standing upright, they measure 1.70 to 2.10 meters, with a muscular, sinewy build, more agile than massive: where the [[_wiki_en/racas/draconideo\|Draconid]] is a fortress, the Rajhar is an ambush. They have retractable, curved claws capable of scaling vertical trunks and tearing tanned leather, a functional opposable thumb (with fine grip control inferior to that of Shadars), vertical pupils, directional hearing through mobile ears, and sensory whiskers that detect vibration and changes in air pressure.

Their fur varies according to the dominant feline sublineage, a phenotypic trait with no correspondence to social organization. Tigers are the largest, striped in amber and black, from tropical forests; lynxes inhabit high altitudes and cold forests, with brown fur and keener hearing; panthers are the most nocturnal, with frequent melanism and superior vision in the dark.

For short periods, Rajhars can run on all fours in a sprinting posture that doubles their speed, without the transformation seen in [[_wiki_en/racas/worg\|worgs]]: the spine does not restructure itself, and the limbs do not change size. The posture is uncomfortable and unsustainable for travel; after a prolonged run, their wrists become inflamed and their shoulders lock up. Their theoretical lifespan ranges from fifty to sixty-five years, but nomadism and isolation greatly reduce the actual average, to around thirty-five to forty: most die from an infected wound, an encounter with a larger predator, or starvation during a dry season.

### Psychology

The defining trait of Rajhars is supreme indifference. They do not hate, despise, or make claims. To them, [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|the Empire]] is a geographical fact to go around, like a mountain range or a wide river, and other races are assessed by risk and usefulness, never by identity. This indifference is more unsettling than hostility because it offers no loyalty to buy, fear to exploit, or pride to provoke. They operate through a territorial pragmatism based on movement, without possession: they know routes, springs, dens, and fruit trees, and this knowledge is survival capital and, when convenient, merchandise. They respond to provocation with predatory patience, acting only when it is efficient.

### Social Structure

There is no social structure in the full sense. Rajhars form temporary pairs or triads for cooperative hunting of large prey, guarding one another during sleep, or crossing dangerous territory, and the group dissolves when its objective is fulfilled. There is no formal leadership: whoever knows the terrain guides, whoever is strongest attacks first, whoever is fastest keeps watch, and the role disappears along with the group. Mating is seasonal and casual, with no lasting bond between parents; the mother raises the cubs to maturity and drives them out, and the father, in most cases, never knows he had offspring.

### Culture

There is no organized oral tradition, collective ritual, or art, only a repertoire of behaviors passed from mother to cub during the years of rearing. Rajhars mark trees with coded scratches (direction of travel, presence of danger, availability of water, warning of disease), a signaling system that is read as tracks are read. They receive a recognition sound from their mother in childhood, discarded at maturity; adults identify one another by scent, fur, and scars. When they need to offer a name to other races, for trade or a mercenary contract, they invent one on the spot, and the same Rajhar may have six names in six different cities.

### Voice

Rajhars speak little, and what little they say is observation: they describe what they perceive and let the listener draw the conclusion. Their sentences are short and use concrete vocabulary, “smells like blood” in place of “there is danger.” They inform without arguing, persuading, or threatening, and if the information is not enough, they fall silent. They use the third person to generalize, “a hunter does not sleep near stagnant water,” because the world does not revolve around the speaker. When irritated, they stop speaking, and silence is the warning that precedes the claws.

### Relationship with the Empire

The Empire barely recognizes Rajhars as a racial category: in the censuses of [[_wiki_en/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]], they appear as “humanoid beasts of the green,” when they appear at all. Contact occurs at the margins. Individual Rajhars appear in frontier villages, trading posts, and [[_wiki_en/racas/pindoriar\|Pindoriar]] camps offering what no one else supplies: herbs from deep forests, fungi with alchemical properties that rot before traveling the roads, pelts absent from official bestiaries, and information about routes the Empire has never mapped. They act because there is demand and they have access, without anti-imperial ideology. For subjugated races, a Rajhar at the edge of the market may mean medicine, an alchemical component, or an escape route; to the Empire, it is trade too marginal to justify organized repression. They accept occasional mercenary contracts, with a reputation for quiet efficiency: they guide, protect, and disappear at the end without negotiating bonuses or demanding gratitude, but they also disappear if treated as subordinates.

### Magic

Rajhars access the green through [[_wiki_en/magia/symbiosis_naturae\|Symbiosis Naturae]], but in a way that defies the system’s definition: without invoking spirits, maintaining balance, or recognizing a network, they practice arcane instinct, and the green is in their blood as breath is in a Draconid’s throat. Its manifestations are sensory and predatory: heightened perception of movement in dense vegetation, suppression of scent and noise while hunting, accelerated wound healing in contact with forest soil, resistance to plant and fungal toxins. There is no invocation or formula.

The cost is proportional and insidious: a progressive territorial compulsion. The more a Rajhar accesses the green, the stronger their anchoring to the territory they roam becomes, until routes cease to be a choice and become a physiological necessity. Old Rajhars who have abused the instinct develop an inability to leave a particular forest: the idea of leaving brings nausea, disorientation, and panic. [[_wiki_en/racas/sylvano\|Sylvans]] call the phenomenon an involuntary bond, the green reclaiming what is its own. For a race whose identity is movement, taking root is equivalent to a living death.

### Internal Conflicts

The [[_wiki_en/magia/macula-sombria\|Shadow-Taint]] and imperial expansion shrink the forests that sustain Rajhar nomadism: travel routes used for generations now cut across imperial clearings, military roads, and deforested Pindoriar settlements, and each forest lost reduces the space in which the race exists. With no community to organize resistance, Rajhars migrate to what remains.

Those who frequent the imperial margins as smugglers and mercenaries come to depend on what the forest does not provide: salt, worked metal, cloth, information on military movements. Those who learn to negotiate in a frontier village return to the deep forest more often and for shorter periods. In this way, the pure nomad gradually becomes a frontier nomad, and the frontier nomad is one contract away from settling down.

The lack of a collective identity, which protects Rajhars from organized persecution, also prevents any coordinated defense. When a Rajhar is captured, no one comes for them; when a route is destroyed, no one rebuilds it; and when the species dwindles, no one counts those who are missing.

### Life Cycle

Rajhars are viviparous, with a gestation of about five months; females give birth to one or two cubs, rarely three. A cub is born with soft fur and closed eyes, which open after ten days, and is raised by its mother alone, in a temporary den chosen for safety. Weaning occurs at four months and full maturity around ten years, when the mother drives the young Rajhar out of the den without sentimentality. Most deaths occur in the first five years after expulsion, when young, inexperienced Rajhars make fatal mistakes.
