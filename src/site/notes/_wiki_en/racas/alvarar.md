# Álvarar

The Álvarars are angelic humans touched by celestial blood, paladins of the luminous pantheon whose order dates back to [[_wiki/conceitos/idade_esquecida\|the Forgotten Age]]. They are the ontological opposite of the [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]]: where the latter make pacts with [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/diabos\|devils]], the Álvarars receive celestial blessings.

### Physiology

With stature within the human baseline (they are modified humans, no larger than ordinary humans), they have fair skin and eyes in golden or silver hues, and a physical presence described as crushing, capable of making recruits fall to their knees involuntarily. Their longevity reaches ninety-six to one hundred years, and their bond is with the angels. They bear an outline of spiritual wings around their bodies: these are neither physical nor tangible wings, but an aura that extends slightly backward, suggesting a wingspan without matter. In Álvarars with strong faith, the outline is clear enough to be perceived as a luminous silhouette behind the shoulders in low light; when faith is weakened, it recedes until it almost disappears, and it intensifies during moments of sacred invocation. The manifestation is spiritual, distinct from the physical, feathered wings of the [[_wiki/racas/aquilar\|Aquilars]] and [[_wiki/racas/fringal\|Fringals]].

### Origin

The order arose in the Forgotten Age as the militarized arm of the Church that worshiped the angelic gods; it launched the Crusades against the elves. The Álvarars emerged among the most devout practitioners of [[_wiki_en/magia/devotio_sancta\|Sancta]], magic through faith, which preceded them in the Crusades. The Álvarar identity became established in the [[_wiki/conceitos/primeira_era\|First Era]], in the schism that followed the [[_wiki/eventos/queda_dos_luminares\|Fall of the Luminars]]: of the humans who participated in the massacre, those who sought absolution made faith the home of penance, and those who embraced guilt broke away and became the Shadars.

### Culture and Society

In the pre-conquest period, the Álvarars occupied the top of the military and religious hierarchy of [[_wiki/locais/myrgarde\|Myrgarde]]: an organized, visible, and militant force. The conquest of Myrgarde and [[_wiki/locais/elenir\|Elenir]] dismantled this public structure. At present, they are not extinct, but have become rare and reclusive: they maintain culture, hierarchy, and religious practice in hidden sanctuaries, small communities in valleys protected by inhospitable geography, and contemplative orders in semi-abandoned fortresses. Seclusion preserved the lineage, the practice of Sancta, and transmission across generations, at the cost of political invisibility and the loss of their historical military reach. [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|The Empire]] presumes the lineage extinct and acts as though it were: the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/culto_sombrio\|Shadow Cult]] no longer searches, considering the work done. It is this operational error that sustains Álvar survival.

The most celebrated Álvarar of Myrgarde is Sir [[_wiki/personagens/grimald\|Grimald]], the Golden Blade: golden armor and the crushing presence characteristic of the lineage. He detected an entity hidden in the pocket of a human recruit from Myrgarde and rejected his enlistment, saying that the young man carried something that did not belong there.

### Weaknesses

Seclusion is forced and takes its toll: an exceedingly rare lineage, small communities, an existence dependent on geography and secrecy. Moral rigidity, with purity elevated to a principle, has already led to public defeat and remains a cultural trait even in seclusion.
