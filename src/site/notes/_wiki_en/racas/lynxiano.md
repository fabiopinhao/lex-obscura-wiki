# Lynxian

The Lynxians are a family of feline hybrid humanoids, linked to the titans, nomadic, and without fixed territory. The family comprises two subraces that share little beyond feline traits: the [[_wiki_en/racas/rajhar\|Rajhars]], of humanoid stature, solitary predators and smugglers on the imperial fringes, and the [[_wiki_en/racas/margau\|Margáus]], of gnomish stature, arboreal symbionts more integrated into the civilized green world.

### Origin

Like all hybrid humanoids, the Lynxians trace their origins to the [[_wiki_en/eventos/guerras_raciais\|Racial Wars]], when green entities, the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/titas\|titans]], crossed humans with wild felines to produce predators aligned with the natural world. Titanic interference operates according to a different logic from demonic or diabolic interference: where [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/demonios\|demons]] forged soldiers and [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/diabos\|devils]] shaped agents, the titans merely made room. There was no fusion ritual or deliberate selection; the green absorbed what was feline in the material plane and returned something that walked on two legs.

The consequence is a race without a founding myth. [[_wiki_en/racas/draconideo\|Draconids]] claim ancestral dragons, [[_wiki_en/racas/worg\|worgs]] carry theories about their genesis, [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orcs]] know the fire that rewrote them; the Lynxians have no origin narrative because the titans do not narrate. No titan claims them as devotees, servants, or wards: the relationship is ecological, not theological. The titan that inhabits the forest where a Rajhar hunts does not know the Rajhar’s name, and the Rajhar does not know the titan’s name. Both continue to exist.

### The Two Subraces

The Rajhars, of humanoid stature, are nomadic predators with fur that varies according to sublineage (tiger, lynx, panther), retractable claws, and a partial capacity for quadrupedal locomotion. They move between the deep forests and the fringes of [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|the Empire]] as rare mercenaries and smugglers of exotic goods. Solitary by nature, they form temporary pairs or triads for cooperative hunting and disperse when the objective is achieved, without claiming territory, forming clans, or offering devotion.

The Margáus, of gnomish stature, are compact, arboreal felines, symbiotic with the flora and smaller fauna. Less wild and less independent, they found a niche within the organized green world: they live on the outskirts of [[_wiki_en/racas/sylvano\|Sylvan]] and [[_wiki_en/racas/elarino\|Elarin]] communities, integrated enough not to be intruders and distant enough not to be members.

### The Non-Rupture

The relationship between Rajhars and Margáus is the absence of a relationship. There was no primordial coexistence to break, no symbiosis to degenerate, no hierarchy to invert. The [[_wiki_en/racas/vulfano\|Vulfans]] had packs before predation, the [[_wiki_en/racas/sauriano\|Saurians]] have the active rejection of Draconids by [[_wiki_en/racas/kobold\|kobolds]]; the Lynxians do not even have the energy of conflict. Rajhars and Margáus cross paths in the forests and move on, without recognizing one another as family or rejecting one another as intruders. The “Lynxian” classification is an imposition by imperial naturalists who grouped everything with fur and vertical pupils under the same entry.

### Decline

The Lynxians are part of the phenomenon of debestialization that affects all hybrid races, but the decline operates less visibly. There is no clan fragmentation to document, no urban populations to census, and no servitude to quantify: the Lynxians simply appear less often. The forests shrink under the [[_wiki_en/magia/macula-sombria\|Shadow-Taint]], hunting routes narrow, and transit territories diminish; a Rajhar who ranged across a hundred leagues of continuous forest two generations ago now encounters imperial clearings every twenty, and the Margáus, more dependent on stable ecosystems, retreat as the Sylvan communities retreat. The Empire does not pursue them with the determination it employs against rebellious Sylvans, because pursuit would first require finding them, and finding them would require the Lynxians to stand still. The decline is silent: fewer cubs, fewer encounters, fewer tracks.
