# Hallirin

Hallirins are surface gnomes, artisans and alchemists long integrated into human society. They constitute the gnomish baseline, the original reference from which the other subraces ([[_wiki_en/racas/derro\|Derros]], [[_wiki_en/racas/goblin\|goblins]], [[_wiki_en/racas/spriggin\|Spriggins]], and [[_wiki_en/racas/nalvirin\|Nalvirins]]) derived through corruption, adaptation, or migration.

### Origin

Hallirins have no founding event. They were neither corrupted by demonic emanations like goblins, nor shaped by centuries of isolation like Derros, nor drawn to titanic forests like Spriggins, nor did they migrate to distant continents like Nalvirins. They stayed where they were: on the plains, in cities, in markets. Their natural affinity with angels does not indicate innate sacredness, but the same religious tendency as humans. Hallirins and humans gravitate toward the same gods because they share the same habitat and the same position in the world, ordinary mortals without a predetermined patron, and for them faith, when it exists, is a choice and not a racial imposition.

Integration with human societies is ancient, but not primordial. In earlier eras, gnomes lived in communities of their own, separated by custom and mutual distrust. The rapprochement was gradual, driven by trade and mutual usefulness: humans needed precision artisans, and Hallirins needed larger markets than any gnomish community could sustain. In the cities on the plains of [[_wiki_en/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]], coexistence became established in mixed neighborhoods and shared guilds. It was, however, a metropolitan phenomenon: Hallirins were concentrated in centers such as [[_wiki_en/locais/myrgarde\|Myrgarde]] and [[_wiki_en/locais/elenir\|Elenir]], where population density justified specialization, and were absent from rural villages and remote settlements such as [[_wiki_en/locais/niedellin\|Niedellin]].

### Physiology

Of standard gnomish build, with a typical height between one point one and one point three meters, Hallirins have proportionate physiques, without the deformities of Derros or the oily texture of goblins. Their skin has human tones, their hair varies in color, and their ears are slightly pointed. Their hands are nimble, with proportionately long fingers, a universal gnomish inheritance that in them translates into artisanal dexterity. A lifespan of one hundred years is the gnomish standard without factors that shorten it, attained when the world permits.

### Psychology

The defining trait is practical ingenuity: genuine interest in how things work, an aptitude for solving problems with what is at hand, pleasure in work well done. It is gnomish curiosity in its uncorrupted form. Where goblins transformed it into compulsive activity and Derros converted it into avarice, Hallirins maintain the original balance. Intelligence compensates for physical weakness, channeled into production. Socially, they are adaptable and pragmatic, not subservient: they know that the world belongs to larger races and that surviving in it requires being useful.

### Culture and Society

Hallirins have no political structure of their own. In human cities they organized themselves by trade, not by lineage: alchemists’ guilds, artisans’ associations, trade cooperatives. The social unit was professional. In Myrgarde, Alchemists’ Street was the informal center of the Hallirin community, without anyone having designated it as such. Their culture is functional and values competence, quality of work, and reputation; celebrations are modest, tied to milestones in their trades. There is no distinct gnomish religious tradition, and those who practice a faith follow the local cults, predominantly white, of [[_wiki_en/panteao/azazel\|Azazel]] and [[_wiki_en/panteao/israfil\|Israfil]]. Names are short and practical, like [[_wiki_en/personagens/pip\|Pip]], a trader from Myrgarde: among Hallirins, the name suffices and the work speaks for the rest.

### Relationship with Magic

Aligned with angels, Hallirins access magic through [[_wiki_en/magia/devotio_sancta\|Devotio Sancta]], on the same terms as humans: genuine faith as a channel, vulnerability to a crisis of faith, protection against demonic corruption. In practice, Hallirin spellcasters are exceedingly rare, since gnomish magical alignment is lower than that of elven, human, and bestial races. The race’s aptitude finds expression less in invocation and more in alchemy and the technical handling of arcane substances: a Hallirin understands how a reagent works without being able to reproduce the effect through their own will.

### Under the Empire

The [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadar]] conquest destroyed the integration that had taken generations to build. The cities of the plains fell one by one, and with them the entire infrastructure of the Hallirin community collapsed. There was no organized exodus or significant resistance: Hallirins are small, unarmed, and dependent on urban structures that ceased to exist. In the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/terceira_era\|Third Era]], virtually all were killed or captured, and the survivors work enslaved beside the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/nexus\|arcane nexuses]] of the old Crystal Fortress, in Elenir. The [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|Empire]] employs them as technical labor in reagent extraction, arcane equipment maintenance, and the distillation of [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/magi\|magi]], because Shadars do not do meticulous work, [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orcs]] lack the dexterity, and goblins improvise too much for precision tasks. It is enslavement through competence: the same aptitude that integrated Hallirins into human society now chains them to imperial service. The result is an invisible people, too small to be noticed, too useful to be eliminated, too scarce to be feared.
