# Drul

Druls are diabolic elves from [[_wiki/locais/vor-ghol\|Vor-Ghol]], descendants of the [[_wiki_en/racas/luminares\|Luminars]] who broke with the people of the light and descended into the depths. They organize themselves into Matriarchal Houses, have practiced the diabolic pact since before any other race, and cultivate treachery as a virtue.

### Origin

In [[_wiki/conceitos/idade_esquecida\|the Forgotten Age]], a faction of elves broke with the people of the light and descended to Vor-Ghol, where they forged the first diabolic pacts. The separation was not abrupt: entire generations adapted to the depths before recognizing themselves as a distinct race. What defined them was not darkness itself, but the choice to embrace it as an advantage, not as exile. They traded Luminar light for the illumination of bioluminescent fungi and the cold glow of their new gods’ eyes. The Luminar heritage persists in their bodies (longevity, night vision, a fine and resilient bone structure) and in their pride: Druls consider themselves superior to all races by lineage and merit, survivors of Vor-Ghol.

### Physiology

Skin ranges from purple-black to bluish gray, with subtle gleams reminiscent of polished stones. Their eyes develop bioluminescent pulsations that reflect strong emotions, in shades of violet, red, gold, or electric green; a dangerous female Drul has learned to keep her eyes dull. Night vision allows them to see with absolute clarity up to forty meters in total darkness, but direct sunlight dazzles them and causes discomfort, and the discomfort increases with exposure without blinding them: on the surface, they seek shade and avoid the high sun. Hair white as webbing, silver as mercury, or black with metallic highlights. Modified salivary glands produce paralytic toxins, a legacy of generations consuming poisonous fungi. Their canines are sharp. Their lifespan is around one hundred twenty years, and their bond is with devils. Aging is subtle: the skin darkens, ocular bioluminescence weakens, and the hands reveal the age the face conceals. Prolonged exposure to black magic accelerates this darkening and can produce streaks of obsidian beneath the epidermis, similar to the black veins of pact-bound Shadars, but arranged in straight lines and angles rather than the organic pattern of veins.

### Social Structure

Drul society is matriarchal, vertical, ritualized, and lethal. Power is concentrated in the Matriarchal Houses, each governed by a matriarch who combines religious, political, and magical authority. [[_wiki/organizacoes/casa_durzken\|House Durzken]] dominates [[_wiki/locais/ghilanna\|Ghilanna]], the subterranean capital; [[_wiki/organizacoes/casa_morghast\|House Morghast]], smaller and subordinate, guards the capital’s gates and supplies field [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/ophideias\|ophideias]]. Noble daughters compete for succession with ritualized ferocity, and matricide is an accepted practice: a matriarch who dies of old age is considered weak. Noble sons are relegated to secondary roles, valued as instruments, never as equals: they may become mages, assassins, or military commanders, but they will never govern a House, and diplomacy is forbidden to them because it is a direct extension of matriarchal rule. The discipline that keeps them in their place is physical and public. A male who raises his eyes before a hierophant without permission is forced to polish the obsidian floor with his own tunic, dragging himself on his knees until his superior considers the lesson learned; the punishment is not an exception, it is pedagogy. Covens, magical decision-making circles that bring together matriarchs and hierophants to determine political and spiritual directions, exclude males from every deliberation that matters. Ophideias occupy a singular position: hierophants trained from childhood as spies and assassins, and the term, from Old Drul, means “she who is a serpent”. An ophideia’s ultimate loyalty depends on the ophideia, and it is this ambiguity that makes them indispensable and dangerous to all sides.

### The Ruling Houses

Vor-Ghol is governed by six ruling matriarchs, each at the head of her House:

- [[_wiki/personagens/nylothrae_durzken\|Nylothrae Durzken]], of House Durzken, dominant in Ghilanna;
- [[_wiki/personagens/quavarra_ulvondra\|Quavarra Ulvondra]], the Obsidian Spider, of House Ulvondra, in [[_wiki/locais/xarn_zareth\|Xarn’Zareth]];
- [[_wiki/personagens/belarbreza_vhaskrith\|Belarbreza Vhaskrith]], the Torment-Forger, ruling over the Chambers of Trial;
- [[_wiki/personagens/zilvra_xund_ala\|Zilvra Xund’ala]], the Poisoned Hand, who operates on the surface in the Tower of [[_wiki/locais/khargoth\|Khargoth]], in [[_wiki/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]];
- [[_wiki/personagens/sszaflyn_t_orghvyss\|Sszaflyn T’orghvyss]], the Mother of Beasts, from Ghilanna;
- [[_wiki/personagens/voldriz_vrinn_dal\|Voldriz Vrinn’dal]], the Weaver of Chains, from Ghilanna.

Lesser Houses serve the ruling matriarchs with functional loyalty without holding seats of their own, and distant citadels such as [[_wiki/locais/xilaverth\|Xilaverth]], in the west, resist Ghilanna’s hegemony with varying autonomy.

### Culture and Customs

Names follow a matrilineal structure: a personal name followed by the House name, which precedes the individual in every formal introduction. Treachery is a virtue: murders within one’s own clan are legitimate means of advancement, provided they are carried out with competence and discretion; failing in an attempt at matricide is a crime punishable by death, succeeding is promotion. Paranoia is endemic: lovers test poisons on each other, mothers check their daughters’ hands. Baubles, humanoid trophies displayed in the matriarchs’ halls, demonstrate power; the same term also names the living captive kept on a chain and adorned with the House’s insignia, generally [[_wiki_en/racas/derro\|Derros]] or other gnomes, a form of slavery for display. The hierarchy of loyalties is clear in theory, family before House and House before race, although in practice each Drul rearranges priorities according to opportunity. The economy is based on slavery, served by Derros, [[_wiki_en/racas/murano\|Murans]], and prisoners of war, and the [[_wiki/artefatos/gema-viva\|living gems]] extracted from the depths circulate as high-value currency and an arcane reagent, guarded with care comparable to that which the matriarchs devote to their most promising daughters. Death involves no mercy: corpses are dissolved in fungal acid or given to scavengers, and public mourning is a sign of weakness.

### Relationship with the Empire

The relationship between Druls and [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|the Empire]] operates under the formal name of [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/alianca_subterranea\|the Underground Alliance]], a pact devised by [[_wiki/personagens/xarn\|Xarn]] in the [[_wiki/conceitos/segunda_era\|Second Era]] when he unified Morvgrad and established relations with Ghilanna. The alliance enabled unprecedented cultural exchange: the [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]] absorbed the Drul culture of secrecy, the Druls gained access to surface resources and influence within the imperial apparatus, and the physical connection is the thousand-fathom elevator between Morvgrad and Vor-Ghol. In theory, it is a partnership between equals; in practice, [[_wiki_en/personagens/arothistar\|Arothistar]] turned partnership into de facto subordination after taking power. Distrust is mutual and ancestral: Shadars consider Druls unpredictable and too loyal to the matriarchs, Druls consider Shadars arrogant and incapable of honoring alliances without dominating them. Druls are full citizens of the Empire, but formal citizenship does not erase the asymmetry of an Empire that is Shadar in language, architecture, law, and government.

### Relationship with Magic

The Druls’ primary access system is [[_wiki_en/magia/pactum_diaboli\|Pactum Diaboli]], identical to that of the Shadars in mechanics, but older in tradition: Druls have practiced diabolic pacts since the Forgotten Age, millennia before Shadars existed as a race. The diabolic pact grants access to the same black schools as those of the Shadars (particularly [[_wiki_en/escolas/anatho\|Anatho]], [[_wiki_en/escolas/necro\|Necro]], [[_wiki_en/escolas/umbra\|Umbra]], and [[_wiki_en/escolas/toxo\|Toxo]]), but Druls have developed three exclusive disciplines: Webweaving manipulates webs to create traps, bridges, and arcane bindings; Deep Umbramancy creates antilight that devours others’ illumination, forcing the battlefield into darkness where they have an absolute advantage; and Poisoncraft is the alchemy of toxins that affect body, perception, soul, and mind in simultaneous layers. The primary ritual language is [[_wiki/linguas/gholvyss\|Gholvyss]], the primordial language of the proto-Druls and the first known diabolic language. The magical cost accumulates in darkening skin, loss of ocular bioluminescence, and, in extreme cases, calcification of the finger joints. Ophideias who operate [[_wiki_en/escolas/spectro\|Spectro]] pay an additional cost: probing bodies and spaces for prolonged periods erodes their very sense of solidity, and veterans report difficulty feeling their own boundaries after decades of spectral service.

### Rhetoric

Druls speak with venomous sarcasm and threatening politeness. Each sentence carries at least two layers: what is said and what is hidden, and complex metaphors are preferred to direct statements. Drul courtesy is a weapon, and Shadars who mistake politeness for submission die from that confusion often enough for the misconception to persist among fools. Profanity draws on the vocabulary of Vor-Ghol (poisonous fungi, decomposition, parasites), and blasphemies invoke dead matriarchs or betrayed gods, never their own pantheon.

### Internal Conflicts

The Druls’ central conflict is existential: are they allies or vassals? The Underground Alliance guaranteed them influence and protection, but also subordinated them to a monarch who does not consult matriarchs. The Houses tolerate the asymmetry because open war would be suicidal, but each generation carries less gratitude and more resentment. Among the Houses, competition never ceases, and the question of gender pulses beneath the surface: males of exceptional talent serve and sometimes die for their Houses without ever governing them. Deeper still is their relationship with their own past: Druls know that they descend from the Luminars and that they traded light for darkness, and in the silent covens the question persists beneath layers of sarcasm and venom: what have we lost?
