# Margáu

Margáus are the gnomish subtype of the [[_wiki_en/racas/lynxiano\|Lynxians]], compact, arboreal humanoid felines with a green arcane mark. They live in the canopies and on the fringes of titanic forests, nestled among [[_wiki_en/racas/sylvano\|Sylvan]] and [[_wiki_en/racas/elarino\|Elarin]] communities in a coexistence that neither side defines precisely. They are the most unusual case among hybrid families: the only one in which the gnomish subrace is more socially integrated than the humanoid one.

### Nature

Where the [[_wiki_en/racas/rajhar\|Rajhars]] refuse to belong, Margáus have found how to occupy space without claiming it. Sylvans treat them neither as citizens nor as servants, and Margáus see themselves neither as members nor as visitors: they are there, and the trees do not complain. The reversal of the hybrid pattern is notable, because in every other family the gnomish subrace is marginalized, degraded, or extinct, while among Lynxians the smaller has found a place and the larger refuses to look for one.

### Physiology

They measure from eighty centimeters to one point one meters in height. Their soft, dense fur varies among brown, golden, mottled cream, and, more rarely, silvery gray. Their large, round eyes, with vertical pupils, are larger than those of Rajhars and adapted to the dim light of the canopies. The long, flexible tail serves as a counterbalance during arboreal locomotion, and the hypermobile ankle joints allow them to descend trunks headfirst, with their feet rotated one hundred eighty degrees. They weigh from fifteen to twenty-five kilograms, with fine bones and musculature geared toward agility, not impact: a Margáu does not survive hand-to-hand combat with any sizable humanoid, and so the strategy is never to be where the blow lands. They are strict carnivores, hunters of birds, rodents, lizards, and eggs, so their relationship with flora is one of cohabitation, not food. Their lifespan ranges from forty to fifty years, and aging announces itself as a loss of flexibility: the old Margáu descends to the ground and does not climb back up.

### Psychology

Caution is intrinsic, not cowardice: the constant calculation of a small creature in a world of larger predators, silent and almost always accurate. Margáus are rarely surprised and almost never surprise others. Their belonging is peripheral and deliberate, on the boundary between member and guest of a community: they contribute vigilance and early warning, but do not participate in councils or submit to hierarchy, and if the community migrates, they may or may not follow it. Their curiosity is observational, directed toward watching, memorizing, and cataloging, and what they do with the information is another matter, often nothing, because the act of observing is already enough.

### Social Structure

Margáus live alone or in temporary breeding pairs, but proximity to Sylvan and Elarin communities creates an indirect sociality. Several may inhabit the canopies around the same settlement, each in its own arboreal territory, without direct interaction: coexistence is spatial, not social. In Elarin communities they are tolerated with distant affection, treated as intelligent resident fauna and valued for detecting intruders in the canopies before the sentinels notice them on the ground. There is no servitude, only a tacit exchange in which the Margáu keeps watch, the community does not expel it, and both pretend the arrangement does not exist.

### Relationship with Magic

The bond with the titans grants access to [[_wiki_en/magia/symbiosis_naturae\|Symbiosis Naturae]], and the canonical definition applies more precisely to Margáus than to Rajhars. The symbiosis is with the immediate ecosystem, the inhabited tree, the hunted fauna, the canopy’s microclimate, and deepens with the length of time spent there. It manifests as perception of disease in nearby plants and animals, stimulation of plant growth on a minimal scale, suppression of scent and sound to the point of near sensory invisibility, and rudimentary communication with smaller fauna. The cost mirrors that of Rajhars with the valence reversed: where the Rajhar is anchored against its will, the Margáu dissolves. Deepening the symbiosis too much softens the contours of personality until the distinction between what is Margáu and what is forest blurs; in extreme cases, the Margáu stops hunting, stops moving, and its fur accumulates moss and lichens that are not parasites but an extension. Sylvans have seen it happen and do not interfere, considering the process natural.

### Life Cycle

They are viviparous, with a gestation period of four months. Females give birth to one or two young, which are born with functional claws and a grasping reflex: the mother carries them on her back for the first months, and those that cannot hold on fall, in a selection by gravity. Weaning is early, at three months, and maturity comes at seven or eight years. Separation is gradual, without rupture: the young one begins sleeping in another tree, farther and farther away, until the distance becomes permanent. Those that grow old in the canopies of Sylvan communities tend to live longer than solitary individuals.
