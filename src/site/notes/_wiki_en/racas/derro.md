# Derro

The Derros are the gnomes of the depths, one of the gnomish subraces, shaped by centuries of isolation and arcane exposure in the deep layers of [[_wiki/locais/vor-ghol\|Vor-Ghol]]. In gnomish nomenclature, “gnome” is the generic term for the family, and each pantheon produced its own subrace: the Derros of the devils, the [[_wiki/racas/goblin\|goblins]] of the demons, the [[_wiki/racas/spriggin\|Spriggins]] of the titans, the [[_wiki/racas/hallirin\|Hallirins]] of the angels, and the [[_wiki/racas/nalvirin\|Nalvirins]] of the sidereals. The Derros are the branch of the depths, and the term “Derro” itself was coined by [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]]; the surface gnomes, in contrast, are the Hallirins, the gnomish baseline from which the others derived. They operate the black market, the moneychanger-burrows, and the supply routes between the underground and the surface, and are known as amoral intermediaries throughout the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|Empire]].

### Degeneration

The term “degenerate” is Shadar and carries a moral judgment that the Derros themselves reject among their own kind and accept when it suits business. The transformation is adaptive, the product of two forces that reinforced each other over generations. Environmental isolation confined them to the deep levels of Vor-Ghol, without sunlight and with a diet restricted to fungi and lichens: their bodies shrank, their skin lost pigment, their eyes dilated, and their bones grew thinner. Passive arcane exposure did the rest, since Vor-Ghol is saturated with magical residue, especially in the layers where the moss glows with dark radiation. The effect is not power; it is erosion: heightened senses, nerves on permanent alert, physiological paranoia. The change is not hereditary in the strict sense; it is environmental and cumulative, and after three or four generations underground, reversal becomes unlikely. The Shadar characterization attributes degeneration to isolation and dark pacts, but the “dark pact” is a half-truth convenient to imperial judgment: some Derros do indeed practice [[_wiki_en/magia/pactum_diaboli\|Pactum Diaboli]], but the racial transformation arises from no pact, instead coming from the isolation and arcane contamination of Vor-Ghol.

### Physiology

Typical stature ranges from 1.10 m to 1.30 m, within the gnomish baseline, but a bone structure made slender by generations of adaptation to narrow tunnels distinguishes the Derros from the other gnomish subraces. Their skin is pale to gray, with visible veins at the temples and wrists; their eyes are disproportionately large, with pupils that cover almost the entire iris in darkness, and direct sunlight causes pain. Their ears are pointed and mobile, and their fine, white or mouse-gray hair is usually shaved off or cropped close. Their long fingers have double joints in the index and ring fingers, a trait unique to the race that supports their greatest ability: manual dexterity capable of manipulating locks, forging seals, and setting mechanisms with precision. Their metabolism is slow, and the Derros eat little and store fat efficiently, so the starvation that kills a [[_wiki_en/racas/murano\|Muran]] in days takes weeks to kill a Derro. Their lifespan reaches a hundred years, although actual life expectancy hovers around sixty, shortened by violence and poisoning among peers.

### Psychology

The defining trait of the Derro is avarice as mental architecture. They accumulate information, coins, favors, secrets, and the debts of others, because everything is merchandise and everything has a price. Generosity is a foreign concept, since giving something means losing something, and losing something underground means exposing a flank. Added to avarice is productive paranoia: the Derro assumes that everyone lies and plans to steal, an accurate assumption in Vor-Ghol and only slightly exaggerated on the surface. The result is efficiency, since Derro contracts anticipate contingencies that no one else would imagine. The third layer is calculated theatricality, in which they exaggerate emotions as a tactic, pretending to be fearful, servile, or incompetent to protect their real competence, since underestimating a Derro usually proves costly.

The Derros reverse the Shadar judgment on degeneration: to them, the true degenerates are the surface Hallirins, weak cousins who traded shrewdness for the comfort of light. Among themselves, they call surface gnomes, and by extension every surface dweller, “alvorentos,” an insult whose disputed etymology lies between “high-wanderer,” someone who wanders without a roof, and “close target,” easy to hit. The same distrust turns inward: the Derros who operate on the surface acquire habits that those of Vor-Ghol treat as contamination, tolerance for light, a taste for outside food, and familiarity with Shadar customs, and come to be seen as near-Hallirins, while those above see those below as provincials who do not understand where the money is.

### Moneychanger-Burrows and the Guild

The basic social unit is the moneychanger-burrow, a mixture of trading post, vault, currency exchange, and family residence, managed by an extended family and functioning as a node in an underground trade network. Rival burrows compete for routes, customers, and suppliers, and the competition is rarely violent: physically fragile, the Derros prefer poison, sabotage, and defamation to direct confrontation. The main Derro institution on the surface is the [[_wiki/organizacoes/guilda_dos_gnomos\|Gnome Guild]] of the West Market, in [[_wiki/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]], which brings together hundreds of members and operates the black market under the seal of [[_wiki_en/personagens/arothistar\|Arothistar]], paying regular tribute to the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/culto_sombrio\|Cult]] in exchange for tolerance. Its mercantile leader is [[_wiki_en/personagens/dribbel_olgund\|Dribbel Olgund]], the Trade-Breaker, a covert banker and enforcer of agreements, who has an unusual bodyguard accompany him, the [[_wiki/criaturas/gnoll\|gnoll]] [[_wiki_en/personagens/fandegarron\|Fandegarrón]]. For the citizens of Morvgrad, this market is both a curse and a relief, a place where nobles obtain elixirs, unregistered weapons change hands, and truths are bought or erased.

### Culture

Derro life is permeated by commercial signs and rituals. Toll ink, which glows only under glow-slime, marks the passages of Vor-Ghol and permanently stains the hands of frequent users, functioning as an involuntary credential for those who know the routes. Prosperous Derros keep display-vaults, locked and trapped, at the entrance to the burrow: the contents matter less than the message that there is something to guard and resources to take revenge on anyone who attacks it. They accumulate transaction-names, nicknames derived from memorable deals that replace the family name in everyday use and function as a walking résumé. Even liquidation feasts are business, banquets at which each dish is priced and guests pay with future favors.

### Relationship with the Empire

The Derros operate one step ahead of the law, or one step within it when convenient. Imperial tolerance depends on usefulness: they supply the Empire with what it needs without its wanting to know the origin, from small and lethal artifacts to informants in every caste, forged documents, and calibrated poisons, and the Guild pays tribute punctually because tribute paid on time guarantees invisibility. They are neither subjugated like the [[_wiki_en/racas/karashim\|Karashim]] nor ignored like the Murans; they occupy a third space, tolerated as useful parasites in a symbiotic and fragile relationship. The awareness that this tolerance depends on a stamp they do not control breeds muted resentment and obsessive contingency planning: hidden vaults, escape routes, and alternative identities kept in case protection is revoked.

### Magic

The arcane exposure of Vor-Ghol gives the Derros a residual sensitivity to mana that does not translate into formal spellcasting: most sense arcane currents, detect magical traps, and perceive when an object carries an enchantment, without being able to replicate the effect. Formal access comes through Pactum Diaboli, and the few who practice magic gravitate toward alchemy and the enchantment of mechanisms, making potions with brutal effects, arcane traps, and forged seals. Their mastery lies in craftsmanship: the Derro does not invoke, but makes. The cost manifests as sensory erosion, worsening the deterioration of senses already worn down by contamination, so those who abuse magic go blind, go deaf, or lose their sense of touch. For a race that depends on the dexterity of its fingers, losing touch means losing everything.

### Voice

For the Derro, every word is a transaction. They talk a great deal, and their speech assesses, prices, and positions with every sentence, with commercial vocabulary applied even to matters that have nothing to do with commerce, cost, margin, collateral, interest, liquidate. Emotional exaggeration is a tool: they beg, whine, flatter, and threaten according to how the other person reacts, and resort to diminutives such as “little thing,” “little service,” and “little problem” precisely when they want to seem harmless, so the diminutive is usually a sign of danger. When fear is genuine, however, the theater falls away: speech becomes short, precise, and stripped of flourishes, and what remains is pure calculation.

### Internal Conflicts

Competition between moneychanger-burrows is the race’s permanent conflict, fought over supply routes, customer exclusivity, and access to Drul suppliers. Violence is indirect, consisting of poison, sabotage of merchandise, and anonymous denunciations to the Cult, and reconciliation is rare because it requires someone to give something up.

The distance between the surface and the depths also divides them. The Derros who operate above acquire habits that those below consider contamination: tolerance for light, familiarity with Shadar customs, a taste for surface food. Those underground see those above as near-Hallirins; those above see those below as provincials who have not understood where the money is.

The third conflict is usefulness as a leash. The black market operates under an imperial seal, and tolerance can be revoked at any moment. The Derros live knowing that their institutional existence depends on a stamp they do not control, and this dependence breeds muted resentment and obsessive contingency planning: hidden vaults, escape routes, alternative identities.

### Life Cycle

The Derros are born singly or in pairs and have short childhoods: they work in the burrow around the age of eight, operate on their own at fifteen, and are considered adults at twenty. They live up to a century in theory, but violence, poison, and betrayal bring the average down to sixty. Marriages are contracts, with dissolution clauses, and children inherit the burrow, the debts, and the enemies. Even funerals are an inventory: the body is cremated and the belongings divided according to a will that almost always ends up contested.
