# Shadar

The Shadars are diabolical humans, the dominant race of [[_wiki/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]] and full citizens of [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|the Empire]]. The distinction separating them from other humans dates back to the Fall of the [[_wiki_en/racas/luminares\|Luminars]], in the [[_wiki/conceitos/primeira_era\|First Era]], when the humans who participated in the massacre divided: those who embraced guilt became Shadars, seeking in diabolical pacts the strength that a clear conscience did not offer, and those who sought absolution became [[_wiki_en/racas/alvarar\|Álvarars]], raising cathedrals and codes of penance. The schism never healed, and the war between shadow and alabaster that structures Reinkard is, at its core, a dispute between two responses to the same crime. This identity is reenacted in liturgy: in the solemn ceremonies of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/culto_sombrio\|Shadow Cult]], the assembly answers the officiant’s proclamations with the antiphon “We embrace guilt. We do not seek absolution.”, spoken in unison, from the hierophants to the lowliest acolyte.

### Physiology

Shadar skin ranges from pale gray to marble white, with faintly visible veins that darken during the use of magic and return to normal afterward. The pattern of permanent black veins on the forearms, temples, and neck is not a general racial trait: it is a marker of intensive and prolonged use of black magic, typical of necromancers and those bound by high-intensity pacts, and most Shadars do not develop it. Their eyes always appear in unusual shades, from purple to dark red and absolute black, and more rarely in metallic silver or gold, the latter almost restricted to exceptional bloodlines such as House [[_wiki_en/personagens/nidzina_avaroth\|Avaroth]]. Their bond is with devils, and their lifespan ranges from ninety-six to one hundred years. Because they descend from humans, they retain rounded ears and human facial bone structure, without elven elongation: what distinguishes a Shadar is not the shape but the body’s palette. And, because of their vampiric nature, they visibly age more slowly than humans. The lag is proportional: a Shadar looks about eighty percent of their actual age, so that a fifty-year-old looks like a forty-year-old human, and a ninety-nine-year-old like a seventy-nine-year-old. Severe signs of age, white hair and a deeply lined face, appear only at the end of life, from ninety onward.

Their constitution is similar to that of humans in structure, but their metabolism differs: Shadars require less food, show an appetite for rare meats, strong wines, and bitter spices, and many reject foods considered pure by alabastrine tradition, such as fresh milk, honey, and green citrus fruits. Their sense of smell is sharper for metallic and sulfurous odors, while intense floral scents bother part of the population. Under prolonged direct sunlight, their skin tends to become irritated and dry, which explains their preference for hoods, heavy cloaks, and buildings with narrow windows, a chronic discomfort, not a vulnerability comparable to that of the [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Druls]].

### Psychology of Domination

Compulsive ambition is not a flaw among Shadars but a virtue cultivated from the cradle, measured by the speed of advancement and the intelligence with which anyone in the way is removed. A Shadar who shows no hunger for position is seen as sick or dangerous. Emotional suppression begins in childhood: children learn to channel love into possession, sadness into fuel for revenge, and joy into satisfaction in power. Public displays of uncontrolled emotion are humiliating, and crying is tolerated only in young children and, afterward, only in funeral rituals, with the face covered. Shadar loyalty is hierarchical, not personal: absolute allegiance goes to the structure of the Cult, not to the individuals who occupy its posts, and the criterion for judging a betrayal is competence, not morality.

### Five Core Creeds

The Creeds are not written in a single document or recited as a prayer; they are scattered through the [[_wiki/conceitos/lex_obscura_decreto\|Lex Obscura]], the decrees of the [[_wiki/conceitos/verbum_arkhon_codex\|Verbum Arkhon]], and nursery songs, and every Shadar internalizes them before the age of twelve. There are five: power as the only truth, hierarchy as a sacred foundation, stagnation as death, emotion as a tool, and the endurance of the Cult over the individual, for the shadow persists and people pass.

### Social Structure

Shadar society is a pyramid where each tier serves the one above and devours the one below. At the top stands [[_wiki_en/personagens/arothistar\|Arothistar]], a reclusive monarch who rules through intermediaries and [[_wiki/artefatos/oculus_morvgradi\|onyx]] spheres; below him, the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/conselho_sombrio\|Shadow Council]], whose four seats control finances, armies, intelligence, and doctrine; then the executive institutions, the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/justiciares\|Justiciars]] with power over life and death, the legion generals, the governing shadow lords, and the Archons of [[_wiki/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]]; below them, [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/templarios\|Templars]], [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/ophideias\|ophideias]], and various officers; and at the base, the mass of ordinary citizens, the orc troops, and, below everyone, the [[_wiki_en/racas/pindoriar\|Pindoriars]].

The family exists in subordination to the State. Children are collective property until the age of four, raised in Cult nurseries, and only after the First Shadow do they receive familial recognition, which is still contractual. Marriages are negotiated for political or genetic convenience and dissolved when they cease to be useful. Families of ancient blood, such as the Avaroths, maintain meticulous genealogies and vie for seats on the Council with the same fervor they apply to war. The title of shadow lord designates Shadars of sufficient hierarchical standing to govern territories; it is not hereditary, and it can be granted, revoked, or wrested away by force.

High-status Shadars also distinguish themselves through their armor. Shadow lords, high-ranking Justiciars, and members of Houses of ancient blood wear the tegmen: plates of absolute black, lightweight construction for agile combat and command duties, and a sophisticated finish of inlaid obsidian and personal runes. Two tegmens can differ greatly in silhouette; what establishes the category is this triad. Ordinary troops and mid-ranking officers do not wear it: the social divide is clear in the metal as well.

### Culture and Customs

Names follow a dual convention, a personal name assigned at the First Shadow, generally evoking power or darkness, and a family surname; the personal name can be changed by the Cult at moments of advancement, reflecting the belief that a name is an instrument, not a fixed identity. Rites of passage structure life into rigid stages: the First Shadow at four presents the child to the Cult, the Black Awakening at twelve subjects the adolescent to trials that determine aptitudes, the Binding at eighteen marks the first formal pact with a diabolical entity, every significant promotion requires a ritual Ascension, and the Last Shadow closes the cycle with the body surrendered to the Cult and dissolved in pits of black pitch.

The imperial economy is extractive: tribute flows from the provinces to Morvgrad and from Morvgrad to the Cult, and enslaved Pindoriars sustain agriculture, mining, and construction, while manual labor is considered degrading. The black market operates under license by decree of Arothistar, as an economic pressure valve and a source of intelligence for the Justiciars. Taboos include showing weakness before subordinates, refusing a formal challenge, and speaking a superior’s full name without invitation. Ritual suicide, though not encouraged, is accepted as a dignified way out for those who have lost position, health, or purpose, and its high rate among mid-ranking officers suggests that the existential [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/vazio\|void]] corrodes most strongly those trapped between ambition and the ceiling. In its most elaborate form, ritual suicide becomes spectacle: the Final Oblation is the sacrificial theater in which nobles stage their own deaths as sacred art before an audience, regarded by the Cult as the pinnacle of diabolical devotion in Morvgrad.

Shadar architecture is stone: basalt, granite, obsidian. Wood does not belong to the present Empire, and wherever it appears in a building, it is a mark of another time or another people. In [[_wiki/locais/mor_severith\|Mor’Severith]], Arothistar uses it deliberately in some prison chambers and in the library, in the image of the chambers of the old human empire. The restriction extends to construction; wooden furnishings and objects, such as the Shadow Council’s table or the ebony panels of Ezrath’s sidewalks, fall outside it.

Outside their ranks, uniformed Shadars bear the derogatory exonym “blackie”, common among the lower castes and other races; explanations of its origin differ, some pointing to the black color of regimental uniforms, others to the pallor of those who wear black. Uttering it within earshot of a Shadar is reckless, although outsiders unaware of the cultural subtleties use it without realizing the risk.

### Relationship with the Empire

The Shadars do not control the Empire by accident. Every aspect of domination is designed: [[_wiki/personagens/xarn\|Xarn]]’s Lex Obscura supplied the legal-religious apparatus, Arothistar’s Verbum Arkhon transformed the law into an instrument of personal sovereignty, and the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/alianca_subterranea\|Underground Alliance]] with the Druls secured strategic depth and arcane tradition. The Shadar view of other races is pragmatic: the Druls are necessary allies, respected for their competence and feared for their treachery; the [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orcs]] are war fodder; the [[_wiki_en/racas/sylvano\|Sylvans]] are enemies protected by [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/titas\|titans]]; the [[_wiki_en/racas/elarino\|Elarins]] are trophies more valuable alive; the [[_wiki_en/racas/karashim\|Karashim]] forge weapons under supervision and believe themselves independent within cages the Shadars designed. The provincial system divides Reinkard into territories under shadow lords, and communication with the capital depends on Oculi, couriers mounted on [[_wiki/criaturas/sombralva\|shadow panthers]], and, for messages that cannot be intercepted, female messenger imps.

### Magic

The Shadars’ primary access system is [[_wiki_en/magia/pactum_diaboli\|Pactum Diaboli]], a formal contract with diabolical entities that exists simultaneously on the material plane and in the Nine Hells, marked by the infernal rune burned into the skin during the Binding. The [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/diabos\|devils]] are legalistic: every clause is negotiated, every obligation is rigid, and the soul is pledged after death with no possibility of recovery. From this arrangement arises the most universal fear in the pact-bound Shadar psyche, that of being cursed by one’s own patron, for the curse is the contractual sanction par excellence, and caution regarding the clauses is not devotion, it is risk management.

Among the free peoples, the pledging of the soul has given rise to a doctrine of its own: for eras, it has been taught from pulpit to pulpit that the Shadars sold their souls in dark pacts and therefore have nothing left to lose. The belief generalizes to the entire race what applies to those bound by pacts, and ignores the exceptions, since a Justiciar who operates through Teru pledges no soul at all. True or not, it is the lens through which provincial peasants read any Shadar face. The doctrine’s written formulation is the [[_wiki/artefatos/de_anima_vendita\|De Anima Vendita]], a treatise by the Álvarar cleric Ambrosin of [[_wiki/locais/myrgarde\|Myrgarde]], written in the decades preceding the [[_wiki/conceitos/guerras_reinkardicas\|Reinkardic Wars]]: no peasant has read Ambrosin, and all repeat his conclusion.

The diabolical pact grants access to two schools from each devil to whom the Shadar binds themself, among the ten that make up the race’s signature; those most closely associated with that signature are [[_wiki_en/escolas/anatho\|Anatho]], of curses, [[_wiki_en/escolas/necro\|Necro]], of death and decomposition, [[_wiki_en/escolas/umbra\|Umbra]], of shadows and concealment, and [[_wiki_en/escolas/toxo\|Toxo]], of poison and corrosion. The ontological cost of these schools is cumulative: the more a Shadar invokes, the more the veins blacken and the more the body records the passage of power. Prolonged Shadar presence also corrodes green magic through contact, and wherever Shadars settle, the land blackens and titan wards weaken, a phenomenon whose continental expression is the [[_wiki/magia/macula-sombria\|Shadow-Taint]]. A notable exception is the Justiciars, who operate primarily through [[_wiki_en/magia/teru\|Teru]], a system without a pact that arises from an innate awakening and is refined through technical study: Teru allows access to the ten schools of the practitioner’s own race and requires no pledge of the soul, but it flourishes only in those who awaken and demands decades of study, and it is this independence that allows them to judge even pact-bound Templars without a conflict of interest.

### Conversion Through the Rite of Ossárium

Not every Shadar is born a Shadar. The [[_wiki/magia/rito_de_ossarium\|Rite of Ossárium]] is the conversion process through which ordinary humans acquire the race’s physiological traits and arcane connection, rare and reserved for exceptional circumstances. It required ritualized torture, blood sacrifices, prolonged fasting, and exposure for days to concentrated diabolical energies that rewrote tissue, bone, blood, and planar bonds. The procedure was irreversible, and mortality among candidates exceeded half; those who survived emerged with grayish skin, darkened veins, altered appetites, and a functional arcane connection, but carried internal scars that Shadars by birth did not possess. The existence of the Rite implies that Shadar identity is as much biological as ritual, a condition that can be imposed or chosen, not merely inherited. Forty-four years ago the Rite was prohibited and, at the fall of Myrgarde, abolished for good and declared a capital heresy; the prohibition served the Council’s purism, which had always favored pure bloodlines and treated converts as tolerated infiltrators, never equals.

### Voice and Conflicts

Shadars speak with surgical coldness, choosing each word for weight rather than comfort, with short sentences when giving orders and long ones when humiliating, and use silence as a weapon. They never apologize, for acknowledging an error means losing ground; they correct their course without announcing the correction and expect others to notice. Their vocabulary tends toward the formal, avoiding street slang and regionalisms that would betray provincial origins. Swearing by the Void is a habit of the servant races; for a Shadar, who sees the Void as sacred, it is a minor blasphemy that they suppress in public and that escapes them only under pressure. In thought, or when control fails, the swearword that rises is the common one.

The race’s greatest conflict is internal, and arises from a people built to conquer who discovered they had already conquered almost everything. Thirty years after the fall of Myrgarde, the generation born under the Empire has never needed to fight for survival, and the ambition that was a wartime virtue turns inward: the Council’s factions vie for influence with more venom than they used against Myrgarde, generals such as [[_wiki/personagens/vekraal\|Vekraal]] build personal legions, and the Justiciars operate increasingly as an independent power. Hierarchical dependence is a structural vulnerability, for Shadars removed from the chain of command fall into decision paralysis, and the rate of ritual suicide among provincial officers is four times that of the capital. At its core, the crisis is theological: the Cult was founded to conquer and has conquered, and the Lex Obscura codifies eternal duties without answering the question that no Shadar would formulate in public and that all carry in the void between one pact and the next.
