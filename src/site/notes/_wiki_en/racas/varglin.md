# Varglin

The Varglin is the gnomish subrace of the [[_wiki_en/racas/vulfano\|Vulfans]]: a compact, stealthy canine with keen intelligence and a body made for dens and crevices. Where [[_wiki_en/racas/worg\|worgs]] occupy the threshold between man and beast, Varglins occupy the threshold between existing and disappearing. Rare and despised in the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/terceira_era\|Third Era]], they are almost always mistaken for [[_wiki_en/racas/murano\|Murans]], the sewer vermin, a confusion they have stopped correcting.

### Physiology

They measure eighty centimeters to one meter ten centimeters when upright and, on all fours, reach a human’s waist. They have the build of a fox or jackal, a tapered muzzle, and large ears: hearing is their dominant sense, unlike worgs, which are guided by smell. Their fur is short and dense, in shades of sand, mouse gray, and rusty brown, and wild Varglins develop camouflage markings according to the terrain where they grow up. Their hands have five functional fingers and thin, curved claws, with greater dexterity than those of worgs: they manipulate locks, sew, and set mechanisms. Their eyes are disproportionately large, adapted to the dark, and bright light causes them discomfort. Their metabolism is fast, their hunger almost constant, and their bodies tremble: pups from immaturity, adults because they burn energy too quickly, the old because it is all they have left.

### Abilities

Varglin hearing picks up frequencies that other races do not even register, to the point of hearing heartbeats at five meters and detecting ambushes, lies through an accelerated pulse, and presences concealed by magic; in return, very noisy environments disorient them. Their compact bodies, padded paws, and sound-dampening fur make them almost undetectable when moving slowly, and only worgs, through scent, track them easily. They dig escape tunnels quickly and, in urban terrain, infiltrate through cracks, ducts, and wall cavities. Their spatial memory is total: a path traveled once is memorized forever.

### Psychology

The trait that defines Varglin psychology in the Third Era is the ancestral fear of worgs. Generations of predation have engraved the flight response so deeply that the smell of a worg causes involuntary paralysis followed by an urge to run; in combat, that instant of paralysis costs them their lives. Added to this are hypervigilance, with sleep in intervals of twenty to thirty minutes and awakening at the slightest stimulus, distrust that accumulates from generation to generation, and a fierce attachment to the few they trust. In place of the worgs’ sarcasm, they have a resigned sense of the absurd: they laugh at impossible situations because the alternative is to tremble until they stop.

### Social History

In the primitive packs, the Vargskol, Varglins served as scouts, sentinels, and trappers in a symbiotic relationship with worgs: they mapped the terrain and rigged dens, and received protection and fighting strength. They also had a ritual function, digging the burial dens where the body of a dead worg was placed, work considered sacred. The dissolution of the packs eliminated this protective framework, and hungry worgs in urban environments began preying on them, tracking them by their fear. The Varglins who survived were those who completely abandoned life among worgs and disappeared. Today they live in tiny groups of two to five individuals, or entirely alone, occupying spaces that other races ignore: wall cavities, abandoned ducts, hollow roots, forgotten basements. The population shrinks with each generation, and their actual numbers are unknown, since no census distinguishes them from Murans.

### Surviving Culture

Little of Varglin culture has survived. Den markings, the Gratmark, persist, scratches in stone or wood that signal “safe”, “danger”, “worg was here”, or “food”, changed periodically so that predators cannot read them. Whispered names persist: each Varglin has an external name, generic and disposable, used with strangers, and a true name given by the mother at birth, shared only with those they trust with their life; losing the whispered name is considered symbolic death. The compulsive collection of small objects persists, half a resource for traps and trade, half an assertion of existence in a world that erases them. The funeral rites were lost with the packs: modern Varglins leave their dead where they fall, sometimes covered with leaves, rags, or stones, an instinctive gesture, a remnant of ritual.

### Voice

Varglins speak quickly, quietly, and in incomplete sentences, cutting words in half when they feel watched, as though every syllable were a risk of detection. Subjects are usually omitted, “saw scent, two days, big” in place of the whole sentence, and there is a nervous habit of repeating the other speaker’s last word while processing what they have heard. They avoid saying “worg” aloud, replacing it with “big”, “cousin”, or “that one”, or simply falling silent, and the chronic trembling of their bodies shows through in syllables that falter under stress. On the rare occasions when they trust, their speech expands and reveals a keen intelligence and precise observation. Where worgs are transactional and ironic, Varglins are telegraphic and literal: they operate through immediate exchange, without the credit of *skuld*, because credit presupposes a tomorrow that neither side guarantees.

### Relationship with the Empire

The [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|Empire]] does not distinguish Varglins as a category. Legally classified as Vulfans and therefore subject to the magical collar of [[_wiki_en/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]], in practice they are treated as Murans: vermin unworthy of administrative attention. No imperial census records them separately, and guards who encounter a Varglin in the sewers kick it along with the rats. This bureaucratic invisibility is their greatest protection: vermin receive neither a collar nor a persecution decree. Some find employment in specific niches, such as repairing delicate mechanisms, disarming traps, and spying in inaccessible spaces, operating as anonymous freelancers hired by intermediaries, often [[_wiki_en/racas/derro\|Derros]], who protect the source’s identity.

### Magic

Like all Vulfans, Varglins access magic through [[_wiki_en/magia/pactum_diaboli\|Pactum Diaboli]]. The practice is even rarer than among worgs: their lifespan of forty to fifty years and a life of constant flight prevent sustained study. The few who show aptitude gravitate toward concealment magic, becoming invisible, suppressing sound, erasing tracks, and toward trap magic. The cost manifests as accelerated loss of body mass: a Varglin who uses magic frequently visibly wastes away, and the line between practitioner and dying individual is thin.

### Life Cycle

They are born in litters of one to three pups, smaller than those of worgs; females go into heat once a year. They mature quickly: they walk upright at eighteen months, speak at three years, and become adults at eight. They live between forty and fifty years in theory, but in practice few live past thirty-five. Pups are born with their eyes open and run on all fours within a few hours, and weaning is abrupt, at four weeks: precocity selected for by predation, in which slow pups and immobile mothers did not survive.

### Internal Conflicts

The race’s central conflict is existential in the most literal sense: continuing to exist requires constant effort, and the reward is continuing to exist. Many old Varglins, which their reduced lifespan makes them around thirty, simply stop. They stop eating and hiding, sit in an open place and wait, and the trembling is the last to cease. The young call this going away; the old, stopping pretending.

Some young Varglins, especially those who grew up in cities and have never seen another of their own race, seek contact with the other gnomish races. The reception varies. Derros tolerate them for their usefulness. [[_wiki_en/racas/kobold\|Kobolds]], which serve Shadar masters, ignore them as irrelevant. [[_wiki_en/racas/spriggin\|Spriggins]], in the rare regions where they coexist, show something close to solidarity: two species dying out recognize the similarity without needing to say it.

Beneath both lies the paradox of fear. The ancestral fear of worgs keeps Varglins alive, because it drives them to flee, sharpens their senses, and forces invisibility, and at the same time slowly kills them, because it prevents reproduction, perpetuates isolation, and corrodes mental health. Ridding themselves of it would mean losing the survival tool that has brought them this far; keeping it means that each generation is born more fragile than the last.
