# Aquilar

Aquilars are the largest humanoid subgroup among the [[_wiki_en/racas/aviano\|Avians]], eagle and falcon hybrids and the only ones that flew. Where the [[_wiki_en/racas/fringal\|Fringals]] were born with vestigial wings and feet on the ground, Aquilars were born with enough wingspan to take flight at maturity. They are the most visible mark of angelic interference: humans who received the sky without asking, and who now walk the ground with their wings clipped, reminding everyone of what they once were.

### Physiology

Standing upright, they measure from one point eight five meters to two point two zero meters, a build that rivals that of [[_wiki_en/racas/draconideo\|Draconids]] in breadth, but not in mass. The Aquilar build is aerodynamic, with hollow bones reinforced by dense cartilage and oversized pectoral musculature to sustain flight. They are lighter than their size suggests, and their hollow structure is an advantage in the air and a vulnerability on the ground: heavy impacts that an [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orc]] would absorb break Aquilar bones.

Feathers cover their arms, backs, and napes, with phenotypic variation according to the predominant avian lineage. Eagle lineages tend toward golden-brown and bronze tones, with larger, stiffer feathers; falcon lineages display streaked patterns in gray and rust, with shorter feathers. The feathers are not decoration: they provide thermal insulation, protect the thin skin, and, when intact, allow flight maneuvers that no magical artifact replicates. The wings are extensions of the upper limbs, articulated by additional joints that humans do not possess, and become fully developed around fourteen years of age; before then, the young have partial wings, capable of gliding and cushioning falls. When folded, they tuck against the back with considerable bulk, making conventional armor impractical. Their vision is that of a bird of prey, with greater acuity than that of any humanoid race and motion perception at distances that [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]] would need a spyglass to match; it is the last sense to deteriorate, even in debilitated Aquilars. Their lifespan ranges from fifty to sixty-five years.

### Temperament

Their defining trait is silent dignity. Aquilars do not proclaim pride like Draconids, do not negotiate respect like [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Druls]], do not impose their presence like Shadars: they refuse to bow, and the refusal is neither gesture nor speech, it is bearing. An Aquilar with clipped wings and chains around the wrists keeps a straight spine and a level gaze. The trait is a legacy of the angelic mark, a duty of purity that the race did not choose and that most cannot articulate, but whose weight all feel.

Added to this trait is the melancholy of altitude. Aquilars who have flown carry the memory of flight like an open wound, and do not speak about the sky because they do not need to: their tone of voice, the way their eyes lift when they hear the wind, the pause before passing through a low doorway say enough. The young born after the conquest, who have never flown, develop a variant, nostalgia for something they have never experienced, transmitted through their elders’ bearing and untold stories. They speak with restrained melancholy and a vocabulary of observation, drawing on spatial terms such as horizon, current, and distance to describe emotions and judgments, and pause at length before answering, not out of uncertainty, but because they assess before speaking. They also bear a silent guilt toward the Fringals, who depended on the Aquilar aerial shield and were left at the mercy of predators when the race’s flight was cut off.

### Pre-Conquest Culture

In mixed cities such as [[_wiki_en/locais/myrgarde\|Myrgarde]], Aquilars did not occupy a single niche. The race served multiple functions, integrated into the urban fabric along three main lines. As guardian-sentinels, they were natural watchers of walls, towers, and aerial routes, and flight made them an irreplaceable defensive force: the Temple Guard of Myrgarde recruited Aquilars for elevated positions, and winged armor was an insignia of office. [[_wiki_en/personagens/auren\|Auren]], an Aquilar from Myrgarde, served in this role, with custom armor bearing sacred engravings and elven workmanship that accommodated his wingspan.

As hierophants of [[_wiki_en/magia/devotio_sancta\|Devotio Sancta]], they were curéis, purifiers, and ministers of rites of light; their racial affinity with white entities made Aquilars the most natural devotees of the angelic faith, and temples of [[_wiki_en/panteao/israfil\|Israfil]] and [[_wiki_en/panteao/azazel\|Azazel]] maintained Aquilar hierophants in prominent positions, healing the wounded of all races without distinction. Finally, many were simply integrated citizens, traders, artisans, teachers, and builders, and this normality is the hardest fact to recover after the conquest, because it is the first to disappear.

### Under the Empire

The Aquilars’ relationship with [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|the Empire]] is one of subjugation. They are second-class citizens when tolerated, prisoners when useful, and examples when convenient. Their angelic devotion marks them as antagonistic to the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/culto_sombrio\|Shadow Cult]], and the hostility has an instinctive component: the aversion Shadars feel toward races aligned with angels tempers every interaction with a layer of distrust that logic does not dissolve. The race’s residual utility, the vision of a bird of prey, hierophantic knowledge, healing ability, prevents systematic extermination, and the Empire maintains Aquilars as resources, some in watchtowers with their wings sealed, others treating the wounded in military infirmaries under supervision.

Flight control employs graduated methods, and the variety is not redundancy, but grammar: each way of cutting off flight communicates a different message. Physical mutilation severs tendons or breaks wing bones, permanent and visible, inflicted on prisoners of war and public examples. Arcane shackles are removable magical restraints applied to the wings, for temporary custody. The seal of [[_wiki_en/escolas/nomos\|Nomos]] paralyzes the wing musculature without visible damage, bureaucratic cruelty that can be applied en masse: the Aquilar feels the wings, moves the feathers, but the neural command that initiates flight simply never arrives. And the clipping of ritual feathers, followed by an arcane mark that slows regeneration, must be repeated periodically, turning flight control into recurring humiliation, with flight always there, just beneath the skin.

### Relationship with Magic

Access to power comes exclusively through Devotio Sancta, devotion to angelic entities. Aquilars are the race most aligned with this system: the angelic mark they have carried since their origin facilitates their connection with angels, and faith, when genuine, flows with a naturalness that other races take years to approach. The angelic schools are their natural repertoire, [[_wiki_en/escolas/sacro\|Sacro]], [[_wiki_en/escolas/helio\|Helio]], Nomos, [[_wiki_en/escolas/aegis\|Aegis]], [[_wiki_en/escolas/salvo\|Salvo]], and [[_wiki_en/escolas/aero\|Aero]], the last being the one with the most visceral affinity: Aquilars in flight combined their physiology with control of air currents in a way that no other race replicated.

In the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/terceira_era\|Third Era]], access is eroding. Devotio Sancta requires genuine faith, and genuine faith is difficult to maintain when the god who marked the race without asking does not prevent its mutilation. The Avian crisis of faith is endemic: Aquilars who lose flight gradually lose the ability to invoke, and the angelic mark remains on the body while the magical channel weakens as doubt sets in. Those who maintain faith under oppression are rare and, for that reason, proportionately more powerful, for faith that survives suffering is the purest, and Devotio Sancta rewards purity, not comfort.

### Life Cycle

Aquilars are oviparous. Females lay one to two eggs per clutch, with incubation lasting about sixty days in moderate warmth; unlike [[_wiki_en/racas/sauriano\|Saurians]], who require furnaces, Avians incubate with body heat and sun exposure. Hatchlings are born with fine down that becomes functional feathers over the first year, develop partial wings around eight years of age and full flight wings at fourteen, reaching adulthood at sixteen. Under imperial rule, the average lifespan falls to about forty years, shortened by mutilation, captivity, and loss of access to healing.
