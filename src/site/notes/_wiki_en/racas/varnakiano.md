# Varnakian

Varnakians are humans marked by infernal blood, inhabitants of the hostile mountain ranges of [[_wiki/locais/varnak\|Varnak]], beyond the [[_wiki/locais/dorsal\|Dorsal]]. They share an origin with the [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]], the same species born of the same stock, separated by the gods each people chose: where the Shadars made diabolical pacts, the Varnakians bound themselves to the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/demonios\|red gods]]. Their society is tribal, fragmented, and violent, and their magic is visceral, fueled by fire, blood, and terror.

### Origin

In the [[_wiki/conceitos/primeira_era\|First Era]], pagan communities of the northern plateau developed draconic cults and bound themselves to demons, the red gods. The first demonic traits appeared in that generation: vestigial horns, coppery skin, pupils that narrow under pressure. The Shadars, already organized around diabolical pacts, saw the crimson cultists as an intolerable heresy and expelled the tribes with demonic bonds to the west of the Dorsal, subjugating the others. On the other side of the mountains, the exiles found Varnak: valleys of volcanic ash, elder dragons on the peaks, no infrastructure and no mercy. The survivors forged deeper and more desperate bonds, and demonic blood seeped into the bloodlines at a speed that frightened the shamans themselves. In three generations, what had been a cosmetic trait became a racial mark.

### Physiology

Infernal blood manifests in traits that vary according to the potency of the bloodline. In diluted bloodlines, the alteration is subtle: reddish or coppery skin, body temperature two degrees above the human norm, pupils that narrow into vertical slits under stress. In concentrated ones, it is conspicuous: curved horns of dark bone on the forehead or temples, scarlet skin with a leathery texture, vestigial claws on the fingers, and, in rare cases, a rudimentary tail of hard cartilage. The eyes tend toward warm shades (amber, copper, ember-red, sulfurous orange), and hair ranges from reddish black to volcanic gray. Their elevated temperature makes them resistant to the mountain cold but uncomfortable in humid climates, and their sweat carries a faintly sulfurous odor that other races notice before identifying the source.

They live from ninety-six to one hundred years. Aging dries and hardens the skin until, in elders, the face resembles parchment stretched over bone; the horns darken, thin, and crack. Some shamans read the fissures in the horns as maps of experience, each crack corresponding to a renegotiated bond or a rupture survived.

### Social Structure

Varnakian society is tribal, scattered, and without a unified government. Each tribe is an autonomous unit under a shaman or sorcerer who combines spiritual authority (mediation between mortals and demons) with direct political power. Succession occurs through challenge or the leader’s death, rarely through lineage. Alliances between tribes are temporary, formed for hunting, defense, or trade, and dissolved when their purpose is exhausted. Family is a matter of convenience: children belong to the tribe, not their parents, and the intensity of demonic blood determines social standing before any merit. Children with prominent traits are set aside early for shamanic training; those without visible traits perform manual labor.

The central axis of this dynamic is coexistence with the [[_wiki_en/racas/draconideo\|Draconids]] of Varnak, who share the same valleys in an economy of mutual distrust, exchanging services, reagents, slaves, and information. Varnakian shamans serve as intermediaries between Draconid clans and demonic forces, translating rituals and negotiating pacts. [[_wiki/personagens/wijmarv\|Wijmarv]] exemplifies the archetype: a scholar of dragons, [[_wiki_en/racas/luminares\|Luminars]], and cosmogony, he operated in the service of [[_wiki/organizacoes/cla_myr_vethan\|Clan Myr’vethan]] before betraying his allies and fleeing back to Varnak.

### Culture and Customs

Varnakian names tend toward guttural syllables and fricative consonants, an influence of the demonic language on the [[_wiki/linguas/dialetos_tribais\|tribal dialects]]. There are no family surnames: identification is personal, followed by the name of the tribe or the shaman served. Tradition is both oral and written, which distinguishes Varnakians from other tribal societies of [[_wiki/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]]. Their shamans produce treatises and chronicles that other races consider dangerous, and Wijmarv’s treatise on the [[_wiki/artefatos/fendris\|Fendris]] circulates among the libraries of [[_wiki/locais/mor_severith\|Mor’Severith]], consulted by scholars who would never acknowledge the source. This erudition coexists with everyday brutality without contradiction: the same shaman who writes a study of Luminar cosmogony may, that same night, tear out a rival’s throat in a ritual challenge.

Varnakians have not mastered metallurgy, and quality weaponry comes from plunder, not the forge; in the eras before the Shadar conquest, tribes raided dwarven fortresses to steal weapons, tools, and reagents. Their dead are cremated on pyres fueled by sulfur, and the ashes, mixed with mountain resin, are molded into small effigies that relatives carry close to their bodies; when an effigy falls apart through wear, they consider the spirit to have finally departed. There is no formal mourning: death is transition, not loss, and to weep for a dead person is to insult their strength. Veneration of [[_wiki/personagens/varna\|Varna]], the elder dragon who inhabits Varnak, permeates all tribes without any one controlling it. Some worship her as a protective goddess, others invoke her as a weapon against rivals, and all fear her.

The greatest dishonor has its own term: mraz’kor, or Heart of Ash, from the archaic Varnakian mraz (ash or residue) and kor (core or pulsation). It designates someone who has lost the ability to produce fire, whether through trauma, old age, or a curse; in the warrior culture of the mountain ranges, a mraz’kor is regarded as a dead person walking, for their existence no longer possesses the heat of honor.

### Relationship with Magic

The Varnakians’ access system is [[_wiki_en/magia/vinculum_daemonis\|Vinculum Daemonis]], the bond of force with demonic entities, radically distinct from the Shadar [[_wiki_en/magia/pactum_diaboli\|Pactum Diaboli]]. Where devils are legalistic and negotiate contracts, demons are chaotic and bond through intensity. Access is easy (anyone with sufficient violence attracts a demon’s attention) but unstable, since demons betray out of whim, amusement, or boredom. The principal cost is constant destructive acts: the bond demands a continuous supply of violence, fire, blood, or terror, and leaves grotesque, progressive, and irreversible mutation as its mark, horns that grow and scars that multiply with each invocation.

Vinculum Daemonis grants access to the schools of [[_wiki_en/escolas/pyro\|Pyro]], [[_wiki_en/escolas/rupto\|Rupto]], [[_wiki_en/escolas/phobos\|Phobos]], [[_wiki_en/escolas/belli\|Belli]], and [[_wiki_en/escolas/psycho\|Psycho]]. Varnakian magic is visceral, tied to the caster’s body: blood as fuel, pain as catalyst, screams as vocal component. Veteran shamans carry on their bodies the map of every spell cast, in self-inflicted burns, ritual scars, and deformities. The soul is not pledged after death as in diabolical pacts: it is consumed during life, in installments, and each invocation tears away a fragment. Elders who have survived decades of practice describe a progressive erosion of identity, memories that fade and emotions that dull, until only the craft and the flame remain.

### Narrative Voice

Varnakians speak with heat, not of affection but of combustion: short sentences that crackle, pauses that smolder, threats that need no raised voice to burn. Where Shadars calibrate every syllable, Varnakians speak like someone holding embers in their hands, without flourishes and without backing down. Their shamans tend toward the didactic and explain the world with the authority of those who have seen things others would prefer not to know; their erudition is stripped of academic vanity. Their vocabulary carries mineral and igneous weight (ash, sulfur, lava, crack, ember), and everyday speech mixes [[_wiki/linguas/reinkardico\|Reinkardic]] with demonic terms. There is also an archaic register, predating the pacts, preserved in ritual chants and invocation formulas that shamans transmit from master to apprentice.

### Relationship with Reinkard

The Varnakians’ relationship with Reinkard and [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|the Empire]] is distant and calculated. Varnak lies beyond the Dorsal, outside the direct reach of the imperial purple seal, and few Varnakians cross to the continent; those who do serve specific purposes: trade, espionage, escape, or service to Draconid clans with operations in Reinkard. That Varnakians and Draconids now move through imperial territory with relative freedom, even in small numbers, is a change of recent decades, the result of the Empire’s political convenience, not trust. Shadars see Varnakians as circumstantial allies, exotic and untrustworthy, useful when they need something demons offer and devils do not, disposable when the service ends. Varnakians return the courtesy with identical precision.

### Conflicts

The Varnakians’ central conflict is fragmentation as a permanent condition. There has never been a nation, unified government, or law that extended beyond the next mountain; each shaman is king of his own valley. Dispersion was an advantage in the first generations, when it was impossible to exterminate those who did not gather, but in the [[_wiki/conceitos/terceira_era\|Third Era]] it became a prison: apart, they are minor pieces in the continental game; together, they would be a formidable force, and no one can bring them together. The land itself aggravates the problem, since Varnakians are exiles from the plateau rather than native mountain dwellers, and treat Varnak’s unstable geology as an enemy to be corrected, where Draconids read it as an extension of the body.

The relationship with Draconids oscillates between symbiosis and parasitism, with mutual dependence and reciprocal distrust; serving lizards, the shamans murmur in private, is a necessity, not a vocation. Varna prevents any forced unification and freezes the political balance in a stasis of perpetual tension, resulting in continuous low-intensity warfare. The instability of Vinculum Daemonis corrodes from within: shamans who serve for decades accumulate mutations that isolate them from their own clans, and those who lose their recognizable human form also lose the ability to lead, ending up as oracles in the deepest caves, consulted out of necessity and avoided by instinct. Finally, the [[_wiki/eventos/sopro_do_eclipse\|Breath of the Eclipse]], the continental plague of the [[_wiki/conceitos/segunda_era\|Second Era]], left a collective scar: Varnakians took in human refugees from the Shadar plateau and the contamination decimated entire tribes, and the memory of the plague persists as a muted resentment toward Reinkard, tempered by the awareness that crossing the Dorsal to take revenge would be suicide.
