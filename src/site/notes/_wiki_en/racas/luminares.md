# Luminar

The Luminars, or elves of Light, are the ancestral elven race from which all later lineages descend. They dominated [[_wiki/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]] in [[_wiki/conceitos/idade_esquecida\|the Forgotten Age]], before the reckoning of eras, and disappeared as a unified people, fragmenting into [[_wiki_en/racas/elarino\|Elarins]], [[_wiki_en/racas/sylvano\|Sylvans]], [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Druls]], [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orcs]], and [[_wiki/racas/undine\|undines]]. No named individual survives in the records, and their language, [[_wiki/linguas/luminar\|Luminar]], has reached the present day only in fragments.

### Physiology

Direct physiological data did not survive the extinction. What can be inferred comes from their closest descendants: the Elarins retain delicate features, fair skin with a subtle luminescence, silver, amber, or sky-blue eyes, elongated ears, and slender proportions. The Luminars likely shared these characteristics to a more pronounced degree, with an innate arcane affinity greater than that of any known mortal race. Their longevity was indefinite: the texts describe a civilization that endured for countless eras, without distinguishing between individual life and generational continuity. Their longest-lived descendants live about one hundred twenty years, which may indicate pronounced degeneration or merely the natural limit of weakened lineages.

### Civilization and Dominion

Luminar supremacy was arcane and cultural, not territorial in the imperial manner. Their enchanted citadels rose as monuments to eternity, adapted to their preferred terrain, ponds, hills, plains, or groves, and the surrounding land absorbed centuries of [[_wiki/cosmologia/magi\|magi]] presence. They coexisted with the cycles of the surface and with the races that respected their borders, without becoming conquerors. They tolerated gnomes without considering them important, ignored dwarves with mutual contempt, and saw humans, then scattered tribes of the central plains, as a crude and dangerously fickle species. Xenophobia was mutual, though asymmetrical: humans admired elven magic as intensely as they feared it.

### Spiritual Alignment

The Luminars predated the [[_wiki/cosmologia/cor_arcana\|chromatic system]] that organizes races and gods today, and therefore had no defined arcane color in the modern sense. In the Forgotten Age, white, green, and blue entities exerted far greater influence than in the present day, and the elves of light moved among them without the rigidity of later lineages. The fundamental divide set [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/anjos\|angels]] against [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/titas\|titans]]: those inclined toward the titans valued harmony and adaptation, those inclined toward the angels, purity and judgment. This spiritual tension preceded racial fragmentation and would ultimately determine it.

### Schisms and Fragmentation

Over countless eras, internal factions diverged in belief, ambition, and alignment, and each schism produced a lineage that would never return. The first to separate descended to [[_wiki/locais/vor-ghol\|Vor-Ghol]], drawn by the depths, where they corrupted fragments of the language and culture until they forged something entirely new and established dominion over older races: they became the proto-Druls, and it was with them, not with humans, that the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/diabos\|devils]] opened the first channels of communication. As a result of conflicts in the depths, the Luminars bonded to [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/demonios\|demons]] left Vor-Ghol for the tundras and mountains of the surface, whose harshness shaped their bodies and culture over millennia until it produced the orcs. Another faction migrated east, inhabited coasts and wetlands, and sought transcendence under the guidance of the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/siderais\|sidereal]] gods, becoming increasingly ethereal until it gave rise to the undines.

The final fall came through war against humans. Fueled by the Sancta faith and led by the [[_wiki_en/racas/alvarar\|Álvarars]], human armies imposed a continental siege. The Luminars responded with devastating magic, but each lost citadel was irrecoverable, while humans absorbed defeats and kept reproducing soldiers. The war ended through attrition. Some of the survivors took refuge in the deep forests under the protection of the titans and adapted to the green, giving rise to the Sylvans; others withdrew to citadels hidden by ancient enchantments, preserving what they could of the tradition of light, and gave rise to the Elarins. The most famous of these refuges would become [[_wiki/locais/elenir\|Elenir]].

### Language and Magic

Luminar was both a civil language and a magical medium. The elves of light did not separate everyday speech from arcane invocation: all speech carried the potential for power, and the difference between conversation and spell lay in intent, not grammar. Thus, fragments preserved in ancient texts occasionally produce residual effects when read aloud by unwary scholars, since the words retain their charge even out of context. Complete Luminar exists only in manuscripts locked away in the arcane tower of [[_wiki/locais/khargoth\|Khargoth]], in [[_wiki/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]], where scholars cannot reconstruct the full grammar. The Elarins’ [[_wiki/linguas/aelarian\|Aelarian]] is the closest living language to the original; the Druls’ [[_wiki/linguas/gholvyss\|Gholvyss]] represents its subterranean corruption. The abundance of magi that sustained Luminar civilization began to decline after the extinction and diminishes with each era, to the point that ancient dragons now perceive thinner magi and a more distant divine response.

### Legacy

The Luminars left three enduring legacies in Reinkard. To fight dragons and [[_wiki_en/racas/draconideo\|Draconids]], they forged artifacts of war imbued with ancient magi, and deliberately built the most powerful to reject hands corrupted by diabolical pacts or demonic bonds: [[_wiki/artefatos/fendris\|Fendris]], a dagger capable of wounding any draconic creature, responds only to hands unbound by pacts. They also sealed the mountain ranges around the plateau and the central plains, imbuing the rock with magi so intense that dragons could not cross them, and the barriers remained for eras after the fragmentation. Finally, some of their ruins still retain a magi presence strong enough for the [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]] to erect artificial nexuses on their foundations, on ground that no era has managed to dissipate. The Luminars opened rifts between the planes against the will of the gods, of all spectra, and little remains of what they forged: the hands that shaped ceased to exist before the objects that emerged from them. An Elarin tradition attributes to them the maxim that, in the light, all paths are visible, and darkness is the refusal to choose, though its authenticity is impossible to confirm.
