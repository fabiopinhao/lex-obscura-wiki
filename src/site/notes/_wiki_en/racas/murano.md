# Muran

Murans are agile, stealthy, ratlike humanoids who inhabit the spaces no other race wants: sewers, gaps between walls, collapsed tunnels, and the intermediate layers of [[_wiki/locais/vor-ghol\|Vor-Ghol]], below the [[_wiki_en/racas/karashim\|Karashim]] forges and above the [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Drul]] citadels. They stand between one point one and one point three meters tall, within the gnomish baseline. Most races mistake them for animals, and the confusion is no accident: looking like vermin is safer than looking like people. The [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|Shadow Empire]] despises them and ignores them in censuses, recognizing their value only as disposable informants.

### Cosmogonic Origin

Like all hybrid humanoids, Murans trace their origins to the [[_wiki/eventos/guerras_raciais\|Racial Wars]], when diabolical entities crossbred humans and animals to produce soldiers aligned with the [[_wiki/cosmologia/cor_arcana\|arcane colors]]. Their genesis is linked to the underground experiments of [[_wiki/panteao/warguleth\|Wargûleth]], the Abyssal Hound, in the primordial caverns of what would become Vor-Ghol. The Druls preserve a particularly humiliating version: when fusing human and canine blood to produce the [[_wiki/racas/vulfano\|Vulfans]], Wargûleth is said to have let the material become contaminated by cave rodents, and the result was smaller, hungrier creatures, waste that the god never corrected or cared to eliminate. The Drul version is myth, biased and possibly false, but it is also the only one that exists, since no Muran has ever recorded their own origin. The narrative serves a political function: positioning Murans as a divine byproduct justifies treating them as vermin.

### Physiology

They have a naturally hunched posture, leaning forward, with their arms free, a pointed snout, and a constantly moving nose. Their upper incisors grow throughout their lives and must be worn down by gnawing. Their whiskers are the dominant sensory organ, long and fanned out around the snout, detecting vibrations in the air and on surfaces to the point that a blindfolded Muran can navigate with acceptable precision; cutting them off amounts to functional blindness. Their eyes are small and dark, with excellent night vision and acute discomfort in direct sunlight. Their short, dense, oily fur, in shades of sewer gray and rust brown, repels water and parasites but gives off a musk that other races find repulsive. Their hands have five long fingers and curved claws for climbing and working in confined spaces, and their prehensile tail, sixty to eighty percent of their body length, supports their weight for short periods. Their metabolism is fast: a Muran eats the equivalent of half their own weight every two days, and deprivation leads to muscle hunger, then hallucinations, and, within a few days, death. The urgency of feeding shapes every decision, alliance, and betrayal.

### Sensory Network

Nearby Murans, up to about twenty meters apart, pick up signals from one another through two channels: ultrasonic emissions inaudible to most races and volatile pheromones released by their oily fur. Together, these form a primitive communication network that conveys basic states such as fear, hunger, and direction of escape. It is neither telepathy nor shared consciousness: each individual perceives a directional pressure, something between instinct and intuition, and when a colony flees, the wave of panic spreads faster than any scream. The network works best in enclosed spaces, where pheromones do not dissipate and ultrasound reverberates, and Murans isolated for too long report a deafness in the body, the absence of the network felt as an amputation. It is a natural trait of the race, not a learned ability.

### Psychology

Murans process the world in terms of food: allies are those who bring food, enemies those who take it away, places are where there is food or where there is none. Paranoia is functional, because every Muran assumes they will be betrayed and is right often enough to make it worthwhile: they check exits, sleep in shifts, and never reveal their main hiding place. Their intelligence is often underestimated, since their rapid speech and bestial appearance make other races treat them as clever animals, and anyone who expects little from a rat does not watch the rat. They compensate for their insignificance with grandiloquence, giving themselves titles disproportionate to reality, the only way to build an identity in a race where no one remembers the dead. Loyalty is mercenary, recalibrated with every new piece of information.

### Social Structure and Culture

The basic unit is the colony, with ten to two hundred Murans in a territory defined by access to food and escape routes, without a formal leader: whoever holds the most information about food sources exercises fluid influence, and the sensory network produces collective movements without centralized decisions. There is no vote or order, there is a wave. Surface colonies are smaller and more accustomed to contact with other races; those of Vor-Ghol are larger, denser, and wilder. Their culture is oral, volatile, and oriented toward survival, with nothing recorded in writing: what matters fits in the memory of someone alive and dies with them. Each Muran carries a surface name, generic and disposable, and a litter name, given by their mother at the first nursing and shared only with their siblings; losing all their siblings means losing those who still knew the name. When a Muran finds enough food to share, they offer the first bite to the nearest Muran, and accepting it means acknowledging membership in the colony, the closest thing to a ritual the race possesses.

### Voice

Murans speak fast, loudly, and too much: where [[_wiki/racas/varglin\|Varglins]] spare every syllable, they flood the listener with words, convinced that whoever speaks the most controls the conversation and whoever controls the conversation eats. Their sentences are long and cumulative, changing subjects midway, peppered with references to food even out of context and with reflexive self-promotion, all the grander the greater their insecurity. When frightened, their speech accelerates until it becomes a hiss of compressed syllables; and they avoid the word “death,” replacing it with “end,” “stop,” or “when it ends,” for fear that naming it will draw it to them. If [[_wiki_en/racas/worg\|worgs]] speak to negotiate and Varglins to survive, Murans speak to exist: their logorrhea is the assertion that they are still there.

### Relationship with the Empire

The Shadow Empire does not classify Murans as an administrative category: in censuses they appear under harmful urban fauna, alongside rats and bats, and no decree mentions them by name. Vermin receive no magical collars, are not recruited into legions, and do not merit persecution. Their only recognized role is subsistence espionage: Murans sell information for food, follow targets for a few coppers, and forget what they saw for a little more. Their lack of protection is so complete that brutal punishments inflicted on them do not even generate a bureaucratic note.

### Relationship with Magic

Access to magic comes through [[_wiki_en/magia/pactum_diaboli\|Pactum Diaboli]], but Muran spellcasters are so rare that most arcanists doubt they exist: minimal longevity, constant hunger, and nomadic life prevent training. The sensory network operates as a proto-magical ability, without consuming [[_wiki/cosmologia/magi\|magi]] or producing an arcane signature, a probable residue of the original divine interference. The few Murans with an aptitude gravitate toward concealment magic and the sensory confusion of Phobos, Wargûleth’s school, and the cost manifests as cognitive dissolution: the boundary between individual identity and the sensory network fades, private memories leak to nearby Murans, and the mind fragments while the body remains intact.

### Life Cycle

They are born in litters of three to eight and mature quickly: they walk upright at eight months, speak at two years, and reach adulthood at five. They live twenty-five to thirty-five years, although most die before twenty, with the highest infant mortality among the humanoids of [[_wiki/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]], since predation, hunger, and disease eliminate two-thirds of each litter. There is no mourning for dead pups: stopping to grieve means starving to death. A Muran over twenty-five is rare enough to suggest that they are very clever or that something is wrong.

### A Recent Mystery

An anomalous behavior disturbs the Depths: motionless Murans, with glowing red eyes, guarding passages in Vor-Ghol with a rigidity that contradicts the race’s restless nature. These Murans still emit in the ultrasonic spectrum, but the signal conveys neither fear nor direction, instead conveying immobility and obedience, and entire zones of Vor-Ghol have become inaccessible to free Murans. Cause, origin, and agent remain unknown.
