# Worg

Worgs are large [[_wiki_en/racas/vulfano\|Vulfans]], forever trapped in a duality between man and beast. Too conscious to be wild beasts and too bestial to be accepted as people, they inhabit the threshold between civilization and savagery. They live on the outskirts of cities in [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|the Empire]], tolerated because they are useful and never accepted.

### Dual Nature

Worgs are neither man nor beast, and differ from werewolves in that they do not alternate between separate forms: they are eternally trapped in their duality. The race’s origins are disputed among scholars, with no consensus. Some traditions consider them ancient shapeshifters who became trapped between forms during a corrupted ritual; others, deliberate creations of arcanists seeking soldiers who combined bestial strength and human intelligence; still others, wolves exposed to planar energies over generations. The theories are not mutually exclusive, and different lineages may have distinct origins, all perhaps later interpretations of a simpler event: the divine interference of the [[_wiki_en/eventos/guerras_raciais\|Racial Wars]], when diabolical entities crossbred humans and wild canines.

### Physiology

Bipedal by choice and quadrupedal by instinct, worgs alternate between these postures according to the situation and stand between one point seven and two point two meters tall when upright. Their fur varies by lineage, from iron gray to brindled brown and amber red, and serves as an emotional indicator: it bristles along the dorsal ridge under threat and flattens in submission. Their hands have articulated fingers, opposable thumbs, and retractable claws. Their eyes, yellow, green, or amber, have pupils that contract vertically, and their color perception is altered, with red intensified and blue almost invisible. Their speech is interspersed with growls, as though the words were bitten before being released.

### Abilities

Smell is their dominant sense. Worgs detect emotions by scent, which makes them natural informants but also subjects them, in crowded urban environments, to permanent sensory saturation. They filter sounds precisely, alternate between bipedal and quadrupedal movement without pause, heal faster than humans, and resist most plagues, although this immunity turns them into unwitting vectors of diseases they carry without symptoms.

### Psychology

Every decision a worg makes is a conflict between instinct and intellect, and many develop compartmentalized personalities. Rejected by beasts for smelling wrong and by humanoids for looking wrong, they carry a fundamental loneliness. Without a society to protect them, they have developed extreme pragmatism and moral flexibility, and many exploit their marginalized status as an advantage, since no one suspects that the “animal” in the corner understands complex conversations. Dark humor serves as their armor: they laugh at their own condition and at the other races’ pretensions to moral superiority.

### Society and the Skuld

In the primordial eras, worgs organized themselves into tribal packs known as Vargskol, of five to fifteen individuals, led by whoever kept the group alive and invisible, rather than through brute strength. In these packs, worgs and [[_wiki_en/racas/varglin\|Varglins]] coexisted in symbiosis. In the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/terceira_era\|Third Era]], however, the pack as a structure has practically disappeared: urban worgs live in fluid groups of convenience in outlying neighborhoods, sewers, and port areas, and migrate when a city becomes hostile.

Worg culture revolves around an informal system of debts and favors called skuld. Every favor granted is recorded by their infallible olfactory memory, and every favor received creates an obligation. Loyalty, gratitude, and honor are subject to the calculation of skuld, and the ultimate betrayal among worgs is an unpaid debt. The system functions as a parallel economy and the foundation of the informant networks and black markets where the race thrives.

### Customs

Worgs choose their own names upon reaching maturity, usually descriptive ones. On the three nights of the full moon, the Lunar Gatherings bring together worgs from a region to exchange information and renegotiate territories, a pragmatic custom that feeds superstitions among other races. They do not bury their dead: they leave the body to be consumed by nature and howl the dead individual’s name at dawn for three days, a rite that in cities, where howling is dangerous, is reduced to whispering the name into a glass bottle and breaking it against stone. There is also the taboo of the stákkr, the worg trapped in a single form through disease, mutilation, or choice, seen as someone who has ceased to be a worg, a wound all the more open because almost all urban worgs today live almost exclusively in bipedal form.

### Role in the Empire

The Empire tolerates worgs because they are useful, without ever accepting them. They differ from [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orcs]], who serve in the legions as formal allies, and occupy a third space, that of disposable tools: informants, scouts, carriers of illicit goods, and sometimes low-profile assassins, in exchange for tolerance and the scraps that fall from the imperial table. In [[_wiki_en/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]], they must wear a magical collar that allows them to be tracked. They do not set foot in [[_wiki_en/locais/mor_draconis\|Mor’Draconis]]: the prohibition is ancient, predating the reign of [[_wiki_en/personagens/arothistar\|Arothistar]], and has never been revoked, because the district protects itself, with patrols in groups of six and rotation at blind spots at every shift change. In Morvgrad there is also the Slaughterhouse, an informal institution where worgs process meat, clean sewers, and remove corpses, work that the [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]] call charity and that worgs recognize as voluntary slavery. The tension between accepting the Slaughterhouse and surviving or refusing it and starving is the race’s central economic conflict.

### Relationship with Magic

Worgs access magic through [[_wiki_en/magia/pactum_diaboli\|Pactum Diaboli]], but their physical duality interferes with the stability of invocations, and worg magic tends to be unstable, with unpredictable costs to the body. Most simply do not practice, since their short lifespans and nomadic lives do not favor arcane study. The few who practice gravitate toward tracking magic and shadow magic, natural extensions of canine smell and stealth. Worgs rarely produce battle mages, and their culture views a worg who casts fireballs as someone who has not understood what it means to be a worg.

### Voice

Worgs speak with pragmatism and weary irony, telling the truth packaged as a joke or lying so naturally that the distinction loses meaning. Their sentences are short but always end by hinting at something they will not say, and the transactional vocabulary of skuld permeates even emotional contexts. When nervous or threatened, their syntax fragments and involuntary guttural sounds emerge, the canine form bleeding into their speech. They avoid the word “home,” which they do not have, and replace it with “den,” “corner,” or “ground.”
