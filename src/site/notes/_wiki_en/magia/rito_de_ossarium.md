# Rite of Ossárium

The Rite of Ossárium, also called the Rite of Conversion, is the process by which ordinary humans are transformed into [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]]. Rare and reserved for exceptional circumstances, it is an alternative path into the shadow lineage, distinct from birth.

### Nature

Not every Shadar is born a Shadar. Through the Rite of Ossárium, a human acquires the physiological traits and arcane connection of the race. Its existence implies that Shadar identity is both biological and ritual: a condition that can be imposed or chosen, not merely inherited.

The ceremony requires ritualized torture, blood sacrifices, and the voluntary rupture of the human essence, and conversion takes place through a pact with diabolical entities. Once begun, the process is irreversible.

### Historical Use

During the Reinkardic Wars, the subjugation of [[_wiki_en/locais/ventrista\|Ventrista]] generated two simultaneous and opposing migratory flows. Humans who could not bear living under the shadow of the plateau fled south, toward [[_wiki_en/locais/myrgarde\|Myrgarde]]; others, ambitious, headed in the opposite direction, ascending to the plateau to join the Shadars. The [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/culto_sombrio\|Cult]] accepted those who demonstrated sufficient determination and transformed them through the Rite, forging new shadow soldiers. The two flows grew in the same proportion: the further the shadow advanced, the more people fled and the more people sought it out.

### The Most Notorious Case

The most notorious case of conversion through the Rite is that of [[_wiki_en/personagens/arothistar\|Arothistar]]. About eighty years ago, in the [[_wiki_en/eventos/conversao_de_dravhok\|Conversion of Dravhok]], the Rite transformed him into a Shadar, and he began calling himself Dravhok. He bore an earlier name that no record preserved. Many humans were converted through the Rite, and no one in the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|Shadow Empire]] had known him before his conversion; Dravhok never revealed that he had been human, and no one dared ask him. Dravhok rose to become supreme monarch of the Empire and styled himself Arothistar, the Consecrated of the Heights.

### Prohibition

About forty-four years ago, Arothistar prohibited the Rite of Ossárium. The prohibition coincided with the founding of the system of [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/justiciares\|Justiciars]] and the appointment of [[_wiki_en/personagens/orum-rahru\|Orum-Rahru]] as the first Justiciar. Definitive abolition came fourteen years later, at the [[_wiki_en/eventos/queda_de_myrgarde_e_fim_das_guerras_reinkardicas\|fall of Myrgarde]], when the monarch, in a speech, declared the Rite a capital heresy. The most likely motivation is political: to prevent humans from following the path of conversion and becoming potential rivals to the throne. By prohibiting the process he had undergone, the monarch ensured that his own origin could never be repeated.

### Implications

The Rite raises questions deliberately left open. If racial identity can be rewritten through ritual, what defines a race? If a human can become a Shadar, is the boundary between the races biological, spiritual, or political? The existence of Ossárium erodes the narrative of racial superiority that sustains the Shadow Empire, and the prohibition of the Rite is, in part, an attempt to silence that erosion.

### Unresolved Mysteries

The Rite remains surrounded by gaps, and the subject is ambiguous: does conversion still exist? Who else has undergone it? These are questions the sources do not answer.
