# Standard Structure of Spells

Each school of magic follows the same structure: ten types of spells distributed across power tiers. The structure is constant from school to school and provides a measure of both the reach and the cost of each casting.

### The Four Tiers

The ten types in each school are divided into four tiers. Three minor spells, with a light cost, form the foundation of the repertoire. Three medium spells account for intermediate effects, with a moderate cost. Three major spells achieve advanced effects, at a severe cost. At the top, a single ultimate spell concentrates the school’s catastrophic effect, with a striking name and an extreme cost.

- **Minor** — Quantity: 3 | Nature: Basic, light cost
- **Medium** — Quantity: 3 | Nature: Intermediate, moderate cost
- **Major** — Quantity: 3 | Nature: Advanced, severe cost
- **Ultimate** — Quantity: 1 | Nature: Catastrophic, extreme cost

### Thirty Schools, Six Axes

There are thirty schools in total. The grimoire groups them along axes of opposition that bring together schools of a similar nature: elemental oppositions (fire, water, ice, energy, wind, earth, darkness, light), vital oppositions (death, life, decay, healing), mental oppositions (sleep, mind, fear, knowledge), oppositions of order and chaos, spatial and temporal oppositions, and oppositions of substance. Within each axis, the schools are defined as much by what they share as by what separates them.

### Adjacency and Synergy

Neighboring schools produce effects that at first glance are difficult to distinguish, and the grimoire records the distinctions that matter in practice: fire that burns through combustion is not the energy that makes matter glow, nor is the cold that comes from outside the death that radiates from within. This map of differences is supplemented by one of affinities, since certain schools, when combined, reinforce one another, and these synergies give rise to distinct tactics for siege, field healing, and assassination.
