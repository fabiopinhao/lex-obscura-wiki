# Canonical Principles

All magic, in Lex Obscura, exacts three simultaneous costs. The Canonical Principles are the thesis and taxonomy of these costs, which accompany every spell, from any school, without exception.

### The Three Costs

The first cost is universal, tied to [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/magi\|Magi]]: the invisible fuel, a finite reserve of magical energy consumed by each spell; depleting it and persisting causes overload. The second is thematic, a physical side effect tied to the school used, which scales with the magnitude of the spell and can be mitigated, but never eliminated, through mastery. The third is ontological, a permanent transformation of the caster, which scales with time spent practicing, not with the magnitude of the spell.

### Immediate and Cumulative

The first two costs are immediate and recoverable: the universal cost is restored with rest, the thematic cost with time, shortened by mastery. The third is cumulative and irreversible, and mastery only accelerates the transformation, without ever preventing it.

### The Nature of the Ontological Cost

The ontological cost is not punishment, but a deformation of the relationship with the world. It is not punishment imposed from outside: it is the way decades of practice rewrite the caster’s perception, cognitive biases, values, and, at the extreme, their very identity. The fundamental rule is that the mage collaborates in their own corruption. The costs seduce instead of punishing: each use is comfortable, useful, justifiable, and that is precisely why the transformation advances.
