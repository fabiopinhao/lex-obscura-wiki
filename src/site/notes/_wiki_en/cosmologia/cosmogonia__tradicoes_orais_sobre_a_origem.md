# Cosmogony: Oral Traditions About the Origin

No race or culture in the world of Lex Obscura has a confirmed cosmogony. What exists are contradictory oral traditions, passed down by hierophants, druids, scholars, and storytellers throughout the ages. Each reflects the essence and values of the culture that produced it, and none presents itself as truth.

### The Shadow Version

The hierophants of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/culto_sombrio\|Shadow Cult]], among the [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]], teach that the universe was born from [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/vazio\|the Void]], a conscious blackness that preceded all existence and from which matter emerged as a defect. The gods would be fragments of the Void that acquired wills of their own, and the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/diabos\|devils]] those closest to that original nature. This version serves to justify diabolical supremacy and the legitimacy of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|Shadow Empire]] as a restoration of the natural order.

### The Red Version

[[_wiki_en/racas/varnakiano\|Varnakians]], demonic cults, and [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orc]] clans speak of a [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/incendio_primordial\|Primordial Fire]]: the universe would have been born not from the void but from excess, a detonation of chaotic energy whose shards are the planes and each god a surviving flame. It is an emotional and visceral vision that celebrates chaos as the natural state and condemns stability as betrayal.

### The Green Version

The druidic tradition, preserved in the forests of [[_wiki_en/locais/thal_serin\|Thal’Serin]] and passed down by masters such as [[_wiki_en/personagens/peredur\|Peredur]], teaches that the universe is a living organism that has always existed. There was no creation because there was no beginning. The material plane is the body, the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/plano_etereo\|ethereal plane]] the nervous system, and the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/planos_divinos\|Divine Planes]] the organs. The gods are functions of the organism, not its creators. The [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/titas\|titans]] would be closer to the truth because they understand that everything is cyclical; devils and [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/demonios\|demons]] would be pathologies that try to redirect the body’s functions toward their own ends; the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/anjos\|angels]], an immune system that is necessary but dangerous when hypersensitive; and the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/siderais\|sidereals]], pure thought, the organism’s ability to observe itself. The idea that even stars die, formulated by Peredur, expresses this philosophy: nothing is permanent.

### The White Version

Nearly extinct in [[_wiki_en/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]], the luminous tradition survives in fragments preserved by [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/ordem_de_azazel\|the Order of Azazel]] and by isolated communities. It teaches that the universe was created by a Will, not a specific god, but the collective intention of celestial beings that preceded all known categories. Creation was an act of generosity: these celestials gave power, not essence, and remained whole, though diminished. The angels would be the guardians most faithful to this legacy; devils and demons, those who corrupted their share of the inheritance; the titans, those who clung to the present and forgot the purpose; and the sidereals, those who chose to observe in reverence. It is the most internally coherent cosmogony and the least verifiable, and even defenders such as the priest [[_wiki_en/personagens/abramis\|Abramis]] admit that faith sustains it, not evidence. It also harbors the belief most disturbing to its own faithful: that some angels fell, corrupting the power entrusted to them until they became devils. The existence of [[_wiki_en/panteao/abbhal\|Abbhal]], a liminal entity whose form is that of a Fallen Angel, is cited as proof by some and dismissed as metaphor by others, and the tradition itself hesitates before the implication that no purity is permanent.

### The Ethereal Version

Rare and confined to academic circles, the sidereal tradition proposes that the universe is a mathematical pattern that emerged spontaneously. There was no creator because none was necessary: the laws that govern existence are self-sufficient, like equations that solve themselves. The material plane is the most stable solution, the ethereal plane the intermediate variable, and the gods are recursive patterns that gained consciousness upon reaching a certain complexity. It is the least popular version and the most difficult to understand, and the sidereals who communicate with mortals rarely confirm or correct it, feeding it with silence.

### Races Without a Cosmogony of Their Own

Not every race has an origin of its own. The [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Druls]] share the shadow version with the Shadars, with subterranean inflections: for them, the Void is literally the darkness of the deepest caves, and [[_wiki_en/locais/vor-ghol\|Vor-Ghol]] would be the place closest to the origin. The [[_wiki_en/racas/karashim\|Karashim]] dwarves, the true colorless race, likewise have no unified tradition, and adopt the version of the region or religion in which they live.

### Note on Truth

No tradition is either confirmed or denied. The coexistence of contradictory versions is deliberate and reflects a principle of the world itself: the gods do not explain themselves. Knowledge of the origin remains inaccessible, and every attempt to reach it says more about the questioner than about what is being asked. It is possible that all are wrong, and possible that fragments of each contain truth.
