# Divine Ontology

Divine Ontology brings together what is known and what is speculated about the nature of the gods of [[_wiki_en/locais/thamakard\|Thamakard]]: what they are, how they relate to mortals, and how they differ from one another.

### Nature of the Gods

Gods are eternal. They are not born, do not age, and do not die. There is no confirmed record of a deity ceasing to exist, although the oral traditions of many cultures mention sleeping, forgotten, or vanished gods, without anyone being able to confirm whether these terms describe real states or merely a loss of relevance among mortals. What the gods are, ontologically, remains a matter of speculation: primordial consciousnesses, personified natural forces, entities predating matter, or abstractions that acquired a will. No definition has been confirmed, and the gods themselves, on the rare occasions when they communicate, offer no explanations about their origin.

What is known with empirical certainty is that the gods inhabit existential planes of their own (except the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/siderais\|sidereals]], who wander the Ethereal), exert influence over the material plane through pacts, devotion, or meditation, have specific domains in the form of schools of magic, and can create avatars. They can be imprisoned through the joint action of other gods, except the sidereals, who, having no plane of their own, cannot be imprisoned. They cannot be destroyed.

### Divine Bonds

The relationship between mortals and gods takes radically different forms depending on the category. Only two categories establish direct, formal bonds: the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/diabos\|devils]], through contracts ([[_wiki_en/magia/pactum_diaboli\|Pactum Diaboli]]), and the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/demonios\|demons]], through bonds of force ([[_wiki_en/magia/vinculum_daemonis\|Vinculum Daemonis]]). Both involve indelible physical marks, permanent obligations, and irreversible costs, and are the only systems in which the mortal can properly be called pact-bound.

The other categories operate through mechanisms that do not constitute pacts. The [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/titas\|titans]] relate through symbiosis ([[_wiki_en/magia/symbiosis_naturae\|Symbiosis Naturae]]), a living, reciprocal connection with nature spirits that dissolves if the balance is violated. The [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/anjos\|angels]] grant power through faith ([[_wiki_en/magia/devotio_sancta\|Devotio Sancta]]), proportional to the sincerity of belief. The sidereals require mental discipline ([[_wiki_en/magia/meditatio_aetheris\|Meditatio Aetheris]]), decades of training to establish contact. In these three cases, there is no contract or imposed obligation, the connection can be severed by either party, and recovery after a rupture is possible, though costly. This distinction is crucial to understanding artifacts such as [[_wiki_en/artefatos/fendris\|Fendris]], which reject pact-bound hands: an [[_wiki_en/racas/alvarar\|Álvarar]] devoted to [[_wiki_en/panteao/azazel\|Azazel]], a druid in symbiosis with [[_wiki_en/panteao/morlgrund\|Morlgrund]], or a [[_wiki_en/racas/frenim\|Frenim]] trained by the sidereals are not pact-bound and are not subject to the restriction.

Pacts with devils are recorded on three simultaneous levels: physical (the text or ritual speech), verbal (the magical utterance of sealing), and spiritual (the indelible mark on the soul). Once sealed, they are immutable, and even the god cannot revoke them unilaterally. Demonic bonds are more unstable: the demon can break them on a whim, and the mark never disappears. In both cases, it is the mortals who usually seek out the gods, which positions the deities as selective sources of power with lines of aspirants.

So-called divine monogamy is not a metaphysical law, but a tendency proportional to the mortal’s value, applicable to all categories. Gods prefer exclusivity: an ordinary mortal rarely obtains even one bond, and most who do attract no interest from a second god. Mortals of high value can attract multiple gods willing to share, at an exponential cost. In practice, two simultaneous bonds are already exceedingly rare, about two and a half percent of those who have magic, and three is a historical aberration with no known precedent.

### Color as Essence

The five colors, black, red, green, white, and blue, define alignment, not power. Each represents an essence: a set of emotions, goals, and behaviors that guide the nature of each entity. There are no power tiers, no clear hierarchy among categories, and no consensus, among mortals or among gods, about who is stronger.

- **Black** — Essence: Power, control, ambition | Goal: Dominion, order through imposition | Behavior: Calculating, legalistic, contractual
- **Red** — Essence: Freedom, destruction, passion | Goal: Creative chaos, experience | Behavior: Impulsive, destructive
- **Green** — Essence: Nature, cycles, balance | Goal: Preservation, symbiosis | Behavior: Tolerant, relentless when acting
- **White** — Essence: Justice, purity, moral order | Goal: Protection of the weak, moral code | Behavior: Inflexible, abandons transgressors
- **Blue** — Essence: Knowledge, intellect, observation | Goal: Understanding, universal patterns | Behavior: Without moral judgment, drawn to genius

Bicolored entities combine two essences, with a dominant one that defines the access system. The most revealing contrasts are not between the most distant natures, but between the closest. Angels and titans share a protective inclination, but differ in method: titans are tolerant and intervene only when the balance is catastrophically disrupted, while angels establish absolute codes and abandon those who transgress them. In the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/idade_esquecida\|Forgotten Age]], this difference divided the [[_wiki_en/racas/luminares\|Luminars]] themselves, and their fragmentation into [[_wiki_en/racas/sylvano\|Sylvans]] and [[_wiki_en/racas/elarino\|Elarins]] reflected a spiritual schism predating the fall. Likewise, devils and demons share an ambition for power, but exercise it in opposite ways: the devil builds empires and hierarchies, the demon destroys structures and limits. A devil wants to rule the board; a demon wants to overturn it. This incompatibility explains why [[_wiki_en/locais/vor-ghol\|Vor-Ghol]], in the Forgotten Age, could not accommodate both factions: the Luminars bound to devils, the future [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Druls]], demanded order and subterranean discipline, while those bound to demons, the future [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orcs]], were too chaotic to submit, and the expulsion of the proto-orcs to the surface was an inevitable consequence of two incompatible essences occupying the same space.

### Proximity to the Material Plane

The distance between each divine category and Thamakard is not geographical, but functional, measured by the degree of interest in and influence over the world of mortals. The titans are the closest guardians, since their existence is bound to the nature of the material plane, which they consider their home. The sidereals wander the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/plano_etereo\|ethereal plane]] at varying distances, driven by intellectual curiosity and without moral judgment. Angels, devils, and demons operate from distant planes of their own, exerting influence through the pact-bound, hierophants, and rituals.

### Imprisonment and Reduction of Presence

There are two distinct states of divine weakening. Imprisonment is real, but only through the action of another god or an alliance of gods: the entity is sealed in its own plane, retaining consciousness but losing the ability to influence Thamakard. The best-known case is that of [[_wiki_en/panteao/vozrador\|Vozrador]], imprisoned about fifty-five years ago by an alliance between [[_wiki_en/panteao/malagoth\|Malagoth]] and [[_wiki_en/panteao/velmorain\|Velmorain]]. The fate of existing pacts after a god’s imprisonment is disputed: the most widespread account holds that the pact breaks and the soul is released, while a minority position maintains that the bond remains as a spiritual scar, weakened but capable of pulsing under certain conditions.

Reduction of presence is different: a god loses influence in a region without being imprisoned, as a consequence of the dominance of opposing energies. The white gods suffer from this in [[_wiki_en/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]], where the luminous faith is almost extinct not because Azazel has been defeated, but because the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/manto_das_sombras\|mantle of shadows]] has made the continent inhospitable to Azazel’s influence. The distinction is critical: Vozrador is sealed and conscious, merely weakened, and could be freed through a specific action; Azazel is free, but irrelevant, and would need a civilizational transformation to regain presence.

The absence of hierarchy is demonstrated in Vozrador’s imprisonment itself: Malagoth and Velmorain had to act together and catch him by surprise, even though Vozrador, in other respects, is considered more powerful than both. Relative strength among gods is not a fixed scale, and depends on domain, context, and circumstance.

### Avatars

Gods have no agreed-upon physical form. No race agrees on the appearance of any deity, and iconographic representations vary across cultures and eras. What all traditions record is the phenomenon of avatars: temporary manifestations of a god on the material plane. An avatar is not the god, but a projection of its will, a limited extension of its power that assumes physical form for a set time, acts according to the deity’s interests, and dissolves when its function is fulfilled or when the energy sustaining it runs out.

Not every god creates avatars. Many have never manifested, preferring to operate only through the pact-bound and rituals, and a manifestation is a rare event, recorded in chronicles as a catastrophe or a miracle depending on the perspective. One example is Morlgrund’s avatar in [[_wiki_en/locais/thal_serin\|Thal’Serin]], which manifested when massive bloodshed on sacred ground threatened the balance of the forest. It demonstrates the central principles of the phenomenon: it required an extreme trigger, assumed a form consistent with the god’s essence (earth, stone, tectonic fire), acted with a specific purpose of judgment and protection, and dissipated when that purpose was fulfilled.

How corporeal an avatar can be depends on the category’s proximity to the material plane. For the titans, whose territory has a thin veil, it is a condensed presence, a real fraction of the god that crosses over, and this is why the guardian of Thal’Serin was described as a foundation, not a mere projection. For the more distant angels, devils, and demons, the practical ceiling is an anchored projection, bound to a marked individual, a condemned individual, or a relic. The sidereals, having neither plane nor body, do not manifest corporeally. These modes, from environmental signature to avatar, constitute a scale of increasing cost described in [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/manifestacoes_divinas\|Divine Manifestations]].
