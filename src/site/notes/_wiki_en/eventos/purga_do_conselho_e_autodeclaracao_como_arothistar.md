# Purge of the Council and Self-Declaration as Arothistar

The Purge of the Council is the coup that, during the Reinkardic Wars, transformed General [[_wiki_en/personagens/arothistar\|Aroth]] from a hegemonic commander into the effective ruler of the Cult and gave rise to the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|Shadow Empire]]. Also remembered as Aroth’s Coup, the March on [[_wiki_en/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]], and the birth of the Empire, the episode culminated in the seizure of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/conselho_sombrio\|Shadow Council]] and Aroth’s self-declaration as Arothistar.

### The Stalemate Before Myrgarde

Satellite citadels of [[_wiki_en/locais/myrgarde\|Myrgarde]] that had previously been untouchable fell in succession, and Aroth was advancing on the Central Empire’s protective belt, tightening the siege from the outside inward. Myrgarde, however, remained impenetrable: its sacred walls, reinforced by centuries of celestial blessings, withstood every assault. The [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orcs]] on the front lines fell by the thousands, cut down by enchanted arrows and alchemical bombs that the defenders rained down with the precision of those defending the last citadel in the world.

The city’s impenetrability exposed fractures that the war had kept hidden. The Cult’s generals, mostly [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/templarios\|Templars]] bound to specific temples, began acting on their own, for the glory of conquering Myrgarde was the greatest prize a Templar could aspire to. One by one, they hurled their legions against the walls in uncoordinated assaults, competing with one another for the honor of the first advance. Coordination deteriorated to the point of collapse, and in an episode unprecedented in the Cult’s military history, [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadar]] legions attacked one another, not by mistake, but in a deliberate dispute over who would breach the gates.

### The Rise of Aroth

Aroth watched the chaos from a unique position. He was not a Templar and owed allegiance to no specific temple. The title of Aroth, which had been bestowed on him out of popular admiration, aroused envy in equal measure among shadow lords and much of the Council, and many refused to call him by the name the people had given him, using only the formal rank of councilor.

Under pressure from the chaos in the camps, the Shadow Council ordered Aroth to contain the internal conflicts and provided him with a list of generals and temples considered dissidents from the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/lex_obscura_decreto\|Lex Obscura]]. What the Council expected was discipline; what it received was conquest. Aroth attacked the named generals and took their armies. In some cases, resistance was bloody; in others, the legions simply turned against their own generals and willingly joined Aroth’s legion, preferring a victorious commander to a Templar who led them into repeated slaughter. The insubordination ceased, but Aroth emerged from the operation with an army larger than any force the Council controlled.

### The Truce and the March

The arithmetic was inescapable: Aroth held excessive military power and the support of Morvgrad’s population. Most of the Council concluded that he was a threat to the capital itself. The response was the most important truce of the Reinkardic Wars. The Council proposed a cessation of hostilities to Myrgarde, not out of generosity, but to prevent Aroth from returning from there with a victory that would make him unchallengeable. The truce was an instrument of internal politics, not of peace, and it did not last long.

The Council then ordered Aroth to return to Morvgrad for a phase of reorganization. When they realized that he was ascending the plateau with practically the entire veteran military force of the plains and forests, the tone changed, and they ordered him to halt the march and wait in the orc forts on the outskirts. The rule was ancient and inviolable: no one crossed the fortified perimeter with a legion of orcs, for the forts existed to keep orcs out as much as to keep enemies away. Aroth crossed. Orders to stop him traveled down the chain of command and died upon reaching any officer who had seen Aroth in the field.

### The Purge and the Self-Declaration

Aroth entered Morvgrad and was applauded, not by a faction, but by everyone. The inhabitants took to the streets to behold the general marching to the wall of [[_wiki_en/locais/mor_draconis\|Mor’Draconis]], the city’s middle ring. For the first and last time in the history of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/culto_sombrio\|Shadow Cult]], entire contingents of well-trained and obedient orcs entered the sacred territory of [[_wiki_en/locais/xarn_aroth\|Xarn’Aroth]], and the rare few who tried to attack Aroth were killed or detained by the people themselves.

The purge that followed was surgical. The most powerful councilors lived in the tower of [[_wiki_en/locais/mor_severith\|Mor’Severith]], an old private library and residence of the Council’s elite, and it was there that Aroth’s personal guard entered alongside their leader. Many corpses came out of the tower that night. The councilors who did not reside in Mor’Severith were given an implicit choice, submission or the same fate, and most chose to survive. [[_wiki_en/personagens/xarn\|Xarn]], who had already been living under necromancy for centuries, remained neutral while the general devoured the Council.

With popular support and military power concentrated in his hands, Aroth did what no Shadar had dared before him and declared himself Arothistar, the Consecrated from on High. The name was not merely a title: it was a program, and it marked the transition from hegemonic general to effective ruler of the Cult.
