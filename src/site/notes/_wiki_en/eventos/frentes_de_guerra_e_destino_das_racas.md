# War Fronts and the Fate of the Races

During the Reinkardic Wars, the conflict did not affect all the races of the continent in the same way. The fate of each people reflected its position on the continental chessboard: the geography it inhabited, the alliances it maintained, and the usefulness its nature offered. In the end, humans, gnomes, and [[_wiki_en/racas/sylvano\|Sylvans]] would be the three most enslaved races, while others survived through the protection of the terrain or perished because they refused to surrender.

### The Peoples of the Forests

The Sylvans did not form organized fronts or ally themselves with [[_wiki_en/locais/myrgarde\|Myrgarde]]. Their forces were rangers, hunters, and druids who knew every root and every watercourse in their territories, and fought to defend their own forests, not the human plains. When a forest fell, the survivors tried to reach another before the fire did, and many failed. Alongside humans and gnomes, they were the most enslaved race of the war.

The [[_wiki_en/racas/gerut\|Gerut]] were the least affected. They inhabited only titanic forests: where there was a Gerut people, there was a titan, and where there was a titan, conquest became unfeasible. They survived through the nature of the terrain they chose to inhabit, not through strength of arms.

### The Elarins

The [[_wiki_en/racas/elarino\|Elarins]] occupied a distinct position among the elven peoples. Many had lived in human cities since the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/primeira_era\|First Era]], forming part of Myrgarde’s court, temples, and arcane academies. When war broke out, they fought side by side with the [[_wiki_en/racas/alvarar\|Álvarars]] in the ranks of the Central [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|Empire]], while others defended magical citadels such as [[_wiki_en/locais/elenir\|Elenir]], sustaining arcane barriers and [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/magi\|magi]] nexuses that the Cult still had no means of penetrating. Unlike the Álvarars, however, the Elarins did not fight to the death: when resistance became untenable, they withdrew to citadels protected by ancient enchantments. As the citadels were taken one by one over the course of the war, slavery was all that remained for the captured Elarins, and over time an Elarin slave became a luxury item in [[_wiki_en/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]], a rarity that conferred prestige on the master who owned one.

### The Álvarars

The Álvarars, angelic paladins whose lineage was forged between the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/idade_esquecida\|Forgotten Age]] and the First Era, held leadership positions in Myrgarde’s armies. The Sancta magic they channeled through celestial faith was the most effective answer the plains had to the Cult’s diabolical rituals. Where the Álvarars commanded, human soldiers fought with conviction; where they fell, panic spread as quickly as fire in the forests. They did not surrender and were not enslaved: they were killed.

### Gnomes and the Logistics of War

Gnomes did not serve as soldiers, since their physical stature and temperament made them unsuited to the front lines. They did, however, serve in roles that no other race performed as well: alchemical weapons for Myrgarde’s armies, stewardry and campaign logistics, and an ingenuity that kept supply chains functioning under conditions that would have paralyzed a purely human administration.

### The Struggle for the Underground

The struggle for the forges proved as decisive as the battles on the surface. The dwarves had supplied weapons and equipment to the plains since reopening their mountains at the beginning of the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/segunda_era\|Second Era]], and their forges produced the finest blades, the most durable armor, and the most precise mechanisms on the continent. The war, however, reached them, and the dwarven mines were conquered one by one by the Cult’s forces. Many dwarves fled to the only depths they knew, [[_wiki_en/locais/vor-ghol\|Vor-Ghol]], even though the underground was [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Drul]] territory and relations between the two peoples had never been friendly. The alternative was slavery on the surface.

An unexpected economic ecosystem arose in the depths. The dwarves established small mines in galleries the Druls did not exploit and began supplying ore, tools, and forging services in exchange for protection and space. The Druls rarely enslaved them, preferring cheap, productive labor to resentful, sabotaging servants. The irony did not escape observers: while the [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]] on the surface faced chronic productivity problems with coerced dwarves, the Druls profited from artisans who worked of their own free will. The Reinkardic Wars did not reach Vor-Ghol, and the dwarves of the depths prospered in the shadow of a conflict that devastated everything above their heads. The division between surface dwarves and underground dwarves, born there, persists to the present.

### The Druls in Open Terrain

The Druls did not limit themselves to the role of underground allies. For the first time in the history of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/alianca_subterranea\|Underground Alliance]], Drul contingents fought on the surface alongside the Shadar armies, bringing with them millennia of experience in underground combat adapted to the conditions of open terrain: nighttime ambushes, darkness spells that blinded entire ranks, and poisons that corrupted armor and flesh with equal indifference. Without the Druls, the Shadars would have faced the first half of the war without a significant part of their arcane capability.

### The Logic of Slavery

In the first half of the conflict, Shadars and Druls bore the burden of the front lines, with severe losses on both sides. Myrgarde’s campaigns against the plateau failed because of the steep terrain and the outlying fortresses, which turned every assault into a massacre, but the Cult’s assaults on the plains were also costly. Shadar and Drul losses could not be replaced as readily as the war demanded, and the solution would come from the [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orcs]]. Meanwhile, the Cult accumulated slaves. The first groups were not human: captured Sylvans, gnomes from outlying communities, half-elves from border villages. Humans came later, as the smaller kingdoms capitulated and their populations were absorbed into the imperial machine. Enslavement followed a logic of utility, not random cruelty, and each race was directed toward the work to which its nature predisposed it.
