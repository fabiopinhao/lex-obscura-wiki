# Pax Alabastrina

The Pax Alabastrina, remembered as the golden age of the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/primeira_era\|First Era]], is the long period of prosperity and cooperation that followed the [[_wiki_en/eventos/queda_dos_luminares\|fall of the Luminars]]. It is not a discrete event, but the sociopolitical context of centuries in which celestial faith became a continental institution and magic, previously restricted, began to spread among ordinary humans.

### The Founding Alliance

Despite the founding violence, the centuries following the fall of the Luminars brought unprecedented advances and alliances. The collaboration between [[_wiki_en/racas/elarino\|Elarins]] and [[_wiki_en/racas/alvarar\|Álvarars]], begun in the final years of [[_wiki_en/conceitos/idade_esquecida\|the Forgotten Age]], matured and transformed the continent. The Elarins already possessed arcane power granted by the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/anjos\|angels]] before the fragmentation, but it was in the First Era that mutual exchange with the Álvarars refined the Sancta system. The Álvarars gained elven erudition from this coexistence, and the Elarins absorbed human devotional discipline and formalized it in liturgy. From this union arose the first great cathedrals, built on grounds consecrated by both bodies of knowledge.

Some Elarins began to live among humans, integrating into the courts and temples of the central plains. Others remained in purely Elarin citadels, such as [[_wiki_en/locais/elenir\|Elenir]], preserving the [[_wiki_en/racas/luminares\|Luminar]] heritage with almost devotional fidelity. The elven presence in human kingdoms was seen as a source of prestige and protection, and this religious harmony unified human peoples who had previously warred among themselves, now gathered under a common pantheon of celestial gods.

### Prosperity and Knowledge

With religious unification came material prosperity. Trade between kingdoms flourished, previously dangerous routes were pacified by joint patrols of Álvarars and royal militias, and agriculture expanded into lands that war had left fallow for generations. Alongside faith came advances in knowledge: artisans and scholars began to work methodically, cataloging the properties of materials, experimenting with combinations, and recording results for subsequent generations.

The gnomes, ignored or underestimated in the previous era, found a place that the Forgotten Age had never offered them. The alabastrine religions prohibited the genocide of gnomes, a practice that some kingdoms had tolerated. The prohibition did not mean equality, since gnomes continued to be seen as a lesser race, but under the protection of the new dogmas some followed the Sancta path and began to live in cities. Ingenious and stubborn, they were essential to the development of alchemy, transforming a scattered esoteric practice into a systematized discipline, transmitted through treatises and taught in schools. Even the dwarves, once isolated in underground citadels, began to trade with elves and humans, not out of generosity, but because the kingdoms paid well; their forges produced weapons and mechanisms that no other race reproduced, and their expertise became indispensable to the creation of magical weapons and enchanted seals. It was during this period that the first half-elves appeared, chiefly from unions between Elarins and Álvarars.

### The Limits of Harmony

Harmony was never complete. The [[_wiki_en/racas/sylvano\|Sylvans]], closer to nature and the deep forests, remained faithful to the primordial [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/titas\|titans]] and refused integration with humans, distrustful of the new kingdoms’ accelerated progress and religious presumption. The [[_wiki_en/racas/gerut\|Gerut]] followed a different path: originating from humanity itself, they shared blood and memory with the peoples of the plains, and the tribes whose forests bordered the kingdoms cultivated peaceful coexistence, exchanging herbs, pelts, and forest knowledge for tools, salt, and fabrics. They did not intermingle with the kingdoms or accept their gods, but neither did they reject them with the Sylvans’ disdain. Other Gerut tribes, from the more remote forests, remained as isolated as the Sylvans themselves, and little was known of them.
