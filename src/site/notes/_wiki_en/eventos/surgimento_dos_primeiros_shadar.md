# Emergence of the First Shadars

The emergence of the first [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]] marks the moment when part of humanity broke with celestial orthodoxy and sought profane pacts. Less a datable event than a paradigm shift, it rearranged the balance of [[_wiki_en/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]] and became the driving force of the eras that followed.

### Origin

The moral rift that the [[_wiki_en/racas/alvarar\|Álvarars]] had opened in humanity did not heal with the victory over the [[_wiki_en/racas/luminares\|Luminars]]. The imposition of a code by the Sancta faith produced order, but also suffocation: anyone who rejected celestial doctrine was treated as a heretic, and transgression and transgressors became indistinguishable in the eyes of the high clergy. Orthodoxy did not tolerate ambiguity, and not everyone endured the yoke.

Among the humans who had participated in the massacre of the Luminars, some rejected the absolution the faith offered and embraced the guilt of their own deed. Frustrated by the limitations of the doctrine and suffocated by the moral surveillance of the Álvarars, they broke with the church. They were the first to hear the whispers of the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/diabos\|devils]] and the fallen [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/anjos\|angels]], entities that offered power without demanding purity, only obedience. Those who sought absolution remained in the faith and made it a house of penance. Shadow did not arise spontaneously: it was the response to an excess of light.

### The Black Gods and the Druls

The diabolical pacts proved devastatingly effective. The black gods, long confined to the recesses of [[_wiki_en/locais/vor-ghol\|Vor-Ghol]], saw an unexpected opportunity in humans. For eras, they had considered humans too weak and as resistant to corruption as dwarves, who stubbornly rejected any profane bond. That was why the [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Druls]] had, until then, been their main channels in the material world. Now a human generation was emerging that was open to transgression, ready to channel the whims of dark divinities.

### Breaking the Monopoly

Before the Shadars, the dark alignment was a Drul monopoly: diabolical pacts belonged underground, in the darkness of Vor-Ghol, to a race that had never desired the surface. The Shadars broke that monopoly. They brought the black gods to the plains, to the cities, into human civilization itself. What the Druls did out of millennia-old tradition, the Shadars did out of rebellion; and where the Druls were content to reign in the Depths, the Shadars had the ambition to reign everywhere. This difference in scale would be the driving force of the eras that followed.
