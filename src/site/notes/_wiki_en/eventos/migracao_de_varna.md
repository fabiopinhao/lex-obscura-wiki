# Varna’s Migration to Veinkard

The migration of [[_wiki_en/personagens/varna\|Varna]] was the movement of the last great dragon from the [[_wiki_en/locais/pico_de_nethys\|Peak of Nethys]] to [[_wiki_en/locais/varnak\|Varnak]], in [[_wiki_en/locais/veinkard\|Veinkard]], during the Second Era. Her departure left the Peak permanently deserted and removed the living barrier that, throughout the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/primeira_era\|First Era]], had held the [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orc]] tribes in the [[_wiki_en/locais/dorsal\|Dorsal]].

### The Departure from the Peak of Nethys

Varna endured the plateau longer than any other dragon. Young and territorial, she hunted orcs on the slopes of the Dorsal, and her presence served as a bulwark against incursions from the north. The dark corruption, however, did not distinguish prey from predator: the same diabolic energy that had withered the elder dragons saturated the Peak and made staying untenable. Without a pact with any entity, Varna lacked the resistance the corruption demanded, and eventually abandoned the Peak of Nethys, heading west.

### Arrival in Varnak

In the valleys of Varnak, in Veinkard, Varna was received with veneration and fear, and never departed from there again. The name itself is an external imposition: it is not known for certain whether the [[_wiki_en/racas/varnakiano\|Varnakians]] of the valleys or the [[_wiki_en/racas/draconideo\|Draconids]] of the peaks coined it. Dragons do not use names among themselves; “Varna” is the designation imposed by races that need to name what they fear.

### Consequences in the Dorsal

With the Peak deserted and the region’s magical barrier already depleted, nothing remained to prevent passage across the mountain range. The orc tribes of the north, which throughout the First Era had feared the Dorsal as the dragons’ hunting grounds, noticed the absence and began descending to the plateau in ever larger groups. This also fueled a pragmatic truce between orcs and [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadar]]: peoples with an ancient and mutual hatred, who nevertheless recognized common enemies and credited the Shadars, even if through an accidental effect of the corruption, with expelling the dragons that had preyed on them for millennia. It was no alliance. It was merely the recognition that the enemy of one’s enemy deserved a truce.
