# Ghol-Urarn

Ghol-Urarn, the Diluvial Contagion, is one of the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/titas\|titans]], a deity of three essences who governs waterborne pestilence. He manifests as a hydra with a swollen body and pustulent hide, and his domain combines the corruption of living matter with distribution through currents: the disease that others sow through contact or air, Ghol-Urarn pours into the current.

### Nature

The suppurating hydra bears heads that hang at various angles, some raised and alert, others drooping as though the weight of infection bent them. A different fluid drips from each jaw: from some, yellowish serous fluid; from others, dark pus that coagulates into living crusts on the ground. A sweetish smell of infected wounds precedes the form and permeates the earth, and does not wash away in the rain. Beneath the translucent skin of the necks circulates a cloudy, greenish liquid, too thick for blood and too slow for lymph. The body drags itself along on short, atrophied legs, leaving a wet trail that darkens the soil and kills low-growing vegetation within a few hours. The heads that die do not fall off: they wither, blacken, and continue suppurating, as though decomposition were merely another phase of functioning.

### Domain

Ghol-Urarn’s power joins [[_wiki_en/escolas/patho\|Patho]], which corrupts the organism through internal decomposition, with [[_wiki_en/escolas/hydro\|Hydro]], which ensures that the corruption travels faster and farther than legs or wind would allow. Rivers, lakes, wells, and water tables become territory for contagion, and contamination scales with the available water: entire villages have fallen ill from a single well, and provinces have lost crops and herds when the plague reached the river. The titan does not attack; he accelerates what is already underway. All organic matter decomposes, water already carries impurities, bodies already harbor dormant pathogens, and Ghol-Urarn merely removes the interval between potentiality and actuality. The contamination has no target because water has no loyalty: it flows where gravity directs, and disease follows the same path.

### The Diluvial Contagion

Where Ghol-Urarn passes, the local hydrology becomes a disease distribution system. Rains carry invisible spores, springs well up cloudy, and fish float belly-up in schools that rot before sinking. Survivors from stricken regions describe the water as something whose nature had changed: it still looked like water, still quenched thirst, still ran downhill, and quenched thirst and sowed fever at the same time. The suppurating hydra does not need to attack; all it takes is for rain to fall. Among the titans, who preserve the natural world, Ghol-Urarn occupies an uncomfortable position, for he corrupts it from within, using the hydrologic cycle itself as a weapon. The tension is not accidental: decomposition is as much a part of the green cycle as growth, and Ghol-Urarn governs the half that the other titans prefer to ignore.

### Manifestation

When the floodwaters recede, sweetish-smelling sludge remains that rots vegetation within a few hours, and fish rise belly-up with cloudy eyes: the most widely recognized sign of the passage of the diluvial contagion.
