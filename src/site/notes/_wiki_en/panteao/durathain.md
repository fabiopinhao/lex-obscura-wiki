# Durathain

Durathain, the Filthy Hand, is the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/titas\|titan]] of manual labor, forges, and the underworld. His domain combines [[_wiki_en/escolas/toxo\|Toxo]], poison and corrosion, with [[_wiki_en/escolas/geo\|Geo]], the earth and what lies hidden beneath it. Among the titans, he is the only one who rules by utility: where the others preside over primordial forces or instincts, Durathain presides over craft.

### Nature

He manifests as a behemoth with armored hide, covered in layers of mineral deposits, dried mud, and smelting slag that never fall away. His hands are disproportionate to his body, as broad as mine gates, with fingers that end in claws worn down by use and stained by substances that corrode whatever they touch. Fragments of raw ore have become embedded in his hide over the ages, and acid scars form deep furrows where the skin has grown back thick and uneven. His eyes are small for the size of his skull, buried beneath crusts of calcification, and shine with the dull color of an ember covered in ash. The smell is of sulfur and hot iron over churned earth.

### Domain

Geo tears the ore from the rock; Toxo processes it through corrosive baths, acidic vapors, and residues that poison the surrounding soil. The combination is the entire cycle of extraction. Durathain presides over the work that no one wants to do and everyone needs done: mines, forges, foundries, tanneries, dye works, every trade that leaves hands dirty with something water cannot wash away. The earth exacts a toll from those who dig into it. Miners breathe dust that calcifies their lungs, smelters swallow vapors that rot away their sense of taste before they notice the loss, and in the tanneries the baths eat the skin off hands layer by layer. None of these men offers a sacrifice to Durathain: the trade has already taken its toll.

### The Hand

“By Durathain’s dirty fingers!” is a curse used by miners, smiths, and any worker whose trade requires contact with substances that burn or poison. The invocation is not reverence, but recognition of kinship: those who work deep in a tunnel or before a forge know the filth that defines the titan and the filth he imposes on those who approach his domains. The epithet is as much a warning as an identity. The filthy hand does the necessary work, and the price is to carry on its fingers the residue of everything it has touched. There is no temple dedicated to his name. His devotees recognize one another by the state of their hands.

### Manifestation

A mine gallery that begins to exude sulfur, with the ore staining the hands and corroding the tool, is a sign recognized among miners: the Filthy Hand has passed underneath.
