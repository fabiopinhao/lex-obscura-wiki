# Myralune

Myralune, the Sleeping Tide, is a sidereal-titan entity that projects sleep through water. It manifests as a submerged leviathan of proportions that defy estimation, resting in depths where sunlight has already died out, and its domain combines the suppression of consciousness with distribution through ocean currents.

### Nature

The leviathan’s body rests coiled upon itself in spirals that resemble fossilized shells. Its skin emits a pale glow in slow waves, a sourceless luminosity that pulses to the rhythm of an ancient, submerged breath, and vestigial fins drag along the ocean floor like curtains of dead fabric. Its eyes are closed; they always have been. Theologians of the ethereal orders interpret the dormancy as saturation: the leviathan’s consciousness has already absorbed everything it needed to perceive, and what remains is excess, radiated by the body like residual heat from an extinguished furnace. Myralune finished before falling asleep, sleep came afterward as a consequence, and the entire ocean became a vehicle for distribution.

### Domain

Myralune’s power unites [[_wiki_en/escolas/hypno\|Hypno]], which suppresses wakefulness without violence, with [[_wiki_en/escolas/hydro\|Hydro]], which distributes the suppression across every connected surface and depth. Each current that leaves the sleeping body carries torpor that dissipates with distance, potent enough to put entire schools of fish to sleep nearby and induce drowsiness in sailors leagues from the source. Myralune’s sleep is contagious through the very substance that sustains life. As a sidereal-titan entity, it occupies a rare threshold between ethereal discipline and natural symbiosis: those who channel its power inherit the overlapping costs of both schools, and Hypno’s chronic exhaustion merges with Hydro’s emotional instability. Over time, practitioners cease to distinguish what they dreamed from what they experienced, and their emotions change direction like currents that lose their bed.

### The Sleeping Tide

Fishermen in coastal regions recognize the sleeping tide by the texture of the water, oily and warm, with reflections that move more slowly than expected. Nets pulled from these waters bring up living fish in a catatonic state, with motionless fins and glassy eyes that follow movement without registering presence. Ships that cross saturated currents report entire crews succumbing to sleep midway through a shift: lookouts collapse over the helm, rowers let go of their oars with relaxed hands, and awakening comes hours later, sometimes days, with no memory of the interval. The calm that precedes the phenomenon is absolute, without any wind, and the water’s surface seems to breathe in slow waves of pale luminosity. Sailors who fall asleep under this calm invariably report the same dream: spirals that repeat endlessly. Those who dive into the waters closest to Myralune’s body fall asleep before reaching any significant depth and descend slowly, unconscious, until they come to rest on the seabed beside the leviathan, and no one knows whether any of them awaken. Where [[_wiki_en/panteao/elethvaen\|Elethvaen]] induces sleep as healing rest and [[_wiki_en/panteao/dremharak\|Dremharak]] uses it as a doorway into the minds of others, Myralune offers no utility: the sleep it radiates takes away wakefulness and gives back silence, and those who seek prophetic visions or dream healing seek another patron.
