# Solvara

Solvara, the Immortal Ash, is a deity of a demon-angel nature who manifests as an immolated phoenix. Her domain combines the schools of [[_wiki_en/escolas/plasma\|Plasma]] and [[_wiki_en/escolas/aero\|Aero]], while she is trapped in a cycle of immolation that never reaches completion.

### Manifestation

Solvara appears as a phoenix in perpetual combustion, enveloped in bluish-white plasma so intense that the surrounding air vibrates and distorts. The feathers, when visible through the waves of heat, are always at the exact instant between incandescence and ash: white at the root, golden through the middle, crimson at the ends, black at the tips that disintegrate into charged particles. The beak is molten metal that drips and continually reconstitutes itself, and the eyes emit their own light, with no discernible pupils. Solvara does not land: the wind generated by her own combustion sustains her in perpetual circular flight, as if stopping would extinguish her for good.

### Domain

Solvara’s combustion requires no external fuel. The plasma that envelops her is self-generated energy, ionized matter that melts whatever it touches without consuming anything beyond itself, while Aero feeds the cycle: the wind from her wings blows oxygen into the burning and carries incandescent ashes away, sowing ignition wherever they land. The trail is recognizable from a distance. The ground vitrifies beneath her passage, sand melted into smooth obsidian that reflects the sky like a black mirror. Vegetation does not burn: it sublimates, and trees disappear without leaving charcoal, only shadows imprinted on the glassy ground where they stood.

### The Immolation

The cycle of the classical phoenix is death and rebirth; Solvara has corrupted it. Immolation occurs, ashes accumulate, rebirth begins, but combustion reignites before the new form stabilizes. Solvara never finishes dying and never finishes being born. Her ashes are immortal in the most literal sense: each particle carries enough energy for spontaneous reignition. Solvara’s ash spirals upward against the air current and reignites in golden tips before going out again. Communities built on contaminated ground have learned to live with the cycle. Hearths light themselves on cold nights, puddles boil without warning, and smiths discover that furnaces fueled by Solvara’s ash melt metal with half the charcoal, although the resulting alloy is fragile and brittle, as if the borrowed heat exacted in the tempering what it gave in the smelting. No one prays to Solvara: they use what she left behind and pray that the next outbreak does not start beneath the granary. Lands through which the Immolated Phoenix has passed experience recurring fires for generations, outbreaks that spring from buried ashes no one remembers seeing fall.
