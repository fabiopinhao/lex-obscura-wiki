# Vraskilien

Vraskilien, the Shifting Viscera, is a [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/titas\|titan]] that manifests as a [[_wiki_en/criaturas/basilisco\|basilisk]] with translucent scales beneath which flesh ceaselessly reorganizes itself. Its domains are [[_wiki_en/escolas/morpho\|Morpho]], which transforms living tissue into new configurations, and [[_wiki_en/escolas/vita\|Vita]], which ensures that each mutation remains functional.

### Nature

Beneath Vraskilien’s scales, thin as wet parchment, organs migrate, bones bend into new geometries, and muscles merge and divide in real time. The heart beats in different positions with each cycle, the lungs branch and retract, and everything remains visible: veins that change course, tendons that reconnect, viscera that switch places as the basilisk breathes. The blood changes color as it flows between the scales’ junctions, red as it emerges, ocher as it drips, green-black as it dries. The eyes are two open wounds without eyelids, red down to the sclera, and whatever they stare at for too long begins to change: skin thickens into plates, fingers fuse, cartilage sprouts where there was none. The basilisk’s gaze does not petrify. It rewrites.

### Domain

Morpho rewrites living organic tissue, reconfiguring flesh and nerve, cartilage and fiber into arrangements that did not exist before the intervention. Vita sustains each reconfiguration at the threshold of viability, keeping blood circulating through veins that have just changed course and muscles obedient to nerve endings reconnected seconds ago. The combination produces biological plasticity without collapse. No transformation is permanent: each form is a transitory stage leading to the next, and the next has already begun before the current one is complete. To Vraskilien, a fixed form is a vulnerability, because what does not change can be mapped and slain, and stability is the condition that precedes extinction.

### The Viscera

The epithet describes method and legacy. Creatures in regions of prolonged influence from Vraskilien exhibit anatomy that defies cataloging: deer with functional external ribs, birds whose skeletons rearrange themselves in midflight, insects with redundant circulatory systems that make them almost impossible to kill. The mutations are viable, and each alteration serves a function that only reveals itself when the environment demands it. Hunters who encounter these creatures report that the visible interior still moves and adapts even after they are dead. Prolonged exposure does not spare humanoids: communities that inhabit zones of influence for generations report children born with extra joints, redundant organs, cartilage where bone should be rigid. The alterations are functional, never lethal in themselves, but no mother recognizes in the newborn the anatomy she expected. The game animals themselves shed their skin out of season, and the meat from the kill continues rearranging itself beneath the hunter’s knife, the blood changing color as it dries on the blade.
