# Paralda

Paralda, the Lady of the Shroud, is an [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/anjos\|angel]] of the pantheon whose divine form is the Veiled Mourner. Her domain combines the schools of [[_wiki_en/escolas/sacro\|Sacro]] and [[_wiki_en/escolas/salvo\|Salvo]]: her hands restore the dying through touch, or her shroud envelops and sanctifies the end. Her name appears among the nearly extinct sacred ones, mentioned in the papyri of the chapel of [[_wiki_en/locais/niedellin\|Niedellin]] alongside [[_wiki_en/panteao/azazel\|Azazel]] and [[_wiki_en/panteao/israfil\|Israfil]].

### Manifestation

Paralda appears as a humanoid figure wrapped in cloth. The cloth is heavy and gray, and its folds contract and loosen in a slow rhythm that varies from moment to moment, following the weakest heartbeat within range. When a heart stops, the cloth relaxes and hangs motionless for a moment before seeking the next pulse. Beneath the veil there is no face, only a wet surface that deforms into features when someone nearby is dying. Observers described the horror of recognizing, imprinted in the moisture beneath the veil, the face of someone who was still breathing a moment before, and those who touched the cloth reported the warm, moist texture of living skin, as though the cloth absorbed what the dying exhale at the very end.

The hands are the only exposed part, long and pale, with fingers that do not close. The finger joints are locked in perpetual extension, as though the tendons had been cut in a reaching position. They extend forward and to the sides, guided by a heat the living cannot perceive: the fever that precedes death, the cooling that confirms it.

### Domain

Salvo runs through the fingers and enters the body through the surface of the skin, in a tactile, localized restoration capable of closing a wound about to kill and restoring a heartbeat to a stopped heart. Sacro runs through the shroud and envelops the body from outside, consecrating the passage so that death is not a desecration. The two forces do not operate together: it is the touch of the hands or the embrace of the cloth. Where Israfil heals to return the body to the field, Paralda heals to keep the soul from crossing the threshold; Israfil restores the wounded, Paralda intercepts the dying. When the fingers touch flesh and the wound closes, the survivor wakes with the sensation of having been pulled back by something holding their ankles. When the shroud envelops and tightens, those who witness it describe the body relaxing as though it had been given permission to stop.

### The Shroud

In the final moments of someone dying in Paralda’s presence, the cloth detaches from the goddess’s body and spreads over the dying person like a sheet laid over a bed. The cloth touches the skin and clings, breathing slows, pain recedes: what the shroud covers rests. The fingers approach at the same time as the shroud, and at some point between the touch and the embrace Paralda decides. The survivors know they were chosen to live because they wake with pale finger marks where the hands reached them, impressions that take days to fade and tingle when the bearer approaches someone in danger of death. Those who depart know they have been consecrated because they know nothing more.

Even when no one sees the Mourner, those who keep vigil over a dying person report the same sign: the bedsheet smooths itself over the body, and the room takes on a sudden smell of clean linen, without any new sheet having been brought in.
