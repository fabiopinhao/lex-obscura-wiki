# Corvanthos

Corvanthos, the Judgment of the Zenith, is an angel who manifests as a lion of eroded limestone, of the kind that rests atop generals’ tombs in ancient imperial cemeteries. His domain combines the schools of [[_wiki_en/escolas/nomos\|Nomos]] and [[_wiki_en/escolas/helio\|Helio]]: he does not punish; he exposes and immobilizes.

### Manifestation

The mane is composed of tablets of law overlapping like scales, each inscribed in a different language, each inscription containing a specific prohibition. The tablets rearrange themselves with dry snaps when a law under his jurisdiction is violated within his reach, with a click of stone against stone that sounds like a lock’s latch. The body displays weathering cracks that never widen or close, and the eyes are deep cavities that emit two beams of cold white light, as narrow as observation slits in a wall. Where the light falls, shadow disappears completely, and bodies without shadows seem cut out of reality.

### Domain

The process is sequential and invariable. First, Helio falls upon the transgressor and makes the act visible: the light illuminates not the body, but the broken bond, the unfulfilled oath, the violated law. Under the lion’s beams, breached contracts materialize in the air as luminous text legible to third parties, and betrayed oaths are inscribed on the perjurer’s skin in handwriting he himself recognizes as his own. Then, Nomos applies the restriction: the transgressor loses the ability to repeat the act they committed. Whoever has stolen discovers that their hands will not close around another’s property; whoever has lied under oath discovers that their throat refuses to articulate the sentence they used to deceive. The restriction is permanent, and although Nomos recognizes redress, the criteria are so rigorous that most of the condemned bear the sentence until they die.

This use distinguishes Corvanthos from [[_wiki_en/panteao/abbhal\|Abbhal]]: where Abbhal employs Nomos as a diplomatic contract, with negotiated restrictions, Corvanthos applies it as a unilateral sentence, imposed by the court. His Helio, in turn, does not burn like that of [[_wiki_en/panteao/azazel\|Azazel]] or cauterize like that of [[_wiki_en/panteao/israfil\|Israfil]]: it illuminates like a forensic spotlight, making the transgression legible before immobilizing it.

### The Zenith

The Judgment of the Zenith was invoked at noon, when the sun occupies its highest point and shadows shrink to a minimum. The luminous legal tradition credited Corvanthos with establishing the principle that every judgment must take place in full light, and windowless courts were considered a profanation. The logic was literal: shadow allowed concealment, concealment allowed dishonest defense, and dishonest defense corrupted the verdict. The residual effect persists in popular expressions that survived the dark conquest without speakers remembering their origin. “To speak under the noonday sun” means to confess without omission; “to lose one’s shadow” means to be condemned without appeal. In [[_wiki_en/locais/myrgarde\|Myrgarde]], judges recited the formula “Under the Zenith, without shadow, without appeal” before pronouncing capital sentences, invoking Corvanthos without naming him. Even in an empty court, at the hour of judgment, the dry click of a stone latch can still be heard: the mane rearranging itself, a sentence without an audience.
