# Abbhal

Abbhal, the Profaned Grace, is a devil of angelic origin, the only one among the twelve whose fall began in the luminous pantheon. He manifests as a fallen angel: wings of blackened feathers with veins of faded white, eyes that emit pale light, and a torn priestly tunic that he wears as a shroud. The light from his eyes gives no warmth. It radiates the cold of an overcast day in an empty sanctuary, and every feature of his form evokes the sacred and belies it.

### The Fall

Abbhal did not choose to fall. When the humans who participated in the massacre of the [[_wiki_en/racas/luminares\|Luminars]] divided in the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/primeira_era\|First Era]], the division itself brought about his fall. Those who embraced guilt became [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]]; those who sought absolution became [[_wiki_en/racas/alvarar\|Álvarars]]. At that moment of schism, something also broke in the angelic sphere, and Abbhal, until then linked to the luminous pantheon, was dragged to the dark side by the collective weight of those who chose darkness. There was neither rebellion nor betrayal, only an ontological fracture that split a race and took an angel with it.

### Domain

His domain lies at the intersection of rule and sentence, shared between [[_wiki_en/escolas/nomos\|Nomos]] and [[_wiki_en/escolas/electro\|Electro]]. Nomos establishes the transgression: it identifies and seals. Electro responds with a discharge. The bond already contains the verdict, and mediation is superfluous: there is no tribunal under Abbhal, no defense or appeal. The rule is declared and lightning strikes. Where [[_wiki_en/panteao/corvanthos\|Corvanthos]] imposes a unilateral sentence and [[_wiki_en/panteao/gul_vakresh\|Gul’Vakresh]] binds and consumes, Abbhal declares and executes in the same gesture.

### Profaned Grace

The epithet is no metaphor. Abbhal carries real vestiges of his former nature: the angelic form, the remnants of light, the instinct to arbitrate. But what is clemency in the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/anjos\|angels]] is automatic condemnation in him. Grace was inverted without being extinguished: he still judges, still arbitrates, only he does not forgive. In the moments preceding the sentence, the surrounding light cools to the pale hue of an empty sanctuary, and hanging scales tilt to one side on their own; a white lightning bolt then falls upon the perjurer, without any thunder. When the sentence falls, witnesses describe a brief radiance reminiscent of a blessing, lasting as long as an inhalation.

### Role Among the Devils

Abbhal occupies a singular position on the Council of the Twelve. His angelic origin grants him a legitimacy the other [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/diabos\|devils]] do not possess: he is the only one who can invoke the language of order without it sounding like imposture. Internal arbitrations among devils pass through him, and pact-bound mortals who violate the terms of their pacts receive their sentence directly from him. No other devil challenges this prerogative, since challenging it would require offering an alternative arbiter, a burden no one on the Council desires.

### The Warrior Who Became a Judge

Before the fall, Abbhal was lord of battles, the angel whose just war was measured by lightning. The division transfigured him into the supreme judge, the God of Justice among the cultists, and the Electro that once opened combat came to seal sentences. This transition, from warrior to judge, is the very mold of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/justiciares\|Justiciars]], who reenact it at ordination, invoking Abbhal’s judgment. In its most extreme form, this judgment descends upon the condemned marked with the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/sigilo_de_abbhal\|Sigil of Abbhal]], the imperial tribunal’s maximum sentence, in which the devil’s projection seals the soul inside the living body.
