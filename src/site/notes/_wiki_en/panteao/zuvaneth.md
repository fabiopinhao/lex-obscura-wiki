# Zuvaneth

Zuvaneth, the Celestial Enigma, is a sidereal deity whose domains are [[_wiki_en/escolas/gnosi\|Gnosi]] and [[_wiki_en/escolas/urano\|Urano]]. She manifests as an oracular sphinx and answers questions by altering whoever asked them: the price of each revealed truth is the former identity of whoever receives it.

### Nature

Zuvaneth manifests as a sphinx whose body is an orrery of bone and stellar metal. The leonine form is suggested: forelegs made of concentric rings that rotate at different speeds, a torso of mechanical arms carrying spheres in orbits that cross without colliding. Condensation forms at the junctions between the rings, trickling down in slow rivulets and evaporating before touching the ground, as though the mechanism were breathing. The face is a concave disk of polished metal where features should be; no light returns from the surface, and the metal records the last question the observer asked themselves, transcribed in glyphs that rearrange themselves according to the viewing angle. The wings are unfolded diagrams, articulated flat surfaces that open and close like pages of a celestial atlas, displaying maps of constellations that no mortal astronomer has cataloged.

### Manifestation

A more subtle sign is also attributed to her: hairline fissures open in the floor, or appear as patterns among stars in the sky, and, if traced, spell out the question the observer has not yet formulated for themselves.

### Domain

Gnosi detects the gaps in the understanding of those who approach, often before the mortals themselves recognize them; Urano raises those who receive the answer to a cognitive level from which there is no return to the previous one. The mechanism is irreversible. Whoever processes a truth revealed by Zuvaneth loses access to the version of themselves that existed before processing it. The sphinx does not exact payment in pain or sanity: she exacts it in identity. Each answer replaces an earlier certainty with another that the recipient did not choose and cannot give back.

### The Riddle

The question appears on the facial disk as a sequence of glyphs comprehensible without the mediation of language, as though the statement existed in the mind before existing in the metal. Zuvaneth does not speak, and answering is optional: understanding the question is enough. The act of processing the statement dissolves convictions that supported earlier decisions and exposes the fragility of premises that seemed to form a foundation. Cartographers who sought the sphinx out of academic curiosity returned unable to trust their own scales; jurists abandoned courts upon concluding that the foundations of their codes were choices disguised as axioms. The solution is irrelevant. The riddle requires the recipient to accept that the structure of their thought has been edited by something that neither asked permission nor offered an option to refuse.
