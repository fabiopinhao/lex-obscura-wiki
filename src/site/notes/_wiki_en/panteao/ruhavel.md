# Ruhavel

Ruhavel, the Mirage of Return, is a deity of a sidereal-demonic nature that manifests as a fateful djinn. His domain combines the schools of [[_wiki_en/escolas/psycho\|Psycho]] and [[_wiki_en/escolas/chrono\|Chrono]]: the first invades the mind, and the second unanchors the perception of time.

### Manifestation

Ruhavel appears as unstable substance, made of silver smoke and dry heat that distorts vision. His face changes continually between versions of himself at different ages: young one moment, aged the next, childlike immediately afterward, mature again before the observer finishes blinking. His hands, when visible, repeat circular patterns already traced, as if each gesture were quoting an earlier gesture. His voice arrives before it is uttered or after it has already passed, never at the exact instant. Those who see him face-to-face feel they have seen him before; those who try to remember when discover that the memory belongs to a moment that has yet to happen.

### Domain

The psychic invasion locates the moments the target most wishes to revisit, and the temporal manipulation presents them as real, attainable possibilities, one pace away. The victim believes they can return to the choice they should have made, to the face they should have held. The belief is the hook: the harder the target strives to reach the offered moment, the more the present dissolves around them. Ruhavel consumes the now to feed the illusion of then.

### The Return

The Mirage of Return is the mechanism and the trap. Ruhavel projects into the target’s mind the reconstruction of a past moment, complete with smells and voices they recognize as authentic, and the target enters it voluntarily because it seems more real than the surrounding reality. Each attempt to remain costs perception of the present: first the peripheral details disappear, then contemporary faces lose definition, next the names become confused, and finally the target forgets where they were before Ruhavel arrived. The scene begins again unchanged, and they accept it again because the alternative is to admit that the return is impossible. The victims are found motionless, eyes open, breathing shallowly, trapped in a present that consists entirely of a past that recycles itself endlessly.

The phenomenon is not limited to a single victim: entire rooms have reported, at the same time, the certainty of having lived that exact instant before, a collective déjà vu that no one can locate in time. On hot roads, travelers describe glimpsing, amid the heat waves, the trembling silhouette of someone who departed years ago, recognizable for an instant before dissolving into the distorted air.
