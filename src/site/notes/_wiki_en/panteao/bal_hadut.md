# Bal’Hadut

Bal’Hadut, the Incandescent Throne, is a [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/demonios\|demon]] of the background pantheon who manifests as a volcanic dragon. With dominion over fire and war, he embodies destruction as an act of foundation: the conflagration that clears the way for a kingdom.

### Manifestation

Bal’Hadut appears as a dragon armored in living basalt, tectonic plates fitted over incandescent musculature that pulses between the fissures. Each heartbeat expels magma through the joints of the armor, and the smell of sulfur precedes the sight of him by hundreds of meters. The wings are membranes of solidified ash that reignite when they open, and the horns of volcanic obsidian, fused to the skull, split into veins of perpetual embers. Wherever Bal’Hadut lands, stone melts and hardens into the shape of a throne, and heat radiates in waves that distort the landscape in the background.

A subtler sign is also attributed to him: a stone outcrop is found at dawn melted and bent into the shape of a seat, the smell of sulfur hangs in the air without a visible source, and forges overheat on the eve of a battle.

### Dominion

Bal’Hadut’s dominion unites [[_wiki_en/escolas/pyro\|Pyro]], the elemental power of combustion that consumes everything in its path, and [[_wiki_en/escolas/belli\|Belli]], the doctrine of channeled aggression, pack instinct, and blood as fuel. From this combination arises the concept of incandescent sovereignty, in which the right to rule belongs to whoever burns strongest and longest. Where other demons destroy out of impulse or necessity, Bal’Hadut destroys as a foundational act: the charred battlefield is the first stone of the kingdom. Among the pure red demons, he is distinguished from [[_wiki_en/panteao/tzarakul\|Tzarakul]], who breaks things through entropy, by the intent he carries. Fire is not an end, it is a foundation.

### Worship

The title of Incandescent Throne carries literal weight. In the clandestine cults that invoke him, the consecration ritual requires the celebrant to kneel on heated stone until the flesh sizzles. The burns on the knees are a mark of submission and aspiration at the same time: whoever kneels before fire claims the right to impose it on others. Each scar of genuflection counts as a title of nobility in a pantheon that recognizes only strength as legitimacy. In the face of deities that destroy out of sheer appetite, Bal’Hadut demands that destruction serve a purpose, the foundation of a dominion that burns without going out.
