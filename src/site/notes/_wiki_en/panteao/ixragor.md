# Ixragor

Ixragor, the Abominable Womb, is a deity in the pantheon of Lex Obscura, classified as a demon. Combines the domains of [[_wiki_en/escolas/anatho\|Anatho]] and [[_wiki_en/escolas/morpho\|Morpho]], an intersection from which corrupted metamorphosis arises: impossible fusions and creatures that exist as permanent biological errors.

### Nature

Manifests as a chimera in perpetual decomposition, composed of parts of creatures that should never coexist. Reptilian limbs sprout from mammalian torsos. Insect wings hang from shoulder blades covered in fish scales. Lidless eyes open at joints, and jaws grow in the wrong places on the body. The flesh at the junctions oozes a thick, yellowish liquid that pulses with heat of its own, as though infection were the only tissue capable of holding the parts together. No configuration lasts: limbs are reabsorbed, rot, and are replaced by others equally incompatible. Ixragor is always becoming something else, and that something else is always wrong.

### Domain

Separately, Morpho and Anatho operate in opposite directions: one transforms with precision, the other degrades permanently. In Ixragor’s domain, transformation is degradation. The distinction from [[_wiki_en/panteao/vraskilien\|Vraskilien]] is structural: where Morpho and [[_wiki_en/escolas/vita\|Vita]] produce viable mutations that adapt to the environment, Morpho and Anatho produce mutations that accumulate error. Each new form carries the flaw of the one that preceded it, error upon error until the result defies any anatomical logic. The Abominable Womb generates life that functions despite itself: hearts that pump into vessels that terminate in dead ends, lungs that breathe fluids that corrode them from within. The creature survives because the curse prevents it from dying and the transformation does not stop long enough for the body to find equilibrium.

### The Gestation

The Womb of Ixragor produces, and each abomination is unique because the process combines living organisms into a single body under the simultaneous pressure of curse and transformation. The fusion is violent and irreversible. The resulting beings retain fragmentary memories of the original creatures, overlapping consciousnesses that contend for the same nervous system: some pursue prey that belongs to the instincts of incompatible species, others try to fly with limbs that once served to dig. Regions where these abominations establish themselves suffer residual contamination, and the flesh of animals they hunt begins to heal in the wrong configurations, with wounds that close with tissue from another species and bones that fuse at previously nonexistent angles. What the Womb gives birth to functions well enough to exist, and what exists contaminates what it touches. In the herds of these lands, double and malformed young are born, feathers sprouting where there should be skin, scales where there should be hide.
