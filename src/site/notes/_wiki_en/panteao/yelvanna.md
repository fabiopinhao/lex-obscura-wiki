# Yelvanna

Yelvanna, the Consecrated Requiem, is a deity of the angel category associated with the schools of [[_wiki_en/escolas/aegis\|Aegis]] and [[_wiki_en/escolas/sacro\|Sacro]]. She manifests as a misty siren of compacted salt and marine bone, and her domain inverts the principle of the wall: where other shields repel, her song attracts.

### Manifestation

Yelvanna’s figure is feminine, molded from salt and bone. The face exists beneath a layer of salt so thick that it resembles a death mask, and the mouth is a horizontal slit through which air passes, vibrating as it emerges. The body is hollow: the torso contains irregular cavities, each shaped by the death of someone who perished under the requiem’s protection, and the wind passing through them produces harmonics that the human ear processes as choral singing. The smaller ones correspond to children who did not survive consecrated childbirth, the larger ones to soldiers who fell within the barrier. A salty mist emanates from the form, depositing fine crystal on everything it touches, with a smell of low tide and ash from an extinguished censer.

A subtler sign is also attributed to her: on nights when a besieged wall holds, the besieged report hearing the requiem in the wind, harmonics of choral singing without any voice singing them, while a salty mist deposits fine crystal on stone and metal.

### Domain

Yelvanna’s protection operates in two layers. The requiem generates acoustic compulsion proportional to the listener’s hostility: charging soldiers feel their feet correcting their course toward the siren. Once inside the mist, Aegis does its work, as microscopic crystals settle in the joints and respiratory passages, and each second within the mist is slower than the previous one, until the invader sinks like an insect in amber. Sacro consecrates the space the mist occupies: the territory covered by the requiem becomes temporarily sacred, desecration cannot be sustained there, and demonic bonds burn. The effect lasts as long as the song lasts; when the last cavity stops resonating, the barrier evaporates and the salt dries on the ground like a tide mark.

### The Requiem

The Consecrated Requiem was performed over battlefields after combat. Each recent death within the perimeter hollowed out a new cavity in the salt torso, changing the timbre in real time, and approaching looters were drawn in and immobilized by the crystalline deposit before reaching the wounded. In times of peace, the requiem protected infirmaries and birthing rooms. Children born within the song emerged with a residue of fine salt on their skin that did not dissolve in water, and the mothers called the residue the “first shroud,” believing it protected against sudden death in the first months. The belief was never verified.
