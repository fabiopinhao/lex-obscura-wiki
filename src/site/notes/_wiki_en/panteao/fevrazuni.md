# Fevrazûni

Fevrazûni, the Lacerating Delirium, is a demon whose domain combines [[_wiki_en/escolas/psycho\|Psycho]], access to the mind through fixation, with [[_wiki_en/escolas/rupto\|Rupto]], the rupture that fractures cognition from the inside out. It turns delirium into a weapon, dissolving the bond between thought and meaning as others dissolve arcane bonds.

### Nature

It manifests as a lunatic succubus: a humanoid form with pale, luminescent skin, as though moonlight were trapped beneath the dermis. The body oscillates between solidity and fragmentation, with pieces of anatomy that shift and float out of position before regrouping into configurations that almost match the original form. The face concentrates the effect, with features that approach those of someone recognizable without ever settling, like a portrait of a loved one painted by trembling hands. The eyes are vertical slits through which cold, bluish light flows, and the fingers, too long, bend at extra joints unknown to human anatomy. When it communicates, the voice does not come from its mouth: it emerges from inside the listener’s skull, like an intrusive thought that was not summoned.

### Domain

Psycho provides the key, access to the target’s mind through a fixation: a desire or a memory, an unanswered question or a longing without an object, any thought the target cannot abandon. Fevrazûni enters through that point, infiltrating the crack like water under pressure. Rupto provides the destructive method: once inside, the cognitive architecture is fractured, memories dissociate from their contexts, and emotions detach from the events that caused them. The precision is surgical. Each fragment of cognition torn out by Rupto is replaced by a hallucination positioned by Psycho in the exact space of the fracture, and the resulting delirium is indistinguishable from genuine thought because it occupies the place that thought occupied.

### The Delirium

The Lacerating Delirium operates in cycles that mirror lunar phases. In the intervals between episodes, the target seems lucid: thinks clearly, recognizes faces, articulates coherent sentences. Lucidity is the trap. Each interval grows shorter while each successive episode penetrates deeper into the structure of the mind, until the target’s thought becomes an arena of contention between its own fragments and the demon’s intrusions. The hallucinations operate with the internal logic of dreams, absurd when examined from the outside and irrefutable when experienced from within. The target is delirious without knowing it, and believes itself lucid for the first time. The final cruelty is that the laceration feels like revelation: the target embraces the rupture because Psycho has wrapped it in certainty.

### Manifestation

Fevrazûni’s presence announces itself through subtle signs: a glow of moonlight that persists in a room long after the cloud covers the moon, the face of a loved one that appears in the mirror for a blink and is not there, a thought that arrives in a voice other than one’s own.
