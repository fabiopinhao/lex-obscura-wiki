# Faelyndrë

Faelyndrë, the Coral Cipher, is a deity of the sidereal pantheon that manifests as a Coral Nereid. It governs the schools of [[_wiki_en/escolas/hydro\|Hydro]] and [[_wiki_en/escolas/gnosi\|Gnosi]], and exists as a reef that processes information: it encodes knowledge in water and records it, permanently, in the growth of its own body.

### Nature

The nereid’s form exists only as a vestige, a humanoid silhouette fossilized at the center of the coral mass, visible when light passes through the structure at specific angles. The reef grows without interruption. Cerulean and chalk-colored polyps open and close in sequences that vary according to the water’s chemical composition. Some record salinity and temperature, others capture traces of dissolved [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/magi\|magi]] or emotional residues from creatures that passed through the current. The internal silhouette never moves. The eyes, if they were ever eyes, are cavities filled with crystallized aragonite that refracts light into spectra mortal vision cannot resolve. The mass moves along the seabed at an imperceptible speed, dragging itself over the rocky substrate in a migration that takes decades to cover the distance across a field.

### Domain

Faelyndrë encodes information in water and decodes it through growth. Ocean currents carry data like sediment: each variation in flow, temperature, or pressure constitutes a character in a language only the reef interprets. The filtered knowledge becomes patterns of calcification, permanent records inscribed in the body’s structure. The cipher is the reef. Millennia of growth have accumulated overlapping layers of information, legible to anyone who knows how to cut the correct cross section, like tree rings that recorded events instead of seasons. Mortals who consult it receive answers in the form of subtle changes in the local current: a change in temperature can encode a number, a whirlpool can trace an outline, a shift in the current can indicate direction, and crystallized salt can replicate the map of a place the inquirer needed to find.

### The Cipher

Each accepted inquiry is inscribed as a layer of new coral, added to the mass over a period ranging from days to months according to the complexity of the knowledge requested. The deposited information remains legible for centuries, protected by the hardness of the calcareous skeleton. Fragments torn away by storms or collected by divers retain the information from the corresponding layer, and scholars describe the result as a library distributed across the ocean floor, each piece containing the isolated record of something someone wanted to know. This distribution carries a risk that many scholars underestimate. Isolated fragments contain answers without context. Divers who reconstructed information from pieces separated by kilometers of seabed obtained conclusions that were mutually consistent and factually inverted. The coral cipher does not lie: fragments answer with the same precision as the entire reef, applied to a fraction of the problem. Partial precision is more dangerous than complete ignorance.

On the beaches nearest the reef, the tide leaves smaller traces of the same writing: foam and shells are deposited on the sand in repeating patterns, legible as fragments of cipher to those who know how to recognize the markings. Low-tide pools hold, for an instant before settling, the reflection of the face of whoever looked into them the day before.
