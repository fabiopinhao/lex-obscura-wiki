# Templars

Templar designated the leadership stratum of the temples of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/culto_sombrio\|Shadow Cult]], rather than the entire body: there was a hierarchy among the Templars themselves, and the lower clergy, who served the temple without leading it, did not bear the name. Within this stratum were the Templar knights, the martial branch charged with protecting the temples. Today, a Templar is a [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadar]] or [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Drul]] who belongs to a temple, and the name extends to necromancers, clerics, and [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/ophideias\|ophideias]]; the temples answer to the monarch and operate in parallel with the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/conselho_sombrio\|Shadow Council]], without requiring its approval. The old order survives as a hierophantic remnant, without the martial branch that once distinguished it.

### Origin and Function

Each Templar maintained an individual pact with a patron devil, which granted considerable magical power and, at the same time, divided loyalty: the Templar served the Cult as an institution and obeyed the demands of their own god. The order predates [[_wiki_en/personagens/arothistar\|Arothistar]] and arose from [[_wiki/personagens/xarn\|Xarn]]’s unification in the [[_wiki/conceitos/segunda_era\|Second Era]], when scattered temples began to need coordinated armed protection. The Templar knights were the military-religious elite of the plateau, heirs to martial traditions that combined physical combat and diabolical invocation, and figures of comparable importance to the shadow lords. Not every Templar was a martial member, but every martial member was a Templar. For [[_wiki_en/personagens/orum-rahru\|Orum]], who was one of them, it is this branch that bears the full name: the Templar knights stayed out of external conquests and protected the Cult from within, as police and a special guard, subordinate to the temples and the hierophants.

### Characteristics

The order’s culture of secrecy was inherited from the Drul matriarchs: through the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/alianca_subterranea\|Underground Alliance]] between Shadars and Druls, the Templars absorbed operational secrecy, complex ritual practices, and deference to hidden hierarchies. They did not speak of their pacts or their orders, a culture that made them efficient as agents and dangerous as subordinates. Unlike the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/justiciares\|Justiciars]], who practice [[_wiki_en/magia/teru\|Teru]] without a pact, the Templars answered to two masters, the Cult and the devil: when both were aligned, their effectiveness was devastating; when they diverged, the Templar became an involuntary double agent. They also possessed their own jurisdiction in the temples and surrounding areas, investigating heresies, protecting relics, and executing religious traitors, an autonomy that rivaled the power of the monarch and the regular armed forces.

### Decline and Military Extinction

Forty-four years ago, Arothistar founded the Justiciars as a calculated replacement, in a political purge disguised as institutionalization. The Templars posed a threat on several fronts: a deep connection with the Druls through the cults of [[_wiki/locais/vor-ghol\|Vor-Ghol]], operational autonomy that rivaled the monarch, pact ties that could contradict imperial orders, and a culture of secrecy that made it impossible to know whom they actually worked for. Arothistar needed judge-enforcers who answered only to the [[_wiki/conceitos/verbum_arkhon_codex\|Verbum Arkhon]], without divine pacts, without ties to matriarchs, and without a tradition of shared secrecy.

By the monarch’s decision, no martial branch dedicated to temples may exist: the entire defense structure was unified under the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|Empire]]’s military, beneath the monarch himself, a deliberate concentration to prevent Templar revolts. In practice, some temples maintain informal militias, without institutional recognition. In the past, the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/serpente_purpura\|Purple Serpent]] was linked to some temples of the Cult. The martial sense of the term fell out of use, and with it the restriction to a stratum. At first “Templar” survived only for ophideias, sorcerers, and necromancers of the temples; over time, everyday usage extended it to any member who works for a temple, including clerics and hierophants, a hierophantic body rather than an armed force, reaching the lower clergy, who had not previously been called that. The name moved down the hierarchy.

### Survival

Fifty or more remaining members stayed active as subordinate clerics, without formal military power, but the ideological identity persisted. A “Templar” faction of the Shadow Council champions the return of the Templars and the decentralization of power as it existed before Arothistar. The core, however, wants to restore the principle the order embodied: religious supremacy over monarchical authority, with an end to the pairing of Verbum Arkhon and [[_wiki/conceitos/lex_obscura_decreto\|Lex Obscura]] so that only the Lex remains, a cause the [[_wiki/organizacoes/ordo_zathar\|Ordo Zathar]] carries forward.

Orum-Rahru embodies the tension between the two orders. The only Templar to become a Justiciar, he was chosen as the first of them in a decision that honored the Templar tradition while subordinating it to the new system. His silver Templar armor, preserved since the pre-imperial era and rarely removed, is a physical symbol of this divided loyalty.

### Legacy

The Templar legacy persists in three forms. In ritual practices, anointing ceremonies, ritual duels, and consecration formulas survive in the temples, and the anointing of the Justiciars in the [[_wiki/locais/templo_de_azkaroth\|Temple of Azkaroth]] preserves elements of the original Templar ceremony. In weaponry, Templar bastard swords, with long grips and ceremonial designs, became relics, as did Orum’s enchanted armor, which worked even against the undead. In ideology, the doctrine of religious primacy survives, and the difference lies in the method: the old Templars imposed it by the sword, the new ones impose it through institutional infiltration.
