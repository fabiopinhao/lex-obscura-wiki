# Tradition of Sirana

The Tradition of Sirana is the contemplative discipline through which [[_wiki_en/racas/nalvirin\|Nalvirins]] are trained in the practice of [[_wiki_en/magia/meditatio_aetheris\|Meditatio Aetheris]]. It is neither a hierarchical order nor an institution with a single headquarters: it is a pedagogical discipline widespread throughout the [[_wiki_en/racas/frenim\|Frenim]] citadels of [[_wiki_en/locais/neranthis\|Neranthis]], passed from masters to apprentices through continuous practice and oral instruction.

### Nature and Function

The reason for its existence is simple and painful: Nalvirins are not natural contemplatives. Gnomish nature is restless, made of curiosity, hands that need occupation, and a mind that wanders, while Meditatio Aetheris demands the opposite, namely, decades of discipline, mental stillness, and prolonged contemplation. Where a Frenim meditates as naturally as breathing, a Nalvirin meditates with the concentration of someone holding their breath. Sirana was the first Nalvirin to systematize this tension: instead of imitating Frenim contemplation, she formulated a discipline specifically for restlessness, with progressive techniques that accept the Nalvirin mind as a restless instrument and train it through effort, not nature.

### Doctrine

The doctrine is condensed into an aphorism attributed to Sirana herself: the mind is a restless instrument that, through effort, becomes a lens. Three principles derive from it. The first is that restlessness is not a flaw to eliminate, but raw material to polish, channeled into successively finer focuses until it becomes contemplative precision. The second is that Nalvirin contemplation is made, not given: built through accumulated acts of discipline, calloused knees, posture maintained for hours, repetition until it becomes automatic. The third is that the lens is an instrument, not a state, because the goal is not to achieve mystical transcendence: it is to become useful in the arcane service of the citadels. Sirana is a pragmatic tradition.

### Structure and Transmission

The tradition has no formal hierarchy or titles like those of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/justiciares\|Justiciars]] or the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/ophideias\|ophideias]]. Transmission occurs through the relationship between master and apprentice within the citadels: a Nalvirin elder takes one or more novices under their tutelage, and the apprenticeship lasts decades, considered complete only when the apprentice begins teaching others. From it emerge roles such as ritual assistant, ethereal instrument technician, phenomenon cataloger, novice meditator, and Nalvirin elder. There are no formal ranks among them; they are specializations that arise according to the trainee’s aptitude.

### Relationship with the Frenims

The tradition exists within the Frenim citadels, with tolerance and sometimes support from the Frenims themselves, without a relationship of subordination. The Frenims recognize that Nalvirins, although they do not advance as far in Meditatio Aetheris, perform complementary roles essential to the functioning of the citadels. The Frenims do not practice Sirana: they meditate by their own nature, which is distinct. Sirana is exclusively Nalvirin, formulated by Nalvirins and for Nalvirins, within Frenim territory. As Nalvirins have never set foot outside Neranthis, the tradition is unknown in [[_wiki_en/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]] and [[_wiki_en/locais/veinkard\|Veinkard]], and exists entirely outside the imperial canonical horizon.

### The Figure of Sirana

Little is known about the historical or legendary figure of Sirana, and versions vary among citadels: for some she was the first Nalvirin to arrive in Neranthis in ancient ages, for others an elder who systematized the discipline centuries after the migration. What remains stable is the symbolic function: Sirana is the name of the Nalvirin effort to become contemplative despite gnomish nature. No standardized iconographic representation exists, and different citadels portray her in different ways, old or young, with eyes closed or open. The lack of definition is treated as a characteristic of the tradition, not as an absence to correct.
