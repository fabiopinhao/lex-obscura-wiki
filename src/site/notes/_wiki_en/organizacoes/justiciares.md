# Justiciars

The Justiciars are the armed magistracy of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|Shadow Empire]]: judge-enforcers who answer to the law of [[_wiki_en/personagens/arothistar\|Arothistar]] and the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/conselho_sombrio\|Shadow Council]] of [[_wiki_en/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]]. The name derives from the ancient [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadar]] *justis-kar*, “bearer of the sentence,” and reflects the office’s dual nature: they are **judges** when they preside over trials and interpret the [[_wiki_en/artefatos/codex_somniorum\|Codex Somniorum]], and **enforcers** when they investigate, carry out sentences, or enforce martial law. They are magistrates and executioners at the same time. They are also called the “Blade of Aroth”: *Aroth*, “sacred” or “divine” in archaic Shadar, is the root of Arothistar’s own name, and the title frames them as living weapons of the law, the demigod’s sharp instruments.

### Origin

The system was founded forty-four years ago, during the Reinkardic Wars, as both a legal reform and a political purge. The former [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/templarios\|Templars]] maintained dangerous allegiances: ties to [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/diabos\|devils]] and religious bonds with the [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Drul]] matriarchs of [[_wiki_en/locais/vor-ghol\|Vor-Ghol]], who rivaled the monarch’s authority. Arothistar replaced them with judge-enforcers without pacts, exclusively Shadars of pure lineage, who operate through [[_wiki_en/magia/teru\|Teru]], magic independent of divine patrons. As a military institution, the Templars were abolished; at first, the term survived only for the temples’ subordinate hierophants.

The reform also carried an explicit racial conviction of Arothistar’s: Shadars were held to be humans suited to centralized order; Druls, elves bound to matriarchal traditions and autonomous allegiances. The Justiciars were conceived to remove that influence from imperial administration.

### Ordination

No birth guarantees the black mantle. Selection demands excellence in three pillars: martial and arcane mastery, legal scholarship, and devotion to the Cult, with a preference for war veterans. The anointing takes place in the [[_wiki_en/locais/templo_de_azkaroth\|Temple of Azkaroth]], where the candidate kneels before the mural of Arothistar. In the rite’s central gesture, the officiant lays the consecrated sword across the candidate’s open palms, with its edge facing the candidate: the law is received by supporting it, without the hand grasping it by the hilt. The candidate then receives three symbols of authority: the [[_wiki_en/artefatos/spina_lexis\|Spina Lexis]], scepter of the sentence; the Codex Somniorum, a tripartite grimoire; and the [[_wiki_en/artefatos/oculus_morvgradi\|Oculus]], a communication sphere. At the end of the rite, the anointing formula is pronounced, *Arothistar est lex. Abbhal est vox. Tu es martellus.*, and the assembly responds in one voice with the antiphon: *We embrace guilt. We do not seek absolution.* The ceremonial toga is worn only at some of Morvgrad’s most important Templar ceremonies.

The order’s [[_wiki_en/artefatos/tegmen\|tegmen]] is issued as part of its equipment: standardized in cut, it serves as a visible credential, and there is no separate insignia identifying a Justiciar.

### Powers

They operate with almost absolute autonomy within the Cult: they investigate any member regardless of rank, override local authorities, requisition military resources, and may pass sentence in the field. The power of life and death has a limit, however: the Justiciar may interrogate, torture, and threaten the prisoners of the Reinkardic Wars, but may not decide their fate, because the death of these captives is determined collectively and never at the will of one alone. The promise to grant or deny death, common in interrogations, is rhetorical leverage, not a prerogative. Their magical emphasis falls on [[_wiki_en/escolas/necro\|Necro]] (extracting truths from the dead and dying), [[_wiki_en/escolas/nomos\|Nomos]] (containment), and [[_wiki_en/escolas/anatho\|Anatho]] (punitive curses). The inquisitorial protocol runs from the accusation received by the Council to the report encoded in the [[_wiki_en/artefatos/tabua_reversa\|Reverse Tablet]]. The [[_wiki_en/conceitos/veredictum_exkarthon\|Veredictum Exkarthon]] is one of the few procedural protections: it allows the person under investigation to demand evidence before sentencing. Among their interrogation instruments is tonic salt, a caustic mixture of mineral salts and ironroot extract created during the Reinkardic Wars to revive unconscious soldiers; Shadar executioners refined it for inquisitorial use, as it prevents the victim from losing consciousness under torture.

### Subordination and Structure

A Justiciar’s sole subordination is to the collective interests of Morvgrad, the axis formed by Arothistar and the Shadow Council; none answers to a temple or a particular devil, and a Justiciar’s hierarchical rank is equal to or higher than that of a temple leader. This framework sustains the apparent paradox of their serving the Council while still being able to judge corrupt Councilors when the interests of Arothistar and Morvgrad demand it. When the Archons of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/tribunal_de_morvgrad\|Tribunal of Morvgrad]] sit in judgment, the role is reversed: they are the judges, and the Justiciar acts as prosecutor, legal representative, or, depending on the case, defense attorney. Among the Justiciars themselves, there is no formal hierarchy: length of service and reputation create tacit distinctions, and they operate alone or in small groups, sometimes accompanied by specialized assistants, such as [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/ophideias\|ophideias]] who add hierophantic espionage to execution.

### The Seven in Service

Twenty-seven Justiciars have been ordained in the forty-four years since the founding, and seven currently serve. Mortality is a consequence of the occupation: the order operates in every province, in hostile territory, on missions that seldom forgive error.

[[_wiki_en/personagens/orum-rahru\|Orum-Rahru]], the first and oldest, serves as dean in Morvgrad. [[_wiki_en/personagens/draven-thol\|Draven-Thol]] is responsible for [[_wiki_en/locais/thandruk_varn\|Thandruk Varn]], from which no one has had news for a few moons. [[_wiki_en/personagens/belev-hesnek\|Belev-Hesnek]] investigates, in the strait between the [[_wiki_en/locais/dorsal\|Dorsal]] and [[_wiki_en/locais/thal_serin\|Thal’Serin]], the officers who sell passage to the tribes descending before the ice. [[_wiki_en/personagens/milorsevogh_velshard\|Milorsevogh Velshard]], the most recent to receive the mantle, took his first case in the [[_wiki_en/locais/ventrista\|Ventrista]]. [[_wiki_en/personagens/nidzina_avaroth\|Nidzina Avaroth]] operates between the capital and the underground. [[_wiki_en/personagens/tashkur\|Tashkur]] is responsible for the eastern border, operating from [[_wiki_en/locais/qadesh\|Qadesh]], where cargo crosses the gates with an imperial seal and laundered provenance. [[_wiki_en/personagens/sarquin\|Sarquin]] remains bound to the Tribunal’s public docket.

### The Weight of the Office

The mantra sums up their doctrine: *Glory does not move my hand. Order demands blood.* Conceived by Arothistar at the order’s founding, it was canonized only at the beginning of the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/terceira_era\|Third Era]]; the second half, *order demands blood*, circulates on its own as the monarch’s ritual utterance. They see themselves as surgeons of the social order, the only ones capable of judging even lords and generals. This claim generates constant friction with the military arm. The office takes its toll: they live in isolation, without ties that compromise judgment, and many develop extreme philosophies to bear the weight of executions: Orum in the certainty of the law, Nidzina in the ambition for excellence, Milor in military discipline.
