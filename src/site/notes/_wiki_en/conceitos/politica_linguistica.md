# Language Policy of the Shadow Empire

The language policy of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|Shadow Empire]] treats language as an instrument of sovereignty. The imposition of [[_wiki_en/linguas/reinkardico\|Reinkardic]] is no mere administrative convenience, but a founding act: the sovereign is whoever decides in which language the exception is declared. The [[_wiki_en/conceitos/lex_obscura_decreto\|Lex Obscura]], written by [[_wiki_en/personagens/xarn\|Xarn]] in the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/segunda_era\|Second Era]], already formalizes Reinkardic as the language of government, and the decrees of [[_wiki_en/personagens/arothistar\|Arothistar]] in the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/terceira_era\|Third Era]] radicalize this logic into three layers of control.

### The Three Layers

The administrative layer requires all official documentation, from contracts to military orders, to be written in Reinkardic. Documents in other languages have no legal validity: a [[_wiki_en/racas/gerut\|Gerut]] who records an agreement in his tribal dialect discovers that, to the Empire, the document does not exist.

The ritual layer preserves [[_wiki_en/linguas/tenebraico\|Tenebraic]] and [[_wiki_en/linguas/gholvyss\|Gholvyss]] as the languages of diabolical contracts. Pacts operate in these languages because [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/diabos\|devils]] demand contracts in their own terms: the devils write, the mortal signs, and anyone who has not mastered the language signs without knowing what they are accepting. Mortals capable of drafting their own contracts are exceedingly rare, [[_wiki_en/personagens/jaakurs\|Jaakurs]] and Xarn himself among the known cases, and Tenebraic and Gholvyss are the only official exceptions to linguistic hegemony.

The forbidden layer criminalizes or marginalizes the public use of certain languages. [[_wiki_en/linguas/orcanico\|Orcanic]] outside the military context sounds like insubordination, [[_wiki_en/linguas/draconico\|Draconic]] in the mouths of [[_wiki_en/racas/varnakiano\|Varnakians]] arouses suspicion, and [[_wiki_en/linguas/sylvaen\|Sylvaen]] spoken before imperial officers is treated as disrespect. Gholvyss is a particular case: tolerated for magical use even in [[_wiki_en/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]] by virtue of the alliance with the Matriarchal Houses, but suspect in everyday life on the surface.

### Multilingualism as a Threat

To the imperial state apparatus, a multilingual citizen is a divided citizen, and divided loyalty amounts to disloyalty. By this logic, the most dangerous individuals in [[_wiki_en/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]] are not the powerful, but the polyglots. The [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/ophideias\|Ophideias]], hierophant-spies fluent in Gholvyss, Tenebraic, and Reinkardic, are the exception that proves the rule: their competence is tolerated because it operates in the service of power. The [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/justiciares\|Justiciars]] occupy a peculiar position, since the [[_wiki_en/magia/teru\|Teru]] system requires the technical study of ritual phrases from various schools without a pact. A Justiciar such as [[_wiki_en/personagens/milorsevogh_velshard\|Milor]] utters spells in Tenebraic through arcane study, not devotion, in a fragmented multilingualism that is the only truly safe kind in the Empire.

### Clandestine Languages

Where there is prohibition, there is clandestinity. The Sylvan resistance is the most visible: [[_wiki_en/locais/thal_serin\|Thal’Serin]] maintains Sylvaen as its sole language of internal communication, and [[_wiki_en/personagens/peredur\|Peredur]] conducts rituals in the magical register. The underground resistance of the [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Druls]] operates in silence, since Gholvyss has never been formally proscribed, but its use on the surface, as in the invocations of [[_wiki_en/personagens/syndrith_morghast\|Syndrith]] in [[_wiki_en/locais/niedellin\|Niedellin]], carries risk. The Orcanic resistance is the most diffuse: [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orcs]] preserve their language not out of political conviction, but because it is the language in which they dream, fight, and pray, and the [[_wiki_en/personagens/grasnak\|Lays of Grasnak]], oral epics such as the defense of Khaz-Ankor, survive because no one has ever translated them. The Elarin resistance is silent and proud: in [[_wiki_en/locais/elenir\|Elenir]], the [[_wiki_en/racas/elarino\|Elarins]] speak Reinkardic under surveillance, but among themselves they preserve [[_wiki_en/linguas/aelarian\|Aelarian]], a memory of [[_wiki_en/linguas/luminar\|Luminar]] that the Empire cannot confiscate.

### The Paradoxes of Tenebraic and Gholvyss

Tenebraic is at once the sacred language of power and the language that no ordinary citizen should master. Hierophants learn fragments of liturgy and the pact-bound memorize clauses, but true fluency, the ability to compose original sentences, is the prerogative of very few. Scarcity is functional: as long as Tenebraic operates as a semi-dead language, known in fragments and never in its entirety, mortals do not fully understand the contracts they sign. The rare mortals who achieve complete fluency are watched not for heresy, but for competence, since those who read contracts without mediation can reject clauses, and those who write them negotiate as equals.

Gholvyss embodies the inverse paradox: it is the primordial language that all Druls master and that the Empire tolerates out of political necessity. The [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadar]]-Drul alliance, devised by Xarn in the Second Era, required mutual concessions. The Druls adopted Reinkardic as a second language in which they are fluent, in a state of real bilingualism, without relinquishing Gholvyss as their language of identity, magic, and domestic life. The result is a dual linguistic zone, in which Gholvyss reigns in the internal life of the Matriarchal Houses and Reinkardic serves as the interface with the Empire.
