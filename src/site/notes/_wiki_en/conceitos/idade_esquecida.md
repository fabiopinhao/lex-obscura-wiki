# Forgotten Age

The Forgotten Age is the long period before the reckoning of the eras, when [[_wiki_en/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]] was the domain of the [[_wiki_en/racas/luminares\|Luminars]]. Elves of light and possessors of the continent’s oldest magic, the Luminars lived in harmony with the cycles of the earth. Their supremacy was arcane and cultural, not territorial: they were neither conquerors nor imperial rulers, and for eras nothing disturbed the balance they maintained. The era does not end on a precise date, but with the disappearance of the Luminars as a unified people.

### The Luminars and the Abundance of Magi

The Luminars adapted the regions they inhabited to their own liking, whether ponds, hills, plains, or woods, and the surrounding land became infused with [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/magi\|magi]] over centuries of arcane presence. For this reason, even today, some Luminar ruins retain magi strong enough for the [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]] to erect artificial nexuses on their foundations.

In the Forgotten Age, the white, green, and blue entities exerted far greater influence over the material plane than in the present day. Magi was abundant, the earth responded generously to prayers and rituals, and the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/titas\|titans]] walked among the forests with a frequency inconceivable today. This abundance began to decline after the extinction of the Luminars and diminishes slowly and continuously with each era. In return, [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/diabos\|devils]] and [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/demonios\|demons]], whose influence is more direct and targeted than that of the gods of light, encounter ever less resistance to acting in the world.

### The Internal Schisms

The Luminars were not a homogeneous race. Over eras, internal factions diverged in belief and ambition, and each schism produced a lineage that would never return.

The first to break away descended to [[_wiki_en/locais/vor-ghol\|Vor-Ghol]], drawn by the depths. They carried fragments of the Luminar language and culture, which they corrupted in the caverns until they forged something new, and established dominion over older subterranean races. They became the proto-Druls, and it was with them, not with humans, that the devils opened their first channels. Among those who descended, further friction arose: those who had made pacts with devils remained in the depths and ultimately gave rise to the [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Druls]], while those bonded to demons, whose chaotic nature was incompatible with subterranean discipline, abandoned Vor-Ghol and migrated to tundras, glaciers, and mountains on the surface, where the brutality of the environment shaped their bodies and culture. They gave rise to the [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orcs]].

Another faction followed the opposite course. It migrated east and inhabited coasts and wetlands, isolating itself and seeking transcendence. Through meditation and contemplation of the celestial cycles, its members became more ephemeral and less bound to the earth, and the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/siderais\|blue gods]] guided them toward an ever more ethereal existence. They gave rise to the [[_wiki_en/racas/undine\|undines]].

### Humans and the Other Races

Humans existed on the margins of this civilization: scattered tribes, short-lived and restlessly ambitious, occupying the fertile central plains, where they grew with each generation. Their expansion soon pressed against the elven borders, and the Luminars erected deterrent barriers, seeing the newcomers as a crude and dangerously fickle species. The xenophobia was reciprocal, though asymmetrical: humans admired elven magic as much as they feared it.

Dragons and [[_wiki_en/racas/draconideo\|Draconids]] inhabited the western mountain ranges and volcanic peaks, in Saurian tribes more united than they are today. To the Luminars, both represented the same threat, aligned with the entities of destruction: the reptiles had served the red gods since time immemorial. To fight them, Luminar artificers forged artifacts of war with ancient magi, deliberately shaped to reject hands corrupted by diabolical pacts or demonic bonds. None was more feared than [[_wiki_en/artefatos/fendris\|Fendris]], a dagger capable of wounding any draconic creature, which answered only to hands bound by no pact. The conflict culminated in the great arcane barriers: the Luminars sealed the mountain ranges around the plateau and the plains, infusing the rock with magi of such intensity that the dragons could not cross them. Even after the fragmentation of the Luminars, these barriers would remain intact for eras.

The dwarves had inhabited the interiors of the mountains since times as ancient as the Luminars themselves, isolationist and devoted to stone and the forge. The contempt between the two races was mutual and cordial: neither had any interest in the other. Raided by Saurians throughout the era, the surviving clans learned to defend themselves with unparalleled ingenuity, false tunnels, rock trapdoors, and passages that collapsed on invaders, making their citadels impenetrable. The gnomes lived in small, scattered communities, tolerated by the Luminars and underestimated by the other races. Clever and possessed of a strong sense of self-preservation, they were born survivors, and learned early that human kindness was as unpredictable as human cruelty.

Among humans themselves, a minority rejected conquest and chose to live in harmony with the earth. Tolerated by the Luminars and permitted to inhabit the edges of the forests, these groups entered the woods and never emerged, recognized as the [[_wiki_en/racas/gerut\|Gerut]], a lineage marked by an affinity with nature and a rejection of territorial ambition.

### The Sancta Faith and the Álvarars

Unlike the elves, whose arcane affinity was innate, humans had no natural connection to magi. They found intermediaries in the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/anjos\|angels]], the white gods, who identified most closely with humanity through an affinity of temperament: where the titans accepted the cycle without moralizing, the angels demanded purity and saw imperfection as corruption to be excised. Thus arose the magical system called Sancta, founded on devotion to celestial entities and the channeling of divine power through prayers, dogmas, and rituals. The angels granted power through faith, not through contract like the devils or through force like the demons: the more genuine the devotion, the more generous the response.

Among the most devout practitioners arose the [[_wiki_en/racas/alvarar\|Álvarars]], who claimed descent from the angels and embodied celestial gifts. They were not only warriors, but moralizers: they corrected immorality, punished transgression, and imposed a code wherever they saw disorder. Where preaching failed, the sword purified. The central kingdoms, humanity’s religious hearts, became the dominant centers of the plains, and the largest rose where [[_wiki_en/locais/myrgarde\|Myrgarde]] now rests. Within this faith arose irreconcilable theological disputes between those who advocated coexistence with the elves and those who preached human supremacy as a sacred destiny; the most militarized arm eventually dominated the high clergy.

### The Racial War and the Fragmentation

The military consolidation of the central kingdoms changed the balance of the continent. The orcs, who plundered the borders with impunity, were pushed back to the peripheries in the face of organized armies. With them contained, the kingdoms turned their eyes toward a more ambitious target. Expansion eventually crossed the elven borders: the Luminars’ tolerance ended when armed expeditions began invading their domains in search of artifacts and knowledge, and they banished the intruders with magical force. The banishment enraged and instructed the human kings, who understood the gulf between their military strength and the arcane power they coveted. A race for magic ensued, and religion became an instrument of mobilization: the angels blessed the crusade, the Álvarars wielded sacred standards, and what had been a theological dispute became an expansionist program.

The enmity escalated into a racial war of continental proportions. No human kingdom was strong enough on its own, but shared greed produced previously unthinkable alliances, even as each king marched thinking of what he would take for himself. The combined mass of the armies, fueled by the Sancta faith and led by increasingly powerful Álvarars, imposed a continental siege that the Luminars, outnumbered and rigid in their traditions, could not withstand. The war lasted generations and ended through attrition: citadel after citadel, the elves of light abandoned their positions.

The race was not extinguished, but fragmented. Some sought refuge in the deep forests, under the protection of the titans, adapted to the greenery, and embraced the cycles of nature, giving rise to the [[_wiki_en/racas/sylvano\|Sylvans]]. Others settled in hidden citadels, protected by ancient enchantments, preserving what they could of Luminar culture and remaining closest to the original tradition; they gave rise to the [[_wiki_en/racas/elarino\|Elarins]], whose most famous refuge would become [[_wiki_en/locais/elenir\|Elenir]]. What remained were enchanted ruins, poorly understood artifacts, two elven lineages that would never reconcile, and a continent dominated by a young, ambitious, and divided race.

### The Birth of the Shadow

The moral rift opened by the Álvarars did not heal with victory. Among the humans who had participated in the massacre of the Luminars, some rejected the absolution the faith offered and embraced the guilt of their own deed; frustrated with orthodoxy and suffocated by moral surveillance, they broke with the church and sought profane pacts. They were the first to hear the whispers of devils and fallen angels, entities that offered power without demanding purity, only obedience, and became the first Shadars. The shadow did not arise spontaneously: it was the response to an excess of light. The devils, long contained in Vor-Ghol and who until then had the Druls as their principal channels in the material world, saw an unexpected opportunity in this human generation, and the alliance between ambitious souls and the lords of darkness would forever change the balance of Reinkard.
