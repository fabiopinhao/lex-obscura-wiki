# Sigil of Abbhal

The Sigil of [[_wiki_en/panteao/abbhal\|Abbhal]], also called the Mark of Abbhal, is the ultimate sentence in the judicial system of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|Shadow Empire]]: the eternal imprisonment of the soul within a living body. Reserved for traitors at the highest level, it marks the point at which imperial justice gives way to divine justice. The name derives from Abbhal, the Blind Judge, a devil with a diplomatic and judicial role in the shadow pantheon. According to official doctrine, [[_wiki_en/personagens/arothistar\|Arothistar]] is the only mortal capable of negotiating terms with the god.

### Nature

In the hierarchy of punishments of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/tribunal_de_morvgrad\|Tribunal of Morvgrad]], the Sigil ranks above the Crucitórios and any conventional execution. It does not kill: it condemns one to existence without agency. The body breathes and the heart beats, but consciousness remains trapped in a limbo, with glassy eyes and the mouth open in a silent scream. It is life without life.

### Arcane Mechanics

The Sigil operates at the intersection of institutional magic and direct divine invocation. The ritual inscription in the flesh of the condemned serves as a trigger for the manifestation of Abbhal, who seals the sentence with divine authority: the enforcer prepares the arcane vessel and the devil carries out the imprisonment. The school of [[_wiki_en/escolas/nomos\|Nomos]] has spells with partially analogous effects, such as Sigil, Oubliette, and Confinement, but no source confirms that the Sigil of Abbhal derives from these scales, and the devil’s direct participation suggests a process that transcends conventional magic.

### Ritual Procedure

The enforcer carves the sigil directly into the flesh of the condemned with a blade, making precise cuts that form diagonals, an inverted cross, overlapping triangles, and circles. The blood transmutes into a black substance during the inscription. Once the pattern is complete, the enforcer invokes Abbhal: *Abbhal caecus iudex! Qui videt peccatum sine luce! Carcerem animae, claudite portas! Nec mors nec vita, sed poena aeterna.*

The devil’s manifestation is described as terrifying, shifting between a magistrate of white flames with eyes blindfolded by black fire, a balance whose pans drip a thick, viridian substance, and a devouring absence in human form. The divine presence forces those present into involuntary prostration. The sealing occurs when tentacles of antilight penetrate the inscribed sigil and extract the visible soul of the condemned, sucked into the body, but not through it. The sigil continues to pulse black on the skin after the divine projection dissolves.

Much of what follows the sentence remains unanswered: it is not known whether the condemned retains consciousness, whether they feel what happens to their own body, or whether the sealing can be reversed and by whose hand.

### Significance

The Sigil encapsulates the Empire’s penal philosophy: order does not punish to correct, but to demonstrate that transgression costs more than obedience. The sentence mirrors, on the divine plane, the function the Justiciar performs on the earthly plane.
