# Measurement System

The Measurement System is the imperial standardization of distance, time, weight, volume, and currency imposed on [[_wiki_en/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]] by the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|Shadow Empire]]. Before the [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadar]] conquest, each people measured the world with their own bodies and their own cycles. [[_wiki_en/personagens/arothistar\|Arothistar]] imposed a single standard, summed up in the motto “To measure is to dominate”: the same hourglasses calibrated in [[_wiki_en/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]], the same weights verified in [[_wiki_en/locais/thandruk_varn\|Thandruk Varn]], the same coins minted with his profile. Persisting in measuring with one’s own units is, in the eyes of [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/culto_sombrio\|the Cult]], an act of silent insubordination.

### Distance

The scale follows a bodily and environmental progression, from micro to macro: Grain (5 mm), Finger (2.5 cm), Span (25 cm), Pace (1 m), Fathom (2 m), Marn (200 m), Stretch (1 km), and League (5 km), the standard unit of travel. The smaller units serve precision trades (goldsmiths, apothecaries, tailors, poisoners). The Marn is the quintessential urban measure, the standard distance between watchtowers in Morvgrad and the practical limit of arrow range; the Stretch marks visual range in open terrain; the Fathom measures vertical distances, from the depth of a well to the distance between the scaffold and the ground. The direct conversions are one League to five Stretches, twenty-five Marns, and five thousand Paces. Cultural variants resist the imperial standard: the [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Druls]] use the Drath (1.5 m), shorter because the tunnels of [[_wiki_en/locais/vor-ghol\|Vor-Ghol]] are lower; the [[_wiki_en/racas/sylvano\|Sylvans]] use the Sárn (500 m), the distance at which they distinguish the songs of two different birds; the [[_wiki_en/racas/karashim\|dwarves]] use the Braç’harn (1.8 m), a mine gallery measure; the [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orcs]] use the Rûk (50 m), a tactical charging distance. The standard marching benchmark is four leagues per day for regular infantry and seven on a forced march, and a horse at a sustained trot covers ten leagues over good terrain.

### Time

The day is divided into four watches of six hours. The smallest unit is the Beat (1 second), defined by the resting pulse of an adult Shadar, followed by the Stilha (1 minute) and the Hour. The [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/ophideias\|Ophideias]] train to count internal beats with surgical precision, and the Stilha is the ritual pause before the sentences of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/justiciares\|Justiciars]]. Half a stilha corresponds to about thirty seconds, and among the Ophideias the expression “a bloodletting of stilhas” denotes fifteen stilhas, the average time they estimate for fatal bleeding without treatment. Each watch has its own character: Prima (Deep Shadow, 00:00 to 06:00) is the time for interrogations and executions, with doubled patrols in Morvgrad; Secunda (Ascension, 06:00 to 12:00) opens markets and courts; Tertia (Zenith, 12:00 to 18:00) is the peak of military and commercial activity; Quarta (Dusk, 18:00 to 24:00) imposes the curfew on Pindoriars in imperial cities. It is during Secunda that the Justiciars receive petitions, and it is during Tertia, namesake of the season Zenith, that military hearings and public executions take place. Each watch has left its mark on speech: “born during Prima” is a dark omen, “ascension without light” names the cloudy morning and the ill omen it carries in [[_wiki_en/locais/myrgarde\|Myrgarde]], and “Quarta talk” is a deal closed in haste before dark.

### Calendar and Seasons

The [[_wiki_en/conceitos/lunia\|Lúnia]] (28 days) is equivalent to the month and is divided into four fáscias of seven days, each coinciding with a lunar phase. Contracts and legal deadlines are counted in lúnias, and military wages are paid by the fáscia; the new moon fáscia is the “blood fáscia,” the preferred period for Ophideias operations. The [[_wiki_en/conceitos/varnum\|Varnum]] (365 days) is equivalent to the year, consisting of thirteen lúnias plus [[_wiki_en/conceitos/nullis\|Nullis]], and is literally the complete cycle of the sun (the root *varn-* is linked to heat, as in [[_wiki_en/personagens/varna\|Varna]], [[_wiki_en/locais/varnak\|Varnak]], and Thandruk Varn). The imperial reckoning of Varnums begins with Arothistar’s coronation, and “how many Varnums do you carry?” is the common way to ask someone’s age.

The year has four seasons; the formal names are used in scholarly and ceremonial contexts, while spring, summer, fall, and winter prevail in everyday use.

- **Spring** (Germa): 3 lúnias, 84 days.
- **Summer** (Zenith): 3 lúnias, 84 days.
- **Fall** (Quédia): 3 lúnias, 84 days.
- **Winter** (Brûma): 4 lúnias, 112 days.

Nullis (Day of [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/vazio\|the Void]]), colloquially “Vaz Day,” is the standalone day between the last lúnia of one Varnum and the first of the next. It belongs to no lúnia or season: it is a day of superstition and seclusion, when many Pindoriars close their doors, put out their fires, and do not speak the name of anyone who died in the previous year.

### Weight and Volume

The same ascending logic governs weight and volume. The Grain (about 0.05 g) serves a dual function as micro-distance and micro-weight, and measures poisons, potions, and alchemical doses; the Ounce (about 30 g) is the apothecaries’ standard for magical reagents; the Fist (about 500 g) is the unit of everyday commerce, the basis of a soldier’s daily ration; the Quintal (about 50 kg) measures raw ore and provincial tribute in kind. For volume, the scale runs from the Gulp (about 50 ml), the standard dose of potions and antidotes, to the Mug (about 300 ml) of taverns and barracks, to the Flask (about 500 ml), the imperial alchemical standard, and to the Barrel (about 50 L). Flasks sealed with black wax indicate contents regulated by the Cult, and imperial barrels bear a glyph of provincial origin for cargo tracking.

### Currency

The monetary system is ternary: Crown (gold), Stamp (silver), and Nail (copper). One Crown is worth ten Stamps, and one Stamp is worth twelve Nails. The Crown bears Arothistar’s profile struck in relief and is produced exclusively in Morvgrad; treasures and tribute are counted in crowns, and “how much does a crown weigh?” is a Shadar philosophical question about the cost of power, to which no one expects an answer. The Stamp is the most common coin in formal commerce and pays military wages. The Nail is the coin of the people: “not worth a nail” is equivalent to “worth nothing.” The gnomes of the [[_wiki_en/locais/mercado_oeste\|West Market]] trade in nails, the beggars of [[_wiki_en/locais/ghol_mournak\|Ghol’Mournak]] charge in them for information, and the [[_wiki_en/racas/derro\|Derros]] maintain money-changing burrows where they exchange them for favors and forged seals.

### The Eras

The eras have varying durations and do not serve as a unit of measurement; each is remembered differently depending on perspective.

- **[[_wiki_en/conceitos/idade_esquecida\|the Forgotten Age]]** (*Aetas Lucis*): “The Time of the [[_wiki_en/racas/luminares\|Luminars]],” a name shared by the imperial record and the subjugated.
- **[[_wiki_en/conceitos/primeira_era\|First Era]]** (*Aetas Primaeva*): “The Exodus” in the imperial record, “The Era of Alliances” among the subjugated.
- **[[_wiki_en/conceitos/segunda_era\|Second Era]]** (*Aetas Duorum*): “The Era of Conquest” in the imperial record, “The Deluded Peace” among the subjugated.
- **[[_wiki_en/conceitos/terceira_era\|Third Era]]** (*Aetas Umbrae*): “The Era of Arothistar” in the imperial record, “The Long Night” among the subjugated.

The imperial nickname for the Third Era is debated, since not all Shadars agree on naming the era after a single monarch.

### Idiomatic Expressions

The measurement system permeates the everyday speech of all castes. In distance, “a pace from the shadow” means being on the verge of death, “two fathoms above the ground” alludes to the scaffold, and “two stretches from here” serves as a casual reference. “Not even a stretch away can the truth be heard” and “not a grain of pity” are used by Shadars; “a span of steel resolves what a marn of words cannot reach” is a military proverb; and “three sárns of silence,” among the Sylvans, names the dead zone of the forest, where danger is imminent. In time, “between two watches” marks an indefinite period, and “thirteen lúnias and a void” is a poetic way of saying a year. In currency, “not worth a nail” and “how much does a crown weigh?” circulate everywhere.
