# Tabula Umbrae

The Tabula Umbrae, or Tablet of Shadows, is the third section of the [[_wiki_en/artefatos/codex_somniorum\|Codex Somniorum]], dedicated to arcane and alchemical knowledge. It brings together spells, magical formulas, poison recipes, truth serums, execution rituals, and sigils for summoning spectral witnesses. If the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/verbum_arkhon_codex\|Verbum Arkhon]] is the sovereign’s voice and the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/lex_obscura_decreto\|Lex Obscura]] the body of law, the Tabula Umbrae is the hidden arsenal, the operational arm that turns word and law into material consequence.

### Contents

The section brings together the practical knowledge of black magic institutionalized by the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/culto_sombrio\|Shadow Cult]]: codified execution rituals, alchemical formulas, sigils for spectral summoning, and procedures for extracting confessions. It also includes a subsection on proscribed deities, a catalog of symbols and arcane signatures that allows a [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/justiciares\|Justiciar]] to recognize the mark of a forbidden god at a scene of heresy.

### The Original and the Copies

The original manuscript of the Codex Somniorum was written in the blood of [[_wiki_en/panteao/vozrador\|Vozrador]] and remains under the guard of [[_wiki_en/personagens/xarn\|Xarn]]; the ink version used by the Justiciars is considered an imperfect translation. The copies are reproduced and glossed in a wing of copyists and exegetes of the Cult, under the tutelage of the archons, and each copy receives a ritual cipher and a consecrated colophon. Access to the complete Codex is restricted to Justiciars and the High Clergy; sworn acolytes handle the Tabula only in the copyists’ office.

### Consultation and Application

Consulting the Tabula is not a manual process: as in the other sections of the Codex, the pages turn on their own to the relevant subsection, stopping where few dare to read. It was in this way that [[_wiki_en/personagens/milorsevogh_velshard\|Milor]], who already suspected the god’s name from the carved skull and the kneeling figures, opened the Codex to the Tabula and reached the subsection on proscribed deities, which confirmed the signature behind the desecration he was investigating.

The work bears its own precept: *“The blood of the guilty cries out, but only the judge hears.”* (Tabula Umbrae, Ritual VII).
