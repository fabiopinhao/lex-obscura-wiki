# First Era

The First Era is the period that opens with the definitive fragmentation of the [[_wiki_en/racas/luminares\|Luminars]] and the extinction of the light elves as a unified people. With the last enchanted citadels reduced to ashes or swallowed by the earth, the world ceased to be shaped by elven longevity and came to be governed by the restless pace of humanity.

### Legacy of the Luminars

The relics left behind by the Luminars became as powerful as they were poorly understood: artifacts from a time before the era itself, whose effects and forging methods were never reproduced. [[_wiki_en/artefatos/fendris\|Fendris]], forged for use against dragons, ended up buried in the depths of [[_wiki_en/locais/thandruk_varn\|Thandruk Varn]], a dwarven citadel that guarded it without fully understanding its purpose. Other artifacts were fought over, preserved, or concealed by secret orders, ambitious kingdoms, and silent temples.

The [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Druls]] and the [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orcs]], separated from the Luminars before the fall, already inhabited the depths of [[_wiki_en/locais/vor-ghol\|Vor-Ghol]] and the wilderness of the north, respectively. The destruction of the parent race did little to alter their destinies.

### The Alabastrine Era

Despite the founding violence, the centuries that followed brought unprecedented advances and alliances. The collaboration between [[_wiki_en/racas/elarino\|Elarins]] and [[_wiki_en/racas/alvarar\|Álvarars]], begun in the last years of [[_wiki_en/conceitos/idade_esquecida\|the Forgotten Age]], matured and transformed the continent. The Elarins already possessed arcane power granted by the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/anjos\|angels]], but it was in the First Era that mutual exchange with the Álvarars perfected the [[_wiki_en/magia/devotio_sancta\|Sancta]] system: the Álvarars absorbed elven learning, and the Elarins formalized human devotional discipline into liturgy. From this union came the first great cathedrals and the first half-elves.

The celestial faith ceased to be a practice of devout warriors and became a continental institution, and magic began to spread among ordinary humans. Some Elarins began living among humans, integrating into the courts and temples of the central plains; others remained in purely Elarin citadels, such as [[_wiki_en/locais/elenir\|Elenir]], preserving the [[_wiki_en/racas/luminares\|Luminar]] heritage. Under the alabastrine religions, kingdoms that had once fought one another began to cooperate, trade, and share a common pantheon of celestial gods.

### Prosperity and Knowledge

With religious unification came material prosperity: commerce flourished, dangerous routes were pacified by joint patrols of Álvarars and royal militias, and agriculture expanded. Artificers and scholars began to work methodically, cataloging materials and recording results for subsequent generations.

The gnomes, ignored or persecuted in the previous era, found a place in the new societies: the alabastrine religions prohibited their genocide. The prohibition brought shelter, but no equality. Ingenious and obstinate, they were essential in transforming alchemy from a scattered esoteric practice into a systematized discipline. Even the dwarves, previously isolated in their citadels, began trading with elves and humans, supplying weapons, mechanisms, and enchanted seals that no other race reproduced.

Not all harmony was complete. The [[_wiki_en/racas/sylvano\|Sylvans]] remained faithful to the primordial [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/titas\|titans]] and refused integration, preferring the shade of the forests. The [[_wiki_en/racas/gerut\|Gerut]] along the forest edges cultivated peaceful coexistence and trade with the kingdoms without accepting their gods; more remote tribes remained as isolated as the Sylvans themselves.

### The Rise of the Shadars

The victory over the Luminars brought a new imbalance. The alliance between ambitious humans and [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/diabos\|black gods]] proliferated in human cities, and the [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadars]], born of the first profane pacts, appeared in growing numbers. Rejected as traitors to the faith and a threat to stability, they became targets of persecution: they formed secret cults in ruins, caves, and catacombs, while the dominant church carried out purges and armed sanctifications. The ideological clash between Álvarars, defenders of the status quo, and Shadars, with their doctrine of absolute transcendence, became one of the era’s structuring forces.

Faced with repression, the Shadars migrated to the far north, to the plateau the alabastrine kingdoms neglected. Scattered human communities already lived there, formed by those who had rejected conversion. The plateau offered protection for an unexpected reason: the Luminars’ magical barrier, erected in the Forgotten Age against dragons and still intact, sustained by the residual [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/magi\|magi]] embedded in the rock of the mountain ranges. The barrier confined the [[_wiki_en/racas/draconideo\|Draconids]] to the outer slopes and reduced the flow of orcs onto the plateau. The pagan humans of the north owed their survival to magic that had never been erected to protect them.

The Shadars mercilessly subjugated the pagan communities they encountered, forcibly converting some and sacrificing others. It was in this brutal isolation that their culture took shape, forged in exclusion, discipline, and devotion to dark power. With the definitive expulsion of the dark race from the central lands, five human border citadels united and founded [[_wiki_en/locais/myrgarde\|Myrgarde]], which grew over the centuries and became a great kingdom.

### Draconic Cults and the Varnakians

Not all the pagans of the plateau were absorbed by the Shadars. Generations of watching dragons with fascination and dread transformed fascination into veneration: draconic cults emerged, and through them some drew closer to the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/demonios\|red gods]], some of them deities of fire and blood whose dominion extended over dragons. The first humans with demonic traits, vestigial horns, coppery skin, vertical pupils, appeared among these tribes. When the Shadars consolidated their rule, they expelled these cultists to the west, forcing them to cross [[_wiki_en/locais/dorsal\|the Dorsal]] toward the lands that would become [[_wiki_en/locais/veinkard\|Veinkard]]. They settled in the valleys of [[_wiki_en/locais/varnak\|Varnak]], and the region’s name became the name of their race: [[_wiki_en/racas/varnakiano\|Varnakians]].

To the east, humans fleeing the wars of central [[_wiki_en/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]] sought the knowledge of the [[_wiki_en/racas/undine\|undines]], but refused to relinquish the vitality required to commune with the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/siderais\|sidereals]]. They followed another path of elevation: pure knowledge and the systematic study of the [[_wiki_en/cosmologia/plano_etereo\|ethereal plane]], learning to coexist on both planes. They gave rise to the [[_wiki_en/racas/frenim\|Frenims]]. There was never a war between Frenims and undines.

### The Retreat of the Dragons

The massive presence of dark energy on the plateau, an effect of diabolical pacts practiced on an institutional scale, corrupted the fauna and flora over generations. The Dorsal became inhospitable even to its largest predators: nests were abandoned, and dragons on the slopes facing the plateau wasted away. The Shadars gathered abandoned eggs and tried to corrupt them, but what hatched were not dragons, but diminutive draconic beings, without breath or flight. Over generations of deliberate selection, they attained a stable form: small, agile, obedient reptiles, absolutely devoted to the masters who gave them purpose. They were called [[_wiki_en/racas/kobold\|kobolds]], and they were enslaved.

The dragons migrated west, beyond the Dorsal. The [[_wiki_en/locais/pico_de_nethys\|Peak of Nethys]] was abandoned by all except one: [[_wiki_en/personagens/varna\|Varna]], too young to follow the elders and too obstinate to flee, took possession of the empty nest. She hunted on the slopes of the Dorsal, and the orcs who tried to cross the mountain range made up for the scarcity of prey. Orcish hatred of reptiles was born of this predation, not theology. While Varna hunted, the orcs did not threaten the Shadars of the plateau: a young, hungry dragon did more for the northern border than any fortress.

### Pacts of Shadows

On the northern plateau, the Shadars crossed paths with the Druls. The first contact brought tension, but since the Druls did not desire the surface and the Shadars had no interest in Vor-Ghol, their routes remained separate. In time, they recognized what they had in common: the black gods and contempt for the races of light. Caution evolved into pragmatic collaboration, and this rapprochement gave rise to political and military pacts long before [[_wiki_en/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]]. Together, the two races launched campaigns of enslavement against gnomes of the depths and other underground creatures.

It was also in this era that [[_wiki_en/panteao/vozrador\|Vozrador]], the most complex of the black gods, forged the pact that would define [[_wiki_en/locais/thal_serin\|Thal’Serin]] for eras. Unlike other devils, he sought supremacy over his domains of interest before territories: to him, control of the soul mattered more than possession of the world. [[_wiki_en/panteao/morlgrund\|Morlgrund]] and [[_wiki_en/panteao/titania\|Titânia]], the titans who guarded Thal’Serin, entered the pact because their aims converged: they wanted the forest intact, and that was what Vozrador offered. He promised that the Shadar empire would never burn Thal’Serin, which would remain inviolate as long as he maintained influence over his followers; in exchange, the titans allowed him to draw power from the forest itself, a prerogative no other devil ever had. The true source of his power came to reside in the green sanctuary, rather than the celestial domains, and the agreement sealed Thal’Serin’s fate for eras.
