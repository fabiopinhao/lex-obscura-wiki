# Reinkardic Wars: The Ascension of the Monarch

The Ascension of the Monarch designates the period of the Reinkardic Wars in which Dravhok, a converted general of the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/culto_sombrio\|Shadow Cult]], conquered more territory than any previous commander and, in the end, seized power in [[_wiki_en/locais/morvgrad\|Morvgrad]], declaring himself [[_wiki_en/personagens/arothistar\|Arothistar]]. The orc campaigns had produced generals, and the second half of the war demanded more than competence: it demanded the audacity to turn tactical advantage into permanent conquest. Several tried. One advanced farther than all the others and did not return while there was still land to take.

### Dravhok’s Origins

Dravhok was not a [[_wiki_en/racas/shadar\|Shadar]] by birth. Converted through the [[_wiki_en/magia/rito_de_ossarium\|Rite of Ossárium]], he bore a former name that no record preserved. Many humans were converted through the Rite, and no one in the [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/imperio_sombrio\|Shadow Empire]] had known him before his conversion; Dravhok never revealed that he had been human, and no one dared to ask him.

His difference began with the composition of his troops. Most Shadar generals maintained conservative ratios, one Shadar or [[_wiki_en/racas/drul\|Drul]] for every [[_wiki_en/racas/orc\|orc]], for fear of losing control of their legions. Dravhok operated with ratios his peers considered suicidal, with far more orcs per officer, and assigned captain positions to veteran orcs, a role that Shadar military culture reserved for the Shadars themselves. Where others saw a risk of mutiny, he saw soldiers who fought more fiercely when the hierarchy recognized their worth.

### The Campaign in the West

Instead of attacking [[_wiki_en/locais/myrgarde\|Myrgarde]] directly, Dravhok skirted the Central Empire through the western mountains. His targets were the dwarven fortresses whose forges had armed Myrgarde’s armies in the first half of the war. The tactic required trust between races that rarely had it: Dravhok advanced along the surface, destroying the stone gates, while Drul contingents commanded by matriarchs came up from underground, through mines dug too deep, which had breached the boundaries of [[_wiki_en/locais/vor-ghol\|Vor-Ghol]]. Fortress after fortress, the dwarves’ attention focused on the collapsing gates while death rose through the galleries. Each mountain taken was a forge lost to Myrgarde and gained for Morvgrad.

In [[_wiki_en/locais/thandruk_varn\|Thandruk Varn]], the great volcanic mountain, the underground ambush was impossible, because its depths had no connection to Vor-Ghol. Instead of a massacre, Dravhok offered negotiation. The surrender was conditional: Thandruk Varn would cut off supplies to Myrgarde, redirect production to Morvgrad and [[_wiki_en/locais/ghilanna\|Ghilanna]], the Drul capital, and hand over the [[_wiki_en/racas/luminares\|Luminar]] artifacts it had kept since the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/primeira_era\|First Era]]. In exchange, the clans would keep their forges and their lives. Over time, surrender deteriorated into de facto subjugation, and Thandruk Varn learned what so many others already knew: the Shadow Cult’s terms last exactly until the moment they cease to be convenient. A tunnel was dug connecting the mountain to Vor-Ghol, and that route remains active. Thandruk Varn is the only original dwarven fortress still operating on the continent.

### The Campaign in the East

The second campaign advanced down the east, with other generals as peers, against the human cities that formed the eastern belt of the Central Empire. Unlike in the mountains, the war on the plains was one of direct attrition, with besieged walls and cavalry charges, and all sides bled. Each time a general fell, his legion, without a clear chain of command, joined Dravhok’s troops, the nearest leader strong enough to protect them. Dravhok’s army grew.

By the time he reached Dernessal, the largest independent kingdom southeast of Myrgarde, he already commanded the largest legion the Cult had assembled under a single general. Dernessal fell, the area east of Myrgarde was subjugated, and the campaign continued to the Austral sea. In [[_wiki_en/locais/valdris\|Valdris]], however, the sea answered: waves that no natural storm could explain swept over the city and the legion, carrying away soldiers and equipment. Dravhok ordered a retreat, and crossed back through a continent that, for the first time in decades, saw a shadow army fall back. The retreat did not diminish the achievement, for the map of [[_wiki_en/locais/reinkard\|Reinkard]] had been irreversibly altered.

### The Title of Aroth

Dravhok returned to the plateau as a legend. The [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/conselho_sombrio\|Shadow Council]] recognized that he had conquered more territory than any commander in the Cult’s history, granted him the title of strategic leader of all the generals, and elevated him to councilor. [[_wiki_en/personagens/xarn\|Xarn]], the Cult’s founder, granted him his personal blessing, a distinction no other living Shadar had received. The admiration was not accidental: the Shadars carried the long memory of their expulsion from the central regions of Reinkard and their exile on the frozen plateau, and saw in Dravhok the general who was finally collecting the debt. They began calling him Aroth, a name that became predominant after the eastern campaigns.

Dravhok was the first councilor to serve simultaneously as a general in the field, while the others governed from Morvgrad. Myrgarde’s satellite citadels began to fall, but the capital itself remained impenetrable, with sacred walls reinforced by centuries of celestial blessings.

### The Siege of Myrgarde

Myrgarde’s impenetrability exposed fractures. The Cult’s generals, mostly [[_wiki_en/organizacoes/templarios\|Templars]] affiliated with specific temples, began acting on their own, competing for the glory of breaching the gates. They launched their legions in uncoordinated assaults and were stopped beneath the walls, and the orcs on the front lines fell by the thousands under enchanted arrows and alchemical bombs. Coordination deteriorated to the point of collapse, and Shadar legions even attacked one another in disputes over glory. Aroth watched from a unique position, for he was not a Templar and owed allegiance to no temple, which provoked a corresponding degree of envy among lords and councilors, many of whom refused to call him by the name the people had given him.

Pressed by the chaos, the Shadow Council handed Aroth a list of generals and temples considered dissidents from the [[_wiki_en/conceitos/lex_obscura_decreto\|Lex Obscura]] and asked him to bring the conflicts under control. What it expected was discipline. What it received was conquest: Dravhok attacked the named generals and took their armies, and several legions joined him of their own accord, preferring a victorious commander to a Templar who led them to slaughter. The insubordination ceased, but Aroth emerged from the operation with an army larger than any force the Council controlled, as well as influence in two of the most important temples and popular support in Morvgrad. Most of the Council concluded that he was a threat to Morvgrad itself.

The response was the most important truce of the Reinkardic Wars. The Council proposed a cessation of hostilities to Myrgarde, not for peace, but to prevent Aroth from taking the human capital and returning unchallenged. The truce was short-lived, like all the others, and over the next twenty-five years dozens of truces and breaches followed one another, until Myrgarde’s final fall.

### The Purge and the Ascension

The Council ordered Aroth to return to Morvgrad for a phase of reorganization. When it realized that he was ascending the plateau with almost the entire veteran force, it changed its tone and ordered him to wait in the orc forts on the outskirts, because the old rule forbade crossing the fortified perimeter with a legion of orcs. Aroth crossed. Orders to stop him traveled down the chain of command and died upon reaching any officer who had seen him in the field. He entered Morvgrad to everyone’s applause and marched to the wall of [[_wiki_en/locais/mor_draconis\|Mor’Draconis]], the city’s middle ring. For the first and last time in the Cult’s history, entire troops of trained orcs entered the sacred territory of [[_wiki_en/locais/xarn_aroth\|Xarn’Aroth]], and the few who tried to attack him were killed by the troops or detained by the people themselves.

The purge was surgical. The most powerful councilors inhabited the tower of [[_wiki_en/locais/mor_severith\|Mor’Severith]], and it was there that Aroth’s security force entered with its leader. Many corpses came out of the tower that night. The councilors who did not reside there received an implicit choice between submission and the same fate, and most chose to survive. Xarn, who had already been living under necromancy for centuries, remained neutral while the general devoured the Council. With popular support and military power in his hands, Dravhok declared himself Arothistar, Aroth of Histar, the Consecrated One of the Heights, a transition that took him from hegemonic general to effective ruler of the Cult.
